# Objections to and Motion to Strike Appellant’s Supplemental Appendices 8 & 9

Before addressing the merits of the issues raised by Malcolm in his Appellant’s Brief, Veronica specifically objects to Malcolm’s brief appendices 8 and 9 and any reference to and reliance upon those documents in his brief as those documents are not part of the formal appellate record. Appellant makes no justification for attaching documents which are not part of the formal appellate record and cites no case law allowing such documents.

It is well established that documents attached to an appellate brief, which are not part of the formal appellate record, may not be considered by the appellate court. *See Warriner v. Warriner*, 394 S.W.3d 240, 254 (Tex. App.—El Paso 2012, no pet.) (holding that documents attached to a brief as an exhibit or an appendix, but not appearing in the record, cannot be considered on appellate review); *Fox v. Wardy*, 234 S.W.3d 30, 33 (Tex. App.—El Paso 2007, pet. dism’d w.o.j.) (refusing to consider appellant’s affidavit attached to brief because it was not part of the appellate record); *Cantu v. Horany*, 195 S.W.3d 867, 870 (Tex. App.—Dallas 2006, no pet.) (granting appellee’s motion to strike portions of appellant’s brief referring to documents outside record). The appellate record consists of the clerk’s record and, if necessary to the appeal, the reporter’s record. Tex. R. App. P. 34.1.

# Argument & Authorities

## A. Standard of Review—Abuse of Discretion

Most family law appealable issues are evaluated for abuse of discretion by a trial court. *In re A.B.P.*, 291 S.W.3d 91, 95 (Tex. App.—Dallas 2009, no pet.) A trial court abuses its discretion when it acts arbitrarily or unreasonably, or without any reference to guiding rules and principles. *Worford v. Stamper*, 801 S.W.2d 108, 109 (Tex. 1990) (per curiam). In an appellate factual sufficiency review, all the evidence in the record, both supporting and contrary to the trial courts’ finding is examined. *See Ortiz v. Jones*, 917 S.W.2d 770, 772 (Tex. 1996) (per curiam); *In re C.A.S.*, 405 S.W.3d 373, 382–83 (Tex. App.—Dallas 2013, no pet.). Reversal on appeal is granted only if the trial court’s finding misapplied the law, or is so against the great weight of the evidence as to be clearly wrong and unjust. *Id.*

When determining if the trial court abused its discretion, the appellate court considers whether the trial court (1) had sufficient evidence upon which to exercise its discretion, and (2) erred in its application of that discretion. *Moroch v. Collins*, 174 S.W.3d 849, 857 (Tex. App.—Dallas 2005, pet. denied). A trial court does not abuse its discretion if there is some evidence of a substantive and probative character to support the decision. *Id.*; *see also In re C.A.S.*, 405 S.W.3d at 383.

Trial courts are tasked with dividing the community estate in a “just and right” manner, considering the rights of both parties. Tex. Fam. Code Ann. § 7.001. Thus, a trial court would abuse its discretion in dividing community property if that property was not divided in a just and right manner. Trial courts are afforded wide discretion in dividing marital property upon divorce and, absent an abuse of discretion, this Court will not disturb the property division. *Murff*, 615 S.W.2d at 698; *Chavez v. Chavez*, 269 S.W.3d 763, 766 (Tex. App.—Dallas 2008, no pet.). “Equality in the division is not required, and this Court indulges every reasonable presumption in favor of the proper exercise of discretion by the trial court in dividing the community estate.” *Toles v. Toles*, 45 S.W.3d 252, 264 (Tex. App.—Dallas 2001, pet. denied). Appellate courts reverse only if the record demonstrates the trial court clearly abused its discretion, and the error materially affected the just and right division of the community estate. *Jacobs v. Jacobs*, 687 S.W.2d 731, 732–33 (Tex. 1985).

### Reply Issue I, Point B

> Malcolm incorrectly argues that the first mentioned ground for divorce is the only ground, misunderstanding that a court’s later rulings may add to the grounds for divorce.

Malcolm, in his Appellant Brief on page 31 (Issue I, point B), focuses on the differences in the grounds for divorce as found in (1) the Trial Court’s Memorandum Opinion (CR1 251), (2) the Final Decree of Divorce (CR1 255), (3) the Trial Court’s initial findings and conclusions (CR3 4), and (4) the Trial Court’s supplemental findings (CR3 7 & 8). Malcolm uses these differences to incorrectly conclude that he was found not at fault for the divorce since the Trial Court’s ruling memorandum and the decree only listed the ground of insupportability.

Malcolm, however, fails to acknowledge that under well-established decisional precedent, any conflict between the original findings and later findings are resolved definitely in favor of the later findings. *See Redman v. Bennett*, 401 S.W.2d 891 (Tex. Civ. App.—Tyler 1966, no writ); *Anderson v. Geraghty*, 212 S.W.2d 972, 974 (Tex. Civ. App.—San Antonio 1948, no writ). The later findings merely state other grounds for the divorce, i.e., adultery and cruelty, in addition to insupportability. So, this is not the absolution Malcolm is searching for and his divorce was not granted solely on the ground of insupportability. Of note, Malcolm states no legal precedent for this particular argument. Therefore, this Court should overrule Appellant’s issue I, point B.

## D. Reply Issue III

> By not granting Malcolm a new trial, the Trial Court avoided abusing their discretion as Malcolm has no right to a new trial based on ineffective assistance of counsel as his civil matter does not involve termination of parental rights or involuntary commitment.

Malcolm alleges an issue on appeal based on a right that does not exist for him in this matter. Malcolm requests this Appellate Court to reverse and remand for a new trial due to ineffective assistance of counsel. Effective assistance of counsel, however, is not a right that extends to this case. Generally, the doctrine of ineffective assistance of counsel does not extend to civil cases. *See In re Interest of P.S.*, 505 S.W.3d 106, 110 (Tex. App.—Fort Worth 2016, no pet.); *McCoy v. Tex. Instruments, Inc.*, 183 S.W.3d 548, 553 (Tex. App.—Dallas 2006, no pet.). Civil cases in which the right to effective assistance of counsel has been found to apply involve the termination of an individual’s parental rights, *see In re M.S.*, 115 S.W.3d 534, 544 (Tex. 2003), or the deprivation of liberty, *see Lanett v. State*, 750 S.W.2d 302 (Tex. App.—Dallas 1988, writ denied) (involuntary civil commitment).

Appellant cites no case law supporting his contention that he has a right to effective assistance of counsel, because there is none. There is only case law that this right does not extend to him because this civil case does not concern termination of parental rights or deprivation of liberty. *See In re M.S.*, 115 S.W.3d at 544 and *Lanett*, 750 S.W.2d at 302.

Since Malcolm has no right to a new trial because of ineffective assistance of counsel, it is impossible for the trial court to have abused its discretion in denying a new trial. There is no need to examine how his counsel allegedly failed him with discovery or at trial. Malcolm was already on his second attorney, so we know he knows how to fire and hire a new attorney. *See* the order granting Ben Boothe’s withdrawal, CR1 124-125 and the appearance of Paul Gomez, CR1 120.
