# Fifth Circuit principal briefs: rules

Alias `ca5`. Scope: counseled principal briefs in direct appeals and petitions for review: opening (appellant/petitioner), answering (appellee/respondent), and reply briefs. This file covers the shared court rules; the doc-type sections identify the differences. Verified against primary sources on 2026-10-17.

## Length

| ID | Requirement | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| CA5-LEN-001 | A principal brief may use either the applicable page limit or type-volume limit under FRAP 32(a)(7). A principal brief under the word-count option is limited to 13,000 words; a principal brief using the line-count option is limited to 1,300 lines. | FRAP 32(a)(7)(B)(i), (C); 5th Cir. R. 32.2. | M |
| CA5-LEN-002 | A reply brief under the word-count option is limited to 6,500 words and under the line-count option to 650 lines. | FRAP 32(a)(7)(B)(ii), (C); 5th Cir. R. 32.2. | M |
| CA5-LEN-003 | Exclude from type-volume count the items listed in FRAP 32(f), including the cover, corporate disclosure statement, tables of contents and authorities, statement regarding oral argument, addendum, certificates, signature, and proof of service. ## Brief contents and order

| ID | Requirement | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| CA5-CONT-001 | Include a certificate of interested persons on the first page before the table of contents or index. It identifies financially interested entities on all sides, opposing counsel/law firms, and disclosures required by FRAP 26.1; it is broader than a FRAP 26.1 disclosure statement. A separate FRAP 26.1 statement is not required. | 5th Cir. R. 28.2.1(a)-(b). | M |
| CA5-CONT-002 | Include a short statement about whether oral argument would help (appellant) or is needed (appellee). | 5th Cir. R. 28.2.3; FRAP 34(a)(1). | M |
| CA5-CONT-003 | Every assertion about the record must cite the original record page using the clerk's required citation format. | 5th Cir. R. 28.2.2. | M |
| CA5-CONT-004 | Opening and answering briefs contain, in order after the interested-persons certificate and oral-argument statement: table of contents; table of authorities; jurisdictional statement; issues presented; statement of the case; summary of argument; argument; conclusion; signature; certificate of service if required; certificate of compliance if required. | 5th Cir. R. 28.3(a)-(m); FRAP 28(a). | M |
| CA5-CONT-005 | An answering brief includes the contents required by FRAP 28(a)(1)-(8) and (10), except the jurisdictional statement, issues, statement of the case, and standard of review unless the appellee is dissatisfied with the appellant's treatment of those matters. It must include the table of contents, table of authorities, summary, argument, conclusion, and applicable certificates. | FRAP 28(b); 5th Cir. R. 28.3. | M |
| CA5-CONT-006 | A reply brief must contain a table of contents with page references, a table of authorities, and the argument. It need not repeat the appellant's statements unless the reply raises an issue requiring them; it ends with a conclusion stating the precise relief sought and applicable signature/certificates. | FRAP 28(c); 5th Cir. R. 28.3(j)-(m). | M |
| CA5-CONT-007 | A reply brief must not introduce new issues or arguments beyond the permitted scope of reply. | FRAP 28(c). | A |
| CA5-CONT-008 | Include pertinent statutes, rules, or regulations in an addendum when required by FRAP 28(f). | FRAP 28(f). | M |

|---|---|---|---|

## Doc-type differences

- **Opening brief:** appellant's principal brief; include the full FRAP 28(a) sections and the Fifth Circuit certificates listed above. A record-excerpts volume is separately governed by 5th Cir. R. 30.1 and is not part of this brief template.
- **Answering brief:** appellee's principal brief; the exclusions in FRAP 28(b) apply unless the appellee is dissatisfied with the appellant's treatment of an excluded matter. Cover color for any court-requested paper copy is red.
- **Reply brief:** appellant's reply; 6,500 words or 650 lines under the type-volume option, with the FRAP 28(c) contents. Cover color for any court-requested paper copy is gray.
