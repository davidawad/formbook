# Statement of the Issues Presented for Review

I. Whether the Agency committed an error of law in concluding that Claimant is not entitled to industrial disability benefits.

II. Whether substantial evidence supports the Agency's decision that Claimant is not entitled to industrial disability benefits due to his sequela mental injury.

III. Whether the Agency erred as a matter of law in determining the extent of permanent impairment.

# Routing Statement

This case involves the application of existing legal principals and should be transferred to the Court of Appeals pursuant to Iowa Rules of Appellate Procedure 6.1101.

# Nature of the Case

Appellant Michael Wooff (hereinafter "Claimant) alleges he sustained a cumulative injury to his right shoulder and neck and a sequela mental injury due to his work duties in drywalling with a manifestation date of August 9, 2019. Appellees (hereinafter "Defendants") accepted compensability of the right shoulder injury but denied compensability of the neck and mental injuries. Claimant filed a Petition with the Iowa Workers' Compensation Commissioner on August 11, 2022, claiming entitlement to workers' compensation benefits. This matter came for an arbitration hearing before Deputy Andrew Phillips on October 4, 2023.

In the Arbitration Decision filed on March 21, 2024, Deputy Phillips held that Claimant proved that he sustained a cumulative neck injury that arose out of and in the course of his employment with Defendants. (Arb. Dec. p. 49). He further held that Claimant proved his mental condition was aggravated or lit up by the right shoulder and neck injuries. (Arb. Dec. p. 52). Claimant was awarded temporary total disability benefits from October 18, 2021 through April 21, 2022 and March 15, 2023 through June 24, 2023 for his neck injury. (Arb. Dec. p. 53). Deputy Phillips found that Claimant was not permanently and totally disabled or odd lot. (Arb. Dec. pp. 56-57). He awarded Claimant 55% industrial disability benefit commencing on June 25, 2023. (Arb. Dec. p. 60). In regard to medical expenses, Deputy Phillips ordered Defendants to reimburse Claimant $244,133.02 for medical care; he did not order reimbursement for several emergency room visits and noted there was no indication that the claimant attempted to contact Defendants for authorized medical care prior to go to the emergency room. (Arb. Dec. pp. 62-63). Claimant was awarded 35% penalty benefits on the alleged late payments of weekly benefits. (Arb. Dec. 65). Claimant was awarded reimbursement of his IME with Dr. Segal and case costs of $4,118.64. (Arb. Dec. p. 67).

Defendants filed an appeal of the Arbitration Decision to the Iowa Workers' Compensation Commissioner on April 8, 2024. Claimant filed a Motion for Rehearing on April 9, 2024, requesting clarification regarding which medical expenses were to be reimbursed. The deputy issued a Rehearing Decision and Nunc Pro Tunc Order on April 11, 2024. Claimant filed a notice of cross appeal on April 28, 2024.

In the Appeal Decision filed on October 18, 2024, the Commissioner affirmed that Claimant proved he sustained an injury to his right shoulder, neck, and a sequela mental injury. (App. Dec. p. 2). The Commissioner reversed the deputy's finding regarding industrial disability. (App. Dec. p. 4). The Commissioner determined that the claimant was not entitled to industrial disability because Defendants offered work at the same or greater wages, which the claimant declined. (*Id.*). The Commissioner adopted Dr. Chen's impairment rating for the right shoulder, which was 9% upper extremity impairment or 5% whole body impairment. (App. Dec. p. 5). He adopted Dr. Segal's impairment rating of 35% whole body impairment for the neck. (*Id.*). The Commissioner determined Claimant did not sustain any functional impairment under the AMA Guides due to his mental health injury. (*Id.*). The Commissioner then added a 9% whole body impairment rating with a 35% whole body impairment rating using the Combined Values Chart of the AMA Guides and stated Claimant had sustained functional disability of 41% whole body impairment. (App. Dec. p. 6). Therefore, Claimant was awarded 205 weeks of permanent partial disability benefits. (*Id.*). The Commissioner ordered Defendants to reimburse Claimant for the additional emergency room visits of June 27, 2022, September 14, 2022, and October 6, 2022. (App. Dec. p. 8). Claimant was awarded $2,500 in penalty benefits. (App. Dec. p. 10).

Claimant filed a Motion for Rehearing with the Commissioner on November 6, 2024. On November 12, 2024, the Commissioner denied the Motion for Rehearing. Defendants filed a Request for a Nunc Pro Tunc Order on November 13, 2024 regarding the Commissioner's erroneous calculation of permanent functional disability. The Commissioner denied the Request on November 26, 2024, stating that it should have been filed as a request for rehearing rather than a request for a nunc pro tunc order.

Claimant filed a Petition for Judicial Review with the Polk County District Court on November 14, 2024. Defendants filed an Answer and Cross Petition on December 12, 2024. Defendants filed an Amended Cross Petition on February 7, 2025.

In the Ruling on the Petition for Judicial Review filed on July 14, 2025, Judge Kepros upheld the Commissioner's determination that Claimant is not entitled to industrial disability benefits as Defendants provided a good faith offer of employment. (Ruling p. 12). He determined that even if Claimant was entitled to industrial disability benefits, there was substantial evidence in the record to support the Commissioner's decision that Claimant's mental health condition would not increase the industrial disability award. (*Id.*). Judge Kepros concluded that the Commissioner did not commit an error of law or improperly use Agency expertise in determining the award of functional disability benefits. (Ruling p. 13). Judge Kepros upheld the Agency decision that Claimant sustained a compensable neck and mental health sequela injury. (Ruling pp. 13-14). In regard to the calculation of permanent impairment for the neck and shoulder, the Court ordered Defendants to submit a proposed order regarding the issue. (Ruling p. 14).

Judge Kepros issued an Order regarding the calculation of whole-body impairment on August 5, 2025. He stated that the Agency's calculation of whole-body impairment for the right shoulder and neck is 38% whole body impairment when using the Combined Values Chart of the AMA Guides. (Order p. 2).

Claimant filed a Notice of Appeal with the Iowa Supreme Court on September 3, 2025.

Dr. Carpenter opined Claimant did not sustain a psychological or mental health injury related to the alleged work injury. (Ex. D, p. 66). He stated the records demonstrate Claimant had preexisting depression and longstanding alcoholism. (*Id.*). Dr. Carpenter noted Claimant was diagnosed with adjustment disorder and depressed mood in 2017. (*Id.*). Dr. Carpenter opined Claimant did not sustain any permanent impairment since he did not sustain a mental injury related to the work injury and he did not require any permanent work restrictions. (Ex. D, p. 67).

# Argument

## I. The Commissioner did not commit an error of law in concluding that Claimant is not entitled to industrial disability benefits.

### A. Preservation of Error

Defendant believes that Claimant preserved error regarding this issue.

### B. Standard of Review

Defendant agrees with the Claimant's standard of review.

### C. Argument

The Commissioner was correct in holding that Claimant is not entitled to industrial disability benefits. (App. Dec. p. 4). It was not an error of law or irrational, illogical, or wholly unjustifiable. Even through Claimant sustained an injury to his body as a whole, he is not entitled to industrial disability benefits based on Iowa Code § 85.34(2)(v), which states:

> If an employee who is eligible for compensation under this paragraph returns to work or is offered work for which the employee receives or would receive the same or greater salary, wages, or earnings than the employee received at the time of the injury, the employee shall be compensated based only upon the employee's functional impairment resulting from the injury, and not in relation to the employee's earning capacity.

IOWA CODE § 85.34(2)(v) (2019).

In Nusinovic the deputy held that the claimant was not entitled to industrial disability benefits. *Nusinovic v. UnityPoint Health-Waterloo*, File Nos. 19001748.01; 20009674.01; 20700556.01; 21700201.01; 21700202.01; 21700203.01 (Arb. Dec. Feb. 24, 2022). The claimant was working as a housekeeper at the time of the alleged injuries. *Id.* at \*6. Following her release from medical treatment, the defendant offered the claimant a full-time administrative assistant position where she would earn higher wages than at the time of the injury. *Id.* at \*31. The position was within the permanent work restrictions imposed by Dr. Sassman. *Id.* The claimant declined the job "due to pain", although she never attempted the job. *Id.* Deputy Palmer held that the defendant had offered the claimant work at a greater wage than what she was earning at the time of the work injury that was consistent with her work restrictions, and she refused the work. *Id.* Therefore, she was only entitled to functional disability benefits. *Id.* The commissioner affirmed the decision on appeal. *Nusinovic v. UnityPoint Health-Waterloo*, File Nos. 19001748.01; 20009674.01; 20700556.01; 21700201.01; 21700202.01; 21700203.01 (App. Dec. July 19, 2022).

In contrast, the deputy in Newburry found the claimant was entitled to industrial disability benefits despite that defendant offered the claimant a job after the work injury. *Newburry v. Lutheran Home for the Aged Assoc.*, File No. 21000314.02 at \*11 (Arb. Dec. Jan. 9, 2023). In that case, the deputy found that the job that was offered was not within the claimant's permanent work restrictions and the claimant was not physically capable of performing the position. *Id.* Since the claimant's work restrictions restricted him from performing the job, it was not an offer that was contemplated by Iowa Code § 85.34(2)(v). *Id.*

In this case, Defendants made a good faith offer to Claimant after Dr. Nepola imposed permanent work restrictions for his right shoulder. Defendants sent formal correspondence to Claimant on August 25, 2021, regarding the offer of the superintendent position. (Ex. 7, p. 294). Claimant was offered a position where he would earn $60,000 annually with benefits. (*Id.*). Claimant was also verbally offered the position of commercial estimator. (Hr. Tr. pp. 42-43; Ex. 7, p. 296). Claimant testified that he declined the job offers and stated he felt that he was not qualified to perform the positions. (Hr. Tr. pp. 42-43).

At the time of the work injury, Claimant's average weekly wage was $864.90, which equates to approximately $45,000 annually. The job offers were for greater wages than what Claimant was earning at the time of the work injury. Based on the fact that Defendants offered Claimant positions within his permanent work restrictions that paid the same or greater wages than his pre-injury position and he chose not to return to work with Defendants, he is not entitled to industrial disability benefits under § 85.34(2)(v).

Claimant chose to stop working for Defendants based on his own beliefs about his ability to perform the jobs. The reason for Claimant declining the jobs was not because they were outside of his permanent work restrictions. (*See* Hr. Tr. pp. 42-45). Claimant stated he did not feel capable of performing the jobs due to the "physical and mental demand of that job." (Hr. Tr. p. 43). Claimant never attempted to perform the jobs. Clearly the employer believed Claimant had the necessary qualifications to successfully complete the jobs based on their good faith offers. The nature of the job was within the claimant's expertise---construction work. It was not a job that was completely and totally different from the nature of the claimant's past work experience.

Claimant asserts that he was not at maximum medical improvement for his neck and later underwent surgery on his neck; therefore, he was not physically able to perform the jobs that were offered to him. Defendants offered the jobs to Claimants in good faith. Had Claimant accepted a job and then needed to undergo surgery in the future, the employer's typical policies regarding medical leave would have been followed. Claimant continued to work for Defendants throughout the course of his medical treatment related to his right shoulder. There is no reason to believe that Claimant could not have continued working in a different position for Defendants during the course of his treatment for his neck.

Defendants made a good faith offer of employment within the field of Claimant's prior employment with Defendants. Claimant declined those offers. Pursuant to the plain language of Iowa Code § 85.34(2)(v), this disqualifies the claimant from an industrial disability award. The Commissioner's decision was not an error at law or irrational, illogical, or wholly unjustifiable under these facts and should be upheld.

## II. There is substantial evidence to support the Agency's decision that Claimant's mental health injury should not be considered in an industrial disability analysis.

### A. Preservation of Error

Defendant believes that Claimant preserved error regarding this issue.

### B. Standard of Review

Defendant agrees with the Claimant's standard of review.

### C. Argument

Defendants maintain that Claimant is not entitled to an industrial disability award based on Iowa Code § 85.34(2)(v) and the offers of employment. If it were determined that Claimant is entitled to industrial disability, the Agency adequately took Claimant's alleged mental health condition into account when it assessed the extent of industrial disability. The Agency noted that Dr. Woods, Claimant's mental health expert, did not assign any permanent work restrictions related to his alleged mental injury. (App. Dec. p. 3). Dr. Woods also notes that the AMA Guides do not provide numerical ratings. (Ex. 2, p. 140).

Claimant asserts that Dr. Woods statements that "it is highly doubtful that Mr. Wooff will be able to work a physical labor job ever again" equates to permanent work restrictions. (Clmt Br. p. 34). Defendants dispute that this equates to permanent work restrictions. In the context of Dr. Woods' report, it appears that Dr. Woods is making an assessment of the claimant's ability to return to gainful employment in light of the entirety of his alleged injuries, which is an assessment to be made by the Commissioner.

Claimant further asserts that the Agency did not adequately take Dr. Woods' 40% whole-body impairment rating related to his mental health into account in the industrial disability analysis. However, the Commissioner specifically addressed this in his response to Claimant's Application for Rehearing and stated that Dr. Woods impairment rating was considered and rejected. (Ruling on Motion for Rehearing p. 4).

The Commissioner considered Dr. Woods opinion in the evaluation of the industrial disability award, despite that the Commissioner ultimately determined that Claimant is not entitled to an industrial disability evaluation. The medical records support the conclusion that Claimant had returned to his preinjury mental health baseline, and he did not require any permanent work restrictions related to his alleged mental health injury.

## III. The Agency did not err as a matter or law or use Agency expertise in determining the extent of permanent impairment.

### A. Preservation of Error

Defendant believes that Claimant preserved error regarding this issue.

### B. Standard of Review

Defendant agrees with the Claimant's standard of review.

### C. Argument

The Agency correctly determined the extent of Claimant's functional disability related to his right shoulder, neck, and mental injuries (although the Agency incorrectly added the functional disability amounts together, which the District Court addressed in its Order regarding the calculation of whole body impairment filed on August 5, 2025). The Agency did not improperly use its expertise in determining impairment. The Agency must use logic and reasoning, rely upon its prior interpretation of the AMA Guides, and analyze the facts of the case at hand to determine which impairment rating to adopt. The Agency did nothing more in this case.

The Agency adopted Dr. Chen and Dr. Nepola's opinions that Claimant sustained 6% upper extremity impairment for his right shoulder based on his loss of range of motion. (App. Dec. p. 5). The Agency also adopted Dr. Chen's opinion that Claimant sustained an additional 3% upper extremity impairment related to the distal clavicle resection. (*Id.*). This is consistent with the Agency's prior case law regarding distal clavicle resections.

Claimant asserts that Dr. Segal's impairment rating should be adopted. Dr. Segal stated Claimant sustained 21% right upper extremity impairment, which was 8% for range of motion deficits, 3% for strength deficits, 10% for a distal clavicle excision, and 2% for the biceps. (Ex. 1, pp. 57-58). Dr. Segal is the only physician to opine that Claimant sustained specific impairment to the biceps. Claimant did not undergo any surgery specifically to the biceps. There is no indication that the impairment rating to the shoulder does not encapsulate all impairment related to the right shoulder injury. Dr. Segal's impairment rating to the biceps is an outlier in this case.

The additional 10% upper extremity impairment for the distal clavicle excision is inconsistent with prior Agency case law. The Commissioner held in Jay that it is not consistent with the AMA Guides to add 10% impairment for a distal clavicle excision. The Commissioner stated:

> Table 16-27 of the AMA Guides governs impairment of the upper extremities after arthroplasty of specific bones and joints. Under Table 16-27, a distal clavicle excision is assigned ten percent impairment. Page 498 of the AMA Guides directs the examining physician as follows:

> Conditions not previously described that can contribute to impairments of the hand and upper extremity include bone and joint disorders (Section 16.7a), presence of resection or implant arthroplasty (Section 16.7b), musculotendinous disorders (Section 16.7c) and tendinitis (Section 16.7d), and loss of strength (Section 16.8). The severity of the impairment due to these disorders is rated separately according to Tables 16-19 through 16-30 and then multiplied by the relative maximum value of the unit involved as specified in Table 16-18 ....

> Under Table 16-18, the appropriate multiplier for the acromioclavicular joint is 25 percent.

*Jay v. Archer Skid Loader Service, LLC*, File No. 19003586.01, 2022 WL 17078713 at \*7 (Iowa Workers' Comp. Comm'n App. Dec. Aug. 23, 2022). The Commissioner held that after applying the 25% multiplier to the 10% impairment, the result was 2.5% impairment related to the distal clavicle excision. (*Id.*).

The Commissioner's interpretation of this portion of the Guides was affirmed by the district court on appeal. *Jay v. Archer Skid Loader Service, LLC*, Case No. CVCV064318, at p. 7 (Iowa Polk Cty District Ct. May 22, 2023). The district court held that the Commissioner can determine its own impairment rating if none of the physician ratings in evidence are consistent with the AMA Guides. (*Id.* at p. 6). However, the district court held that the impairment rating must be rounded to a whole number based on the instructions in the AMA Guides. (*Id.* at p. 7). Therefore, the 2.5% rating for the distal clavicle resection would be rounded up to 3% impairment. (*See id.*). The Agency has adopted this approach in subsequent cases as well. *See Vance v. Delaney Concrete Construction Co., Inc.*, File No. 19004997.01 at \*12 (Iowa Workers' Comp. Comm'n Arb. Dec. May 2, 2023); *Brown v. Estes Express*, File No. 20014133.01 at \*5 (Iowa Workers' Comp. Comm'n Arb. Dec. Sept. 6, 2023); *Gebert v. Bridgestone America, Inc.*, File No. 23700429.01, 651494.01, 220005546.01 at \*8 (Iowa Workers' Comp. Comm'n Arb. Dec. April 25, 2024).

The Commissioner did not use its own expertise in coming to the conclusion regarding the extent of functional disability. The Commissioner didn't ignore all of the physician opinions in evidence and determine a totally different impairment rating. The Commissioner adopted a portion of Dr. Nepola's impairment rating but determined his opinion regarding the extent of disability related to the distal clavicle excision was not consistent with the AMA Guides. "The commissioner may accept or reject the expert opinion in whole or in part." *Schutjer v. Algona Manor Care Ctr.*, 780 N.W.2d 549, 560 (Iowa 2010) (emphasis added). That is exactly what the Commissioner did in this case---he rejected and accepted portions of Dr. Nepola's impairment rating. He accepted the entirety of Dr. Chen's impairment rating. The AMA Guides are specifically incorporated by statute and administrative rule, and it would not be appropriate to ignore their provisions.

The claimant's argument that the Agency cannot take into consideration the correct use of the AMA Guides or its prior case law regarding the use of the AMA Guides would lead to irrational and illogical results. The Agency would be required to adopt any rating that a party put into evidence regardless of whether it is logical or consistent with the AMA Guides. The claimant's arguments in the Barry case are similar as in this case. The claimant asserted that the Commissioner violated Iowa Code § 85.34(2)(x) when it rejected his expert's opinion because the impairment ratings were not consistent with the AMA Guides. *Barry v. John Deere Dubuque Works*, 995 N.W.2d 516 (Table), 2023 WL 3612394 at \*3 (Iowa Ct. App. 2023). The claimant asserted that the statute prohibited the Commissioner from finding that the expert misapplied the AMA Guides when weighing credibility and the Commissioner abused his discretion by used the AMA Guides. *Id.* The Iowa Court of Appeals rejected this argument and held that the Commissioner did not abuse his discretion by relying on the AMA Guides to determine expert credibility. *Id.*

Similarly in this case, the Commissioner did not err in relying on the AMA Guides to determine the extent of functional disability. If the Commissioner is not allowed to utilize the Guides, it will lead to unpredictable and absurd results. The intent of the revisions to Iowa Code § 85.34(2)(x) was the prevent unpredictable results that were not consistent with the Guides. The Commissioner's reliance on the Guides is consistent with the statute.

The Commissioner did not err in rejecting Dr. Woods 40% body as a whole impairment rating. Dr. Woods stated that the AMA Guides do not provide impairment ratings for mental conditions. Yet, he provided his own estimation of permanent impairment of 40%. (Ex. 2, p. 140). Given the other evidence in the record, a 40% whole body impairment rating is clearly inflated.

The medical records document that Claimant's mental health condition has improved since 2019, and he has returned to his preinjury baseline. Claimant is currently taking fluoxetine, which is the same medication that he took prior to the work injury. Claimant's alleged mental condition has not caused any significant functional disability or impacted his activities of daily living based on the records in evidence. Claimant declined psychotherapy that was suggested by Dr. Hendrickson on numerous occasions. He weaned off fluoxetine by August 22, 2022 and stated he was "doing good." (Ex. JE1, p. 75). While Claimant began taking some of his leftover fluoxetine in October 2022, he did not experience any significant worsening of his medical condition. (*See* Ex. JE1, p. 79). He remained at baseline.

The medical records do not demonstrate any significant disability due to Claimant's mental condition. Dr. Woods impairment rating of 40% is not consistent with the evidence in the record or the AMA Guides. It is within the Agency's discretion to accept or reject an expert opinion, in whole or in part. *See Schutjer v. Algona Manor Care Ctr.*, 780 N.W.2d 549, 560 (Iowa 2010). The Commissioner analyzed the record and expert opinions and rejected Dr. Woods impairment rating. The Agency was correct in determining that Claimant is not entitled to any functional disability due to his alleged mental health condition and the Commissioner's decision should be affirmed.

# Conclusion

The Commissioner's Appeal Decision and the District Court's Ruling should be upheld. The Commissioner did not err in concluding that Claimant is not entitled to industrial disability benefits as he was offered suitable work with the same employer. The Agency did not improperly utilize Agency expertise in determining the award of functional disability benefits related to the right shoulder or neck.
