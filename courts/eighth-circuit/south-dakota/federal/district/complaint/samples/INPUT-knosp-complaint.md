# II. BASIS FOR JURISDICTION

The basis for federal court jurisdiction is a federal question.

A. The federal statutes at issue are the Fair Credit Reporting Act, including 15 U.S.C. §§ 1681e(b), 1681i(a), 1681i(a)(2), 1681i(a)(5), 1681i(a)(6), and 1681i(a)(7).

# III. STATEMENT OF CLAIM

I reviewed my consumer reports from Equifax Information Services LLC, Experian Information Solutions, Inc., and TransUnion LLC and found inaccurate and inconsistent information appearing across all three reports. I disputed these inaccuracies through each Defendant's online dispute portal, but each Defendant failed to conduct a reasonable reinvestigation as required by 15 U.S.C. §§ 1681i(a), 1681i(a)(1), 1681i(a)(2), 1681i(a)(6), and 1681i(a)(7), and failed to follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy under 15 U.S.C. § 1681e(b). Copies of my consumer reports, disputes, and the Defendants' responses or failures to respond are attached as Exhibits A, B, and C. I also escalated the matter to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and the South Dakota Attorney General; both agencies issued written responses showing that the Defendants refused to correct inaccurate information despite administrative intervention, which are attached as Exhibit D. As a result of these failures, inaccurate information remained on my consumer reports, causing denial of credit, damage to my creditworthiness, emotional distress, and other actual harm.

# IV. RELIEF

I request actual damages under 15 U.S.C. § 1681o, statutory damages under 15 U.S.C. § 1681n, punitive damages where permitted under 15 U.S.C. § 1681n(a)(2), costs of the action, and any other relief the Court deems just and proper. The inaccurate reporting and failed reinvestigations continue to cause ongoing harm, including continued damage to my creditworthiness and the need to pursue litigation after unsuccessful attempts to resolve the matter through the Defendants, the CFPB, and the South Dakota Attorney General.
