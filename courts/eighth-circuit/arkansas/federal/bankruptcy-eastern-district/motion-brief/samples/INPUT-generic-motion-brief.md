# Memorandum in Support

## Introduction

The Debtor asks the Court to authorize the sale of a single piece of equipment that it does not need to reorganize. The sale is for more than the secured claims against the Equipment, so every secured creditor is protected by the proceeds. Section 363(f) allows a sale free and clear of liens where the price exceeds the liens and the sale satisfies one of the statutory conditions.

## Facts

The Debtor operates a metal-fabrication business in Pulaski County, Arkansas. It acquired the Equipment in 2022 and used it until the Petition Date. Since the Petition Date, the Debtor has idled the Equipment because its current contracts are fulfilled by a smaller machine that is not subject to any lien. An independent appraisal dated September 12, 2026, values the Equipment at between $195,000.00 and $225,000.00 in an orderly sale.

The Debtor marketed the Equipment through a broker from August 2026 to October 2026. The broker's report lists four inquiries and two written offers. The Offer is the higher of the two and is the only offer for cash at closing. The Offer is subject to this Court's approval and to a closing within 21 days of the order.

Sample Creditor Bank holds a first lien on the Equipment securing a revolving line of credit with an outstanding balance of $109,000.00. Lien Example Equipment Finance, Inc. holds a second, purchase-money lien securing a balance of $55,000.00. Both creditors were listed in the schedules and both have filed proofs of claim.

## Argument

### A. The sale is a sound exercise of business judgment under 11 U.S.C. § 363(b).

A debtor in possession may use, sell or lease property of the estate outside the ordinary course of business after notice and a hearing. 11 U.S.C. § 363(b)(1). Courts ask whether the transaction is supported by a sound business purpose. Here the Equipment is idle and costs the estate storage and insurance, and the Offer is above the appraised range's midpoint and above both creditors' combined claims.

The Debtor did not sell at a discount. It marketed the Equipment for two months through a broker, and the Offer reflects that process. There is no insider purchaser, and the purchaser has not sought any term that would benefit a party in interest over the estate.

### B. The sale may be free and clear of the liens under 11 U.S.C. § 363(f).

Section 363(f) permits a sale free and clear of an interest in property if one of five conditions is met. Two are relevant here. Under § 363(f)(2) the secured creditor consents, and under § 363(f)(3) the price exceeds the aggregate value of all liens. The Debtor requests relief under § 363(f)(3) for both creditors, because the Offer of $210,000.00 exceeds the $164,000.00 in combined claims by $46,000.00.

Each lien will attach to the proceeds of the sale with the same priority it had against the Equipment. The creditors are therefore adequately protected, and the Debtor asks that the Court order the net proceeds to be held in a segregated account pending a further order determining the claims.

### C. The notice given satisfies Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 9013 and the Local Rules.

The motion is served on the United States Trustee, the secured creditors, and the other parties listed in the certificate of service. Notice of the sale terms, the objection deadline and the hearing date is given in the proposed order. The Debtor has no local rule for motions in this Court that requires a different form. If the Court requires a separate notice of the sale under Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 6004, the Debtor will file it on request.

## Conclusion

The Debtor requests that the Court grant the motion, authorize the sale of the Equipment to the purchaser under the Offer or to a higher and better bidder, approve the sale free and clear of liens with the liens attaching to the proceeds, and order that the net proceeds be held in a segregated account pending further order of this Court.
