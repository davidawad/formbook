# NATURE OF THE CASE

This is a product liability action seeking recovery for catastrophic personal injuries and damages suffered by Plaintiff, after Plaintiff used “Kaboom Bathroom Cleaner” (hereafter generally referred to as “subject cleaner” or “cleaner”) marketed, imported, distributed, formulated, designed, manufactured, marketed, imported, distributed and/or sold by Defendant.

On or about December 21, 2021, Plaintiffs’ family used the Kaboom to clean the family bathroom. Unbeknownst to Plaintiff, that evening, Plaintiff’s feet were exposed to the cleaner. The exposure to the cleaner led to his feet becoming infected; and the infection ultimately festered, resulting in the double amputation of both of his lower extremities.

Defendant formulated, manufactured, produced, marketed, promoted, warranted, distributed and cleaner with harmful chemicals and/or contaminants that made it inherently dangerous to consumers, including the Plaintiff in this case.

Defendant knew or should have known of this contamination, but nevertheless put profit ahead of safety by continuing to sell the cleaner to consumers and failing to warn said consumers of the serious risks posed by said defects despite the risk of significant and serious injuries to Plaintiff and consumers like him.

As a direct and proximate result of Defendant’s conduct, the Plaintiff in this case has incurred significant, permanent and painful bodily injuries, medical expenses, physical pain, mental anguish, and diminished enjoyment of life.

# CLAIMS FOR RELIEF

## COUNT I

## STRICT LIABILITY

Plaintiff incorporates by reference each preceding paragraph of this Complaint as though set forth fully at length herein.

Defendant designs, formulates, manufactures, distributes, markets, promotes, advertises and sells cleaning products, including the cleaner at issue in this case.

At the time of Plaintiff’s injuries, the subject cleaners were contaminated and unreasonably dangerous for use by foreseeable consumers, including Plaintiff.

The subject cleaner was in the same or substantially similar condition as when it left the possession of the Defendant.

Plaintiff and his family did not misuse or materially alter the subject cleaners.

The subject cleaner did not perform as safely as an ordinary consumer would have expected them to perform when used in a reasonably foreseeable way.

Further, a reasonable person would conclude that the possibility and risk of serious harm outweigh the burden or cost of making the subject cleaners safe. Specifically:

1. The multi-purpose cleaners formulated, manufactured, marketed, licensed, sold, and supplied by the Defendant were placed into the stream of commerce in an unreasonably dangerous condition for consumers;
2. The seriousness of the potential infections and injuries resulting from the product drastically outweigh any benefit that could be derived from its normal, intended use;
3. Defendant failed to properly formulate, market, license, manufacture, distribute, supply, and sell the cleaners, despite having extensive knowledge that exposures resulting in injuries could and did occur;
4. Defendant failed to warn and place adequate warnings and instructions on the cleaners;
5. Defendant failed to adequately test the cleaners; and
6. Defendant failed to market an economically feasible alternative design, despite the existence of the aforementioned economical, safer alternatives, that could have prevented the Plaintiff’s injuries and damages.

Defendant’s actions and omissions were the direct and proximate cause of the Plaintiff’s injuries and damages.

^ WHEREFORE, Plaintiff demands judgment against Defendant for damages, as well as punitive damages according to proof, together with interest, costs of suit, and all such other relief as the Court deems proper. Plaintiff reserves the right to amend the complaint to seek punitive damages when evidence or facts supporting such allegations are discovered.

## COUNT II

## NEGLIGENCE

Plaintiff incorporates by reference each preceding and succeeding paragraph as though set forth fully at length herein.

Defendant designs, formulates, manufactures, distributes, markets, promotes, advertises and sells cleaning products, including the cleaner at issue in this case.

Defendant had a duty of reasonable care to design, formulate, manufacture, warn, market, promote, distribute, advertise, and sell uncontaminated cleaning products that are reasonably safe for their intended uses by consumers, such as Plaintiff and his family.

Defendant failed to exercise ordinary care in the manufacture, sale, warnings, quality assurance, quality control, distribution, advertising, promotion, sale and marketing of the subject cleaners in that Defendant knew or should have known that said cleaners created a high risk of unreasonable harm to the Plaintiff and consumers alike.

Specifically, Defendant was negligent in one or more of the following ways:

1. Unreasonably failing to implement appropriate processes, procedures, and systems throughout the manufacture of the subject cleaners;
2. Failing to provide adequate warnings about the risk that the subject cleaners could be contaminated and lead to infections;
3. Failing to ensure that consumers, including Plaintiff, received adequate warnings about the risk to exposure from the subject cleaners;
4. Placing an unsafe product into the stream of commerce; and
5. Were otherwise careless or negligent.

Despite the fact that Defendant knew or should have known of these contamination issues, Defendant continued to sell, distribute, and market the subject cleaners to the general public.

^ WHEREFORE, Plaintiff demands judgment against Defendant for damages, as well as punitive damages according to proof, together with interest, costs of suit, and all such other relief as the Court deems proper. Plaintiff reserves the right to amend the complaint to seek punitive damages when evidence or facts supporting such allegations are discovered.
