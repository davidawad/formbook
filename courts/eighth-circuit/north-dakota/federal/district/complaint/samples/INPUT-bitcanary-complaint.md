Plaintiff is a Wyoming Limited Liability Company with its principal place of business in 5830 E 2nd St., Suite 7000, Casper Wyoming.

Plaintiff’s Manager and member is Diego Martin Duro, a Resident of Spain (“Martin Duro”)

Minecore is a Florida limited liability company with its principal place of business in North Dakota.

# Jurisdiction and Venue

Jurisdiction is appropriate in this Court pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1332. The parties are citizens of different states and the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000.00 exclusive of interest and cost.

Venue is appropriate in this Court pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1391 because a substantial part of the events or omissions giving rise to Plaintiff’s claim occurred in this district, Minecore resides in this district and North Dakota public is impacted by the improper actions.

# Background

Plaintiff is a Bitcoin mining startup.

On or before March 2025, Plaintiff paid $388,098.70 for 94 units of Bitcoin mining hardware and equipment (“Equipment”).

Plaintiff required a host to support the Equipment’s operation.

On or about March 2025, Martin Duro, as Plaintiff’s agent, was introduced to Siddiqui by a third party.

On or about March 2025, Siddiqui represented to Plaintiff that Defendant Minecore provides storage and hosting services for data mining clients at 320 8th Street, West, West Fargo, ND 58078 (“North Dakota Site”).

Siddiqui represented to Plaintiff that the North Dakota Site could support Plaintiff’s Equipment and operations.

On or about 3/18/25, as part of Plaintiff’s due diligence, Martin Duro traveled from Spain to meet Siddiqui at the North Dakota Site.

Plaintiff’s agent, Martin Duro, toured the North Dakota Site with Siddiqui.

During Plaintiff’s visit at the North Dakota Site, Siddiqui represented to Plaintiff that the North Dakota Site was capable of securely hosting the Equipment.

On or about 3/25/25, Defendants sent a proposal to Plaintiff offering to provide hosting services for Plaintiff’s Equipment at the North Dakota Site.

Siddiqui represented to Plaintiff that the proposed agreement included a provision providing “all-risk” property liability insurance.

On or about 3/25/25, Defendants represented to Plaintiff that theft is covered by the “all risk” property liability insurance.

In reliance on Defendants’ representation that the North Dakota Site could host the Equipment, provide security for the Equipment and insurance in the event of theft of the Equipment, on or about 3/25/25, Plaintiff signed the Service Agreement (“Agreement”) and returned it to Defendants. The Agreement is attached as Exhibit 1.

On or about 5/8/25, the Equipment was shipped from Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia for initial delivery to O’Hare Airport, Chicago, Illinois.

On information and belief, on 5/25/25, the Equipment was delivered to O’Hare Airport in Chicago, Illinois.

On information and belief, on 5/15/25, the Equipment were transferred from O’Hare Airport to DIT Chicago Corp. Warehouse in Itasca, Illinois.

On 4/14/25, Plaintiff wired $14,775 to Defendants pursuant to the Agreement.

On 5/20/25, Siddiqui represented to Plaintiff that the Equipment was delivered to the North Dakota Site.

In reliance on Siddiqui’s representation, on 5/20/25, Plaintiff wired a payment of $14,775 to Defendants pursuant to the Agreement.

Defendants represented to Plaintiff that the Equipment would be installed on 5/21/25.

Between 5/20/25 and 6/15/25 no action was taken to install the Equipment.

On June 15, 2025, Martin Duro visited the North Dakota Site and Siddiqui represented to him that the Equipment had been stolen.

Plaintiff demanded Defendants refund Plaintiff’s deposit and pay for the value of the Equipment.

Plaintiff demanded that Defendants produce Defendants’ insurance information so Plaintiff could file a claim.

Defendants failed and refused to refund Plaintiff’s deposit, the value of the Equipment or provide information regarding insurance coverage.

At all relevant times, Defendants knew that it had no intent to host the Equipment.

At all relevant times Defendants knew that they did not possess an insurance policy to cover theft of the Equipment.

In reliance upon Defendants’ representations, Plaintiff sent money and the Equipment to Defendants.

# Count II

Defendants represented that if Plaintiff entered into the Agreement and transferred the Equipment to the North Dakota Site and wired a deposit to Defendants, the North Dakota Site would host the Equipment.

Defendants represented that they would provide security for the Equipment at the North Dakota Site.

Plaintiff entered into the Agreement in reliance on Defendant’s representation that Defendants purchased liability insurance for the North Dakota Site that would include indemnification if the Equipment were stolen.

Defendants intended for Plaintiff to transfer control of the Equipment into the control of Defendants at the North Dakota Site so that Defendants could retain possession.

Defendants never intended to provide security at the North Dakota Site.

Defendants had no intent to host the Equipment at the North Dakota Site.

Defendants did not have insurance to indemnify theft of the Equipment.

Defendants representations were material to Plaintiff’s decision to execute the Agreement, and Plaintiff relied upon this representations in executing the Agreement.

Defendants intended for Plaintiff to rely upon and knew that Plaintiff relied upon the representations, and Plaintiff’s reliance was justified and reasonable.

As a direct and proximate result of the Defendants’ material omissions, material concealments, and material misrepresentations that the North Dakota Site would accommodate the Equipment Plaintiff will be damaged in the absence of rescission of the Agreement.

Plaintiff sought rescission of the Agreement upon learning of the theft.

Accordingly, Plaintiff seeks rescission of the Agreement.

Plaintiff has no adequate remedy at law.

WHEREFORE, Plaintiff prays that the Court enter an order rescinding the Agreement and for such other and further relief as to the Court may deem just and proper.
