# Statement Regarding Adoption

Ms. Hill does not make any adoptions.

# Jurisdictional Statement

Ms. Hill’s claims arise under [42 U.S.C. §§ 1983, 1988](toa:usc1983) for the denial of medical care while she was detained in the Clayton County jail. The appeal is from an order enforcing a settlement agreement, which is a final order because it forecloses any additional claims. [28 U.S.C. § 1291](toa:usc1291) provides that “[t]he courts of appeals . . . shall have jurisdiction of appeals from all final decisions of the district courts of the United States.” This Court has said so directly:

> In addition, we have appellate jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1291, because the order enforcing settlement agreement is a final decision.

[*Affiliati Network, Inc. v. Common Sense Beauty, LLC*, 2019 U.S. App. LEXIS 23376, \*1 (11th Cir. 2019)](toa:affiliati).

The District Court entered its Order granting the motion to enforce settlement on August 3, 2023 (N.D. Ga. Doc. 101). Ms. Hill filed her Notice of Appeal on September 1, 2023 (N.D. Ga. Doc. 109), 29 days later, thereby complying with [Fed. R. App. P. 4(a)(1)(A)](toa:frap4).

# Statement of the Issues

1. Whether the district court erred in granting standing to the defendants and former defendants on a motion to enforce settlement agreement.
2. Whether the district court erred in interpreting the intent of the parties to the confidential settlement agreement.

# Statement of the Case

## Procedural History

Ms. Hill sued under [42 U.S.C. §§ 1983, 1988](toa:usc1983). On December 13, 2022, she and former defendants CorrectHealth LLC and Dr. Charles Clopton mediated and entered into a Confidential Settlement Agreement (N.D. Ga. Doc. 81-2, sealed). Defendants Clayton County and Victor Hill were invited to the mediation but chose not to attend. The District Court dismissed CorrectHealth LLC and Dr. Charles Clopton with prejudice (N.D. Ga. Docs. 69 and 71). Clayton County and Victor Hill then moved to enforce the settlement (N.D. Ga. Doc. 80), and the District Court granted the motion (N.D. Ga. Doc. 101). Ms. Hill’s motion for reconsideration was denied (N.D. Ga. Doc. 116).

## Standard of Review

The standard of review for an appeal of an order enforcing a settlement agreement is abuse of discretion. “We review a district court's order enforcing a settlement agreement for an abuse of discretion.” [*Hayes v. Nat’l Serv. Indus.*, 196 F.3d 1252, 1254 (11th Cir. 1990)](toa:hayes). The construction and enforceability of a settlement agreement is governed by state law. [*See id.*](toa:hayes) This Court reviews the interpretation of a settlement agreement de novo. [*See Managed Care Advisory Group, LLC v. CIGNA Healthcare, Inc.*, 939 F.3d 1145, 1153 (11th Cir. 2019)](toa:managed).

# Summary of the Argument

Clayton County and Victor Hill were not parties to the mediation or the settlement, and the agreement names neither as a party or beneficiary. The former defendants CorrectHealth and Dr. Charles Clopton were dismissed with prejudice, and the settlement agreement did not retain jurisdiction over the dismissed case, so those former defendants cannot bring a motion to enforce it. The District Court incorrectly interpreted the agreement, and its order enforcing the settlement should be reversed.

# Argument

## Standard of Review

The Court uses the applicable state’s contract law to construe and enforce settlement agreements. [*Vinnett v. Gen. Elec. Co.*, 271 Fed. Appx. 908, 912 (11th Cir. 2008)](toa:vinnett); [*Cohen v. DeKalb Cnty. Sch. Dist.*, No. 1:09-cv-1153-WSD, 2009 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 110078, at \*10 (N.D. Ga. Nov. 25, 2009)](toa:cohen).

## Defendants Clayton County and Victor Hill Are Not Parties to the Confidential Settlement Agreement and Therefore Are Without Standing to Bring a Motion to Enforce the Settlement Agreement

### The Confidential Settlement Agreement Identifies the Parties and Beneficiaries to the Agreement

The District Court stated that “Defendants Clayton County and Victor Hill are clearly third party beneficiaries of the Settlement Agreement.” (N.D. Ga. Doc. 101 at 3). The agreement says otherwise. Its opening paragraph identifies the parties:

> “This Confidential Settlement Agreement and Release (‘Settlement Agreement’) is entered this 13th day of December 22, 2022, between Tiana Hill individually and as mother and next friend of [minor child name redacted], her minor child (‘Plaintiff’) and CorrectHealth, LLC (‘Defendant’).” (N.D. Ga. Doc. 81-2 at 1, sealed).

Its binding-effect clause limits enforcement to the parties:

> . . . This Settlement Agreement shall be binding upon, shall inure to the benefit of, and shall be enforceable only by the parties hereto. . . . (N.D. Ga. Doc. 81-2 at 5, sealed).

Georgia law allows a third-party beneficiary to sue on a contract. [O.C.G.A. § 9-2-20](toa:ocga9220). But third-party beneficiary status “depends upon the intention of the contracting parties to benefit the third party, and this intention is determined by a construction of the contract as a whole.” [*Am. Fletcher Mortg. Co. v. First Am. Inv. Corp.*, 463 F. Supp. 186, 195 (N.D. Ga. 1978)](toa*:amfletcher). Clayton County made no payment under the agreement, and any benefit it receives is incidental.

### CorrectHealth Clayton LLC and Dr. Charles Clopton Have Been Dismissed With Prejudice and Are Unable to Bring a Motion to Enforce the Settlement Agreement

Dr. Charles Clopton and CorrectHealth LLC were dismissed by court order with prejudice (N.D. Ga. Docs. 69 and 71). Neither dismissal order retained jurisdiction, and the settlement agreement contains no such retention. Once a case is dismissed, a district court keeps jurisdiction over a settlement only if it takes one of the steps the law requires:

> “We therefore find that for a district court to retain jurisdiction over a settlement agreement where the parties dismiss the case by filing a stipulation of dismissal pursuant to Rule 41(a)(1)(A)(ii), either (1) the district court must issue the order retaining jurisdiction under *Kokkonen* prior to the filing of the stipulation, or (2) the parties must condition the effectiveness of the stipulation on the district court’s entry of an order retaining jurisdiction . . . .”

[*Anago Franchising, Inc. v. Shaz, LLC*, 677 F.3d 1272, 1280 (11th Cir. 2012)](toa*:anago). The District Court therefore erred in holding that these former defendants have standing to enforce the agreement.

# Conclusion

Clayton County, Victor Hill, and the former defendants CorrectHealth and Dr. Charles Clopton do not have standing to bring a motion to enforce the settlement agreement. The District Court incorrectly interpreted the intent of the parties to the Confidential Settlement Agreement.

For the reasons stated above, Ms. Hill moves this Court to reverse the District Court’s Order to Enforce Settlement Agreement and to allow Ms. Hill to proceed with her claims.
