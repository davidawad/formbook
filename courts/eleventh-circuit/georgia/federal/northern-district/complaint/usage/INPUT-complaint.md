# Nature of the Action

This is a civil action for a declaratory judgment under the Declaratory Judgment Act, 28 U.S.C. §§ 2201 and 2202, and the Patent Laws of the United States, 35 U.S.C. § 1 et seq., that ServeCo does not infringe and is not liable for infringement of any claim of United States Patent No. 12,618,781 B2 (“the ’781 patent”), and that the claims of the ’781 patent are invalid.

The device that Martinez accuses of infringement is a device that helps a user to measure the depth of depressions on the top surface of a mattress (the “Device”). The Device was developed into a commercial product by ServeCo with Martinez’s assistance and involvement. Martinez therefore developed the Device, at least in part, while he was an employee of ServeCo, using ServeCo’s time, personnel, facilities, equipment, materials, and funds. Martinez encouraged and assisted ServeCo’s use of the Device in ServeCo’s business. ServeCo used the Device openly, notoriously, and continuously, without Martinez’s objection, for almost 20 months. Only when Martinez was terminated for cause on August 21, 2026, did he raise his alleged concerns that the Device was an unauthorized use of a patent he had filed.

On information and belief, Martinez filed a provisional patent application related to the Device in May 2024. On information and belief, Martinez filed an application for a utility patent related to the Device in May 2025, while he was employed by ServeCo. Martinez kept ServeCo’s Chief Executive Officer informed of the patent application process. At one time, when he was still a ServeCo employee, Martinez suggested that the number of the ’781 patent be placed on the Devices that ServeCo was using in its business.

On or around August 21, 2026, counsel for Martinez sent a cease-and-desist letter to ServeCo, asserting that the ’781 Patent barred ServeCo from making, selling, using, or otherwise commercializing the Device. This was the first time that Martinez alleged that ServeCo’s use of the Device constituted patent infringement.

On information and belief, Martinez has told several of ServeCo’s customers that ServeCo is infringing the ’781 patent by using the Device.

ServeCo does not infringe the ’781 patent at least because ServeCo holds an implied license to practice the claimed subject matter.

The ’781 patent is invalid over the prior art.

# Jurisdiction and Venue

An actual, substantial, and continuing justiciable controversy exists between ServeCo and Martinez concerning the infringement, validity, and enforceability of the ’781 patent, and concerning ServeCo’s right to continue using the Device, which controversy is of sufficient immediacy and reality to warrant declaratory relief.

# Factual Background

### A. ServeCo and Its Business

ServeCo provides post-sale furniture and mattress services to manufacturers and retailers in the home furnishings and bedding industry. Those services include extended warranty and service-contract programs and on-site furniture inspection, cleaning and repair.

A recurring task in ServeCo’s business is measuring depressions in the top surface of a mattress to determine whether the mattress meets certain warranty conditions.

ServeCo and others in the industry have performed such measurements using methods that have been well known for many years. Known methods for measuring the depth of a mattress surface depression include using a taut string stretched across the top surface of a mattress and using a rigid straight edge laid across the top surface of the mattress. Mr. Martinez acknowledged these methods as prior art by describing them in the ’781 patent.

### B. Martinez’s Employment at ServeCo and Development of the Device

Martinez was employed by ServeCo from January 1, 2025 through August 26, 2026, in the position of President of Premier Furniture Consultant, LLC d/b/a ServeCo Repair. During this employment, Martinez reported to Anthony Milano, ServeCo’s Chief Executive Officer.

Martinez was responsible for the management and development of the ServeCo Repair field service part of ServeCo’s business. In this role, Martinez was responsible for supervising and managing field service technicians, and for managing the quality, knowledge, tools, equipment, and vehicles used by ServeCo to provide its in-home services.

During his employment, Martinez worked with several ServeCo employees on the development and commercialization of the Device.

Martinez developed the Device, in part, during his working hours at ServeCo, on ServeCo premises, using ServeCo’s resources, and in the course of performing his work as a ServeCo employee.

Martinez used ServeCo’s resources to develop certain facets of the Device, including without limitation ServeCo’s software-development resources; its laser displacement sensors, microcontroller modules, and 3D-printed housings used to build its units; test mattresses and inspection jobs; and the time of the personnel who built, tested, and demonstrated it. Martinez used ServeCo’s showroom facility for demonstrating the Device and for filming marketing and training videos related to the Device.

# Count III

## Declaratory Judgment of Implied License

Martinez’s entire course of conduct — developing the Device on ServeCo’s time and at ServeCo’s expense, providing the Device for ServeCo’s use, building additional units for ServeCo, training ServeCo personnel in its use, using it himself on ServeCo’s behalf, and encouraging ServeCo’s open use of the Device for almost 20 months — demonstrates his consent to ServeCo’s practice of the subject matter that Martinez purports is covered by the ’781 patent.

Martinez’s conduct would lead a reasonable person in ServeCo’s position to understand that Martinez fully consented to ServeCo’s use of the Device.

Martinez knew of and encouraged ServeCo’s purchase of 100 laser heads for additional devices. After the ’781 patent issued on May 5, 2026, he continued to participate in ServeCo’s commercialization of the Device, including by presenting it to ServeCo’s customer Macy’s, and by continuing to consent and acquiesce to ServeCo’s manufacture and use of the Device in its business.

ServeCo relied on Martinez’s conduct in adopting use of the Device in its business, covering development and manufacturing costs, building and acquiring additional units, training its personnel to use the Device, incorporating the Device into its service protocols and customer commitments, and forgoing the development or purchase of alternative measurement equipment.

Because Martinez claims that the Devices he supplied to ServeCo have no reasonable non-infringing use, Martinez’s supplying the Devices to ServeCo demonstrates that Martinez intended to and did grant ServeCo a license to use the Device in its business.

To the extent that one or more claims of the ’781 patent cover the Device, ServeCo holds an implied license to practice the ’781 patent.

ServeCo is entitled to a declaratory judgment that it holds an implied license to the ’781 patent and that it is not liable to Martinez for using the Device.
