# PRELIMINARY STATEMENT

This is an action for actual damages, statutory damages, punitive damages, costs, and attorney’s fees brought pursuant to the FCRA.

Consumer reporting agencies that create background reports, like SambaSafety, are charged with using reasonable procedures designed to ensure the maximum possible accuracy of the information they report. It is not enough for them to simply parrot information they receive from entities, particularly where a consumer makes a dispute about information reported.

When a consumer, like Plaintiff, disputes information through the consumer reporting agencies, those disputes are transmitted to the party furnishing the information. The FCRA demands that each party separately conduct a reasonable investigation of the consumer’s dispute and correct or delete information they learn to be inaccurate or cannot otherwise verify.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau has noted, “experience indicates that [CRAs] lack incentives and under-invest in accuracy” Consumer Fin. Prot. Bureau, Supervisory Highlights Consumer Reporting Special Edition 21 (Issue 14, March 2, 2017).

# FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

Plaintiff previously had certain criminal records that were expunged by court order (the “Expunged Records”).

Following the expungement, the Expunged Records should not have appeared on any background report concerning Plaintiff.

In approximately June 2026, Plaintiff applied to work as a driver with Uber.

Uber obtained a background report concerning Plaintiff from SambaSafety.

To Plaintiff’s shock and dismay, the SambaSafety background report included the Expunged Records.

As a result of the SambaSafety background report, Plaintiff was denied the opportunity to work with Uber.

On or about June 15, 2026, Plaintiff mailed a written through USPS (Tracking # 9589 0710 5270 3303 4160 94) a dispute to SambaSafety explaining that the Expunged Records had been sealed and should not be reporting. In support of this, Plaintiff included images of his driver’s license and social security number to prove his ID, images of the reporting of the sealed information, and a copy of the Court Order sealing the records in question.

Having not received a response from Sambasafety as to his dispute, on or about September 4, 2026, Plaintiff mailed through USPS (Tracking # 9407 1111 0549 5838 7133 11) another written dispute to SambaSafety again explaining that the Expunged Records had been sealed and should not be reported. In support of this, Plaintiff included images of his driver’s license and social security number to prove his ID, images of the reporting of the sealed information, and a copy of the Court Order sealing the records in question.

SambaSafety failed to take basic steps to verify the details in its background reports, including whether the records it reported had been expunged, and failed to conduct any reinvestigation once Plaintiff disputed them.

SambaSafety clearly does not have policies and procedures in place to ensure the maximum possible accuracy of the consumer reports it publishes.

Had SambaSafety conducted a reasonable investigation, the results would have been different.

Other individuals have lodged complaints similar to the Plaintiff’s in this matter, including direct complaints, CFPB complaints, FTC complaints, and via lawsuits.

As a result of the inaccurate consumer reporting, Plaintiff has suffered damages, including, but not limited to:

1. Defamation as SambaSafety published inaccurate information to third party entities such as Uber;
2. Loss of time attempting to cure the errors;
3. Mental anguish, added stress, aggravation, embarrassment, sleepless nights, and other related impairments to the enjoyment of life;
4. Loss of opportunity and delays in obtaining employment;
5. Plaintiff was denied the opportunity to work with Uber, which he would have otherwise achieved had SambaSafety maintained proper policies and procedures to ensure the accuracy of its reports;
6. Lost wages.
