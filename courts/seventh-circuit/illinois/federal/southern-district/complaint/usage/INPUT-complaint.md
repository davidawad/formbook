# INTRODUCTION

For more than forty years, Plaintiff PLAL, an Illinois 501(c)(3) not-for-profit, funded solely by charitable donations, has used the mark "Pro-Life Action League" continuously and nationwide in connection with its charitable, educational, and advocacy services and the sale of branded merchandise. It is well known nationally within the pro-life community and has spent years and significant resources developing its reputation and a network of financial supporters for its work.

Plaintiff IRL has used the mark "Illinois Right to Life" for over fifty years. Plaintiff IRL was founded in 1968 by a group of doctors and lawyers who were concerned with the direction the nation was headed in on the issue of abortion. Their goal was to create an organization tasked with educating the Illinois public about abortion – one that would honor the dignity and sanctity of human life at all stages. Since then, Plaintiff IRL has expanded to include issues of euthanasia, physician assisted suicide, and IVF. Plaintiff IRL's core belief is that human life should be legally protected at all stages – "from womb to tomb" – without exceptions.

In 2017, Illinois Right to Life merged with Illinois Citizens for Life to create Illinois Right to Life Action – the only 501(c)(4) in Illinois exclusively dedicated to lobbying on behalf of life in Springfield. Illinois Right to Life Action has been using that mark since that time. Both IRL and IRLA are well known nationally within the pro-life community and they have spent years and significant resources developing their reputations and a network of financial supporters for their work.

In November 2024 a new entity calling itself Illinois Pro-Life Action adopted a name confusingly similar to PLAL's, IRL's, and IRLA's famous marks and began an infringing fundraising campaign that trades on PLAL's, IRL's and IRLA's goodwill and misleads donors.

It is unclear when Defendant "Right To Life Action" ("RLA") adopted a name confusingly similar to PLAL's, IRL's and IRLA's famous marks, but it began doing so by at least January 2026. It began an infringing fundraising campaign that trades on PLAL's, IRL's and IRLA's goodwill and misleads donors.

Alex Dorr, Chris Dorr, and Ben Dorr are Defendant IPLA's directors.

Chris Dorr is Defendant RLA's president.

In 2025 and 2026, Defendants mailed numerous solicitation letters from Lancaster, Ohio, and Marietta, Georgia, using "Mailer's Postmark Permit 13" and "Mailer's Postmark Permit 215." Attached as Exhibit 1 to this Complaint is a sampling of true and correct copies of those letters, incorporated by reference.

Because of the similarity in form and content to Plaintiffs' fundraising communications, longtime supporters have misdirected their donations intended for Plaintiffs to IPLA in response to its copycat solicitations.

One PLAL donor who resides in North Aurora, Illinois, sent several checks to IPLA in response to its solicitations. That long-time donor has faithfully supported Plaintiff PLAL based on his personal knowledge of PLAL's founders' honesty and trustworthiness and tireless work on behalf of pro-life causes. That PLAL donor sent at least three checks to Defendant IPLA because he believed IPLA was affiliated with Plaintiff PLAL. But for Defendant IPLA's infringement, that donor would not have donated the funds he donated to Defendant IPLA, but instead would have donated those funds to Plaintiff PLAL.

Defendant IPLA cashed those misdirected "donations" intended for Plaintiff PLAL. After PLAL's longtime donor sent those checks to Defendant IPLA, he contacted Plaintiff PLAL and learned the truth: that Defendant IPLA was misleading Plaintiff PLAL's supporters who intended to donate to Plaintiff PLAL into sending funds to Defendant IPLA.

Defendant IPLA has sent donation solicitations to other PLAL supporters, one of its founders, and at least one employee.

Plaintiff IRL has received phone calls from its donors who received solicitations from Defendants IPLA and RLA and who were confused by those solicitations. Similarly, Plaintiffs IRL and IRLA reasonably believe that Defendant IPLA has been contacting many of their donors and that donations intended for Plaintiffs IRL and IRLA have been diverted to Defendant IPLA.

Defendant RLA also sent solicitations to pro-life donors in Illinois (and on information and belief elsewhere), including Plaintiffs' donors, posing under the name "Right To Life Action." One donor received mail purporting to be from "Right To Life Action," which, like many IPLA mailings, was mailed using Mailer's Postmark Permit 13 from zip code 43130 (Lancaster, OH).[^postal]

Plaintiffs bring claims against Defendant IPLA and RLA for: (a) false designation of origin/false association under Lanham Act § 43(a)(1)(A), 15 U.S.C. § 1125(a)(1)(A) and (b) false or misleading advertising under Lanham Act § 43(a)(1)(B), 15 U.S.C. § 1125(a)(1)(B).

Plaintiffs seek temporary, preliminary, and permanent injunctive relief, corrective notice, an accounting, disgorgement of Defendants' ill-gotten profits, actual damages, treble/enhanced damages, and an award of attorneys' fees under 15 U.S.C. § 1117(a).

# FACTUAL BACKGROUND

## PLAINTIFFS' TRADEMARK RIGHTS

Since at least 1980 Plaintiff PLAL has continuously used the designation Pro-Life Action League as its name and as a trademark in interstate commerce (brochures, newsletters, merchandise, website, social media, event banners, podcasts, and court filings).

Plaintiff PLAL's mark is inherently distinctive and, through more than four decades of nationwide promotion, has acquired substantial secondary meaning.

Plaintiff PLAL's prominence is underscored by extensive media coverage, including features on Nightline, Hannity's America, The O'Reilly Factor, Crossfire, Oprah, Good Morning America, and in Newsweek, Time, the Los Angeles Times, and the Chicago Tribune.

Further, Plaintiff PLAL was the prevailing party in the U.S. Supreme Court's landmark case NOW v. Scheidler, 537 U.S. 393 (2003). It has also been a named plaintiff in other litigation seeking to protect the rights of pro-life advocates and otherwise advance the pro-life cause.

As a result, the Pro-Life Action League mark is famous within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1125(c) and enjoys strong consumer recognition among donors, activists, media, and the pro-life community nationwide.

The Pro-Life Action League mark, as well as IRL's and IRLA's marks, are strong and widely recognized among pro-life donors, activists, organizations, and members of the relevant public.

Plaintiff PLAL has expended substantial sums—millions of dollars over four decades—promoting its brand and cause, thereby generating invaluable goodwill.

Plaintiff PLAL has never licensed or authorized Defendants to use any colorable imitation of the Pro-Life Action League mark.

Plaintiff IRL was founded in 1968 and has, ever since, used the "Illinois Right to Life" name in Illinois across educational services, publications, events, and donor outreach—creating substantial common-law trademark rights and goodwill.

Plaintiff IRL's statewide programs include Project Love (emergency grants for rent, utilities, and other urgent needs) and the Illinois Pro-Life March (formerly the Illinois/Chicago March for Life), which Plaintiffs IRL and IRLA jointly host. These long-running public-facing programs reinforce the distinctiveness of Plaintiff IRL's name among Illinois donors and supporters.

In 2017, Plaintiff IRL merged with Illinois Citizens for Life to create Plaintiff IRLA, aligning lobbying with IRLA and nonpartisan education with IRL, under the shared Illinois Right to Life brand family.

Plaintiff IRL, like PLAL, has prevailed as a named plaintiff in a number of pro-life cases, including Illinois Right to Life v. Pritzker, First Amendment litigation challenging COVID restrictions that inhibited pro-life speech while permitting other speech. IRL has also been a named plaintiff in other litigation seeking to protect the rights of pro-life advocates and otherwise advance the pro-life cause.

Neither Defendant IPLA nor Defendant RLA is licensed or authorized by Plaintiff IRL or IRLA to use or trade on IRL's Right to Life or IRLA's Right to Life Action name, goodwill, or donor relationships.

## DEFENDANTS' WRONGFUL ADOPTION OF CONFUSINGLY SIMILAR NAMES AND THEIR INFRINGING “PRO-LIFE” SOLICITATION CAMPAIGN

On November 6, 2024, Defendant IPLA was incorporated using the name Illinois Pro-Life Action, which is confusingly similar to Pro-Life Action League, Illinois Right To Life and Illinois Right To Life Action.

Shortly thereafter Defendant IPLA disseminated fund-raising letters using IPLA's name and touting IPLA's supposed courtroom victories on behalf of the pro-life movement and urgent need for funds.

Around May 7, 2025, three callers contacted Plaintiff IRL and asked Plaintiff IRL to remove them from its mailing list after receiving mail which they believed was sent by Plaintiff IRL, but which was not sent by Plaintiff IRL. One caller said he received many pieces of mail from Plaintiff IRL and demanded to be removed from Plaintiff IRL's mailing list. But Plaintiff IRL did not find the man's name in its mailing list. Another caller explained that she contacted Plaintiff IRL because Plaintiff IRL was "bombarding" people with mailings. While on the phone with an IRL employee, the caller confirmed that the mailing was in fact from Defendant IPLA, not IRL.

On August 6, 2025, another recipient who received an "Illinois Pro-Life Action" letter called Plaintiff IRL, demanded removal from its mailing list, and threatened to report Plaintiff IRL to the Better Business Bureau. IRL staff were able to explain the caller's confusion and confirm that the letter was from Defendant IPLA, not Plaintiff IRL.

Plaintiff PLAL first learned of Defendants' mail campaigns when a longtime donor from North Aurora contacted a Plaintiff PLAL staff member in July 2025 and asked whether the Defendant IPLA's solicitation letter was from Plaintiff PLAL.

Around the same time, another person who received Defendant IPLA's solicitation called Plaintiff PLAL after conducting a Google search based on the letter's contents, because the letter did not include a phone number, email address, or website associated with Plaintiff PLAL.

Plaintiff PLAL obtained the solicitation letter from a longtime North Aurora donor.

Plaintiff PLAL discovered that Defendant IPLA mailed solicitation letters via the United States Postal Service from Lancaster, PA and Lancaster, OH to Illinois residents, including Plaintiff PLAL's donor.

The solicitation letters request donations payable to "Illinois Pro-Life Action" at a Glen Carbon P.O. Box. The letter stated: "it will be much harder for pro-life organizations like ILPLA to win these victories in court" should a state constitutional amendment pass.

Upon information and belief, contrary to the representations in its solicitation letters, IPLA is not a pro-life organization and has never brought a lawsuit, let alone won a "victor[y] in court" as it represents to victims.

IPLA has also stated in fundraising materials that "there's never been a better time to challenge Illinois' wicked abortion laws in the legislature, and in court"; "With Roe v Wade in the dust, Illinois' wicked abortion laws are ripe for court challenges!"; "[The overturning of Roe v. Wade] opened up a path to fighting for the unborn in court." See Exhibit 1.

These claims falsely imply that Defendant IPLA has a pro-life litigation track record, which heightens the risk that donors encountering "Illinois Pro-Life Action" will erroneously assume affiliation with Plaintiff PLAL, IRL or IRLA.

In June of 2026, an Illinois Right to Life donor or prospective donor left a voicemail with Illinois Right to Life, saying she had given a donation to the office in "Glen Carbon" and had already been asked for more money.

As a direct and proximate result of Defendant IPLA's conduct, PLAL, IRL and IRLA have suffered monetary damages arising from: (a) diversion of prospective donations; (b) reputational harm from misdirected complaints; (c) corrective-communication costs and staff time; and (d) loss of control over their goodwill. The likelihood of confusion and loss of control independently support injunctive relief and other relief.

Defendants' infringement and misleading statements are currently causing irreparable harm to Plaintiffs and Defendants' misconduct threatens to continue unless enjoined.

~ Defendant IPLA's very choice of name demonstrates intent to benefit from the decades-old reputation of the Pro-Life Action League and Illinois Right to Life names.

# COUNT I

## PLAL's Claim For Violation of the Lanham Act False Designation of Origin/False Affiliation – 15 U.S.C. § 1125(a)(1)(A)

Plaintiffs repeat and reallege the previous paragraphs as if fully set forth herein.

PLAL asserts this claim against Defendant IPLA.

PLAL owns common-law rights in the distinctive mark and trade name Pro-Life Action League, which it has used continuously since 1980 in connection with charitable, educational, and advocacy services and related merchandise.

Defendant IPLA's confusingly similar "Illinois Pro-Life Action" name used in interstate mail solicitations has caused actual confusion among Illinois recipients at least one of whom made donations to Defendant IPLA which were intended for Plaintiff PLAL and others who contacted Plaintiff PLAL to complain about the onslaught of solicitations they believed were from them but were actually from Defendant IPLA.

Defendant IPLA adopted and used the name Illinois Pro-Life Action in interstate commerce (including mailings postmarked from the zip code for Lancaster, Ohio (43130) into Illinois) in connection with its solicitation of donations.

Defendant IPLA's use of "Illinois Pro-Life Action" is confusingly similar to PLAL's "Pro-Life Action League" mark.

Defendant IPLA's use is likely to cause, and has caused, confusion, mistake, or deception as to source, sponsorship, affiliation, connection, or approval.

Absent relief, that confusion will continue.

Defendant IPLA's conduct constitutes false designation of origin and unfair competition in violation of 15 U.S.C. § 1125(a)(1)(A).

Plaintiff PLAL has suffered and will continue to suffer irreparable harm for which there is no adequate remedy at law and is entitled to injunctive relief under 15 U.S.C. § 1116, and to disgorgement of Defendant IPLA's profits, damages, treble/enhanced damages, costs, and attorneys' fees under 15 U.S.C. § 1117(a), and destruction/impoundment under 15 U.S.C. § 1118.
