# Indiana Supreme Court: motions, petitions to transfer, and petitions for rehearing

Alias `ind`. Scope: motions and supporting memoranda (`motion-brief`), petitions to transfer (the petition for discretionary review, `petition-for-discretionary-review`), and petitions for rehearing (`petition-for-rehearing`) filed in the Indiana Supreme Court. The Indiana Rules of Appellate Procedure govern practice before both the Supreme Court and the Court of Appeals (App. R. 1); the merits and answering briefs of the Court of Appeals rules are not repeated here because no doc folder for them exists under this court. The Supreme Court has no local rule of its own for these papers that was retrieved. Verified against the Indiana Rules of Appellate Procedure, "Adopted, Effective January 1, 2001; Updated, Effective January 1, 2025" (title page), on 2026-10-21.

Local rules not retrieved: https://rules.incourts.gov/ returned HTTP 000 (connection timeout) on 2026-10-21, and https://www.in.gov/courts/rules/appellate/ returned HTTP 404 on 2026-10-21. The text below is the Internet Archive capture of the court-posted rules PDF. Form #App.R. 43-1 (the front-page form that Rule 43 (I) requires the front page to follow "substantially") is not in the retrieved text; the cover layout is convention (C) drawn from the exemplars. Confirm against the court-posted PDF before relying on a number.

Rule 43 (D) names the faces that qualify; Times New Roman is one of them. Rule 43 (D) sets a 12-point minimum for body text and footnotes. The page limits are not checked by total page count alone: Rule 44 (C) excludes the cover, tables, signature block, certificate of service and word count certificate from both limits, and the word limit of Rule 44 (E) is the alternative to the page limit. The word count is certified by the filer, not counted by the template, so no `page_limit` is set.
