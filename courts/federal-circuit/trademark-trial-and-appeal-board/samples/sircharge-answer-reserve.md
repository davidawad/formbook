# Part II: Amplification of Denials and Affirmative Defenses

Applicant pleads the matters in this Part to amplify its denials and, where a matter is identified as a defense, to assert it in the alternative. By pleading a matter, Applicant does not assume a burden of proof that the law places on Opposer.

## A. The marks differ in appearance, sound, meaning, and commercial impression

16. Applicant's Mark begins with SIR, a complete English word and a title of address. Opposer's mark begins with the two letters SI. The first portion of a mark is the portion that purchasers notice and remember first, and the first portions of these two marks differ in length, in sound, and in meaning.

17. Opposer has told the USPTO that the letters SI in SICHARGE will be understood as a reference to Opposer's own name. In its Response to Office Action filed on February 15, 2019, in the prosecution of the application that matured into Registration No. 5790235 (Serial No. 79233294), Opposer stated that "the letters SI- preceding the word CHARGE in the Applicant's Mark immediately will be associated with the Applicant's name, 'Siemens.'" (Response at 2.) Opposer further stated that "SI- in the Applicant's mark will be pronounced with a clear 'ess' sound either as 'sigh' or 'see.'" (Response at 3.) The word SIR is not associated with Opposer's name, and Applicant's Mark begins with the sound of the English word "sir," not "sigh" or "see."

18. Applicant's Mark as a whole conveys the English title SIR and a play on the English word "surcharge." SICHARGE, by Opposer's own account, conveys "SIEMENS CHARGE." (Response at 2.) The marks therefore differ in meaning and commercial impression, and they are not likely to be confused in the minds of ordinary purchasers.

## B. CHARGE is highly descriptive of the identified goods and services, and the field is crowded

19. The word CHARGE describes the function of electric vehicle charging goods and services. Opposer said so to the USPTO in the same Response, stating that "the common suffix of the Applicant's and Registrant's Marks is the highly descriptive word CHARGE which directly describes the function of the Registrant's goods." (Response at 3.)

20. Opposer also told the USPTO in the same Response that "the register reflects the narrow range of protection which has been afforded to the suffix -CHARGE when used in connection with goods associated chargers or charging devices in Class 9," and that "over 261 marks for these types of goods contain the word CHARGE." (Response at 4.)

21. Since the SICHARGE registration issued in 2019, the USPTO has registered additional marks of the form S plus a word plus CHARGE for electric vehicle charging goods and services, including SPARKCHARGE, Registration No. 7164242 (registered September 12, 2023), which covers "charging station services for electric vehicles" and "installation, maintenance, and repair of electric vehicle charging stations and charging equipment" in Class 37; SPARKCHARGE, Registration No. 6163366 (registered September 29, 2020); and SHORECHARGE, Registration No. 7655264 (registered January 14, 2025), which covers installation of wiring and power supply equipment for electric vehicle recharging stations in Class 37. On information and belief, Opposer did not oppose or seek to cancel those registrations, and, on information and belief, more than one hundred live registrations and applications in Class 37 for marks that contain CHARGE identify electric vehicle charging services.

22. Because CHARGE is descriptive and widely used by others for electric vehicle charging, consumers distinguish among CHARGE-formative marks by their other elements, and the scope of protection of SICHARGE does not extend to a mark that differs from it in those other elements, as SIRCHARGE does.

## C. Opposer's SI- prefix identifies Opposer, and SIR does not

23. Opposer told the USPTO in the same Response that it owned approximately 162 applications and registrations for marks beginning with SI-, and that purchasers therefore associate SI- with Opposer. (Response at 2.) Without adopting Opposer's account as a statement of fact, Applicant notes that on Opposer's own account SI- functions as an identifier of Opposer. SIR is a different and complete word, and purchasers who read SI- as a reference to Opposer will not read SIR in that way.

## D. Affirmative and other defenses

### First Defense: Failure to State a Claim

**[Include this defense only if the Notice has an actual pleading defect; see the cover note.]**

24. To the extent the Notice relies on registrations that Opposer does not own, asserts a family of marks without alleging facts that would establish the use and recognition of the claimed family, or asserts dilution without alleging that a pleaded mark was famous before the earliest date on which Applicant may rely, the Notice fails to state a claim on which relief can be granted. Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6).

### Second Defense: Entitlement to Registration with a Restricted Identification (Section 18, 15 U.S.C. § 1068; 37 C.F.R. § 2.133)

25. Applicant denies that any likelihood of confusion exists. If, contrary to that denial, the Board finds that registration of Applicant's Mark for the services as presently identified would be likely to cause confusion with a mark that Opposer has properly pleaded, Applicant is entitled to registration of its mark for the following restricted identification in Class 37: "Mobile, on-demand roadside electric vehicle battery charging services [rendered to individual electric vehicle owners and drivers], none of the foregoing relating to the sale, installation, repair or maintenance of electric vehicle charging stations."

26. The restriction would avoid any likelihood of confusion, because it would confine Applicant's services to a different kind of service, rendered to different purchasers, and offered through different channels from the goods and services of Opposer. On information and belief, Opposer does not use SICHARGE or any other mark pleaded in the Notice in connection with mobile, on-demand roadside electric vehicle battery charging services, and Opposer therefore will not be prejudiced by the exclusion of other services from the Application.

27. Applicant will move under 37 C.F.R. § 2.133(a) for entry of the restriction at the time the Board directs. If the Board finds at final decision that Applicant is entitled to registration only with the restriction, Applicant requests time under 37 C.F.R. § 2.133(b) to move to amend the Application to conform to that finding.

2. The SICHARGE Registration issued on the Principal Register on July 2, 2019, on an extension of protection under Section 66(a) of the Trademark Act of International Registration No. 1404839. The records of the USPTO identify Opposer as the owner of record. The registration recites, in Class 9, "Apparatus and instruments for controlling electricity, in particular charging stations for electric vehicles," and, in Class 37, "Installation, repair and maintenance of apparatus and instruments for regulating electricity, installation, repair and maintenance of charging stations for electric vehicles."

3. Applicant denies that his mark is likely to cause confusion with SICHARGE. In the alternative, if the Board finds that registration of Applicant's mark for the services identified in the Application, or for any restricted identification, would be likely to cause confusion with the SICHARGE Registration as it now reads, that finding would rest on the breadth of the registration's identification of goods and services, and the restriction requested below would avoid the confusion.

4. The identification in Class 37 of the SICHARGE Registration is not limited by the type of charging system, the class of customer, or the channels of trade.

5. On information and belief, Opposer and its affiliates have used SICHARGE in the United States, if at all, for high-power direct-current charging systems sold to commercial vehicle fleets and to transit and airport depots, and for installation and related services rendered to those customers, and have not used SICHARGE for charging goods or services directed to individual electric vehicle owners or drivers, or for mobile, on-demand, or roadside charging services.

6. Applicant requests that the SICHARGE Registration be restricted under Section 18 and 37 C.F.R. § 2.133 as follows:

    **Class 37:** "Installation, repair and maintenance of apparatus and instruments for regulating electricity, installation, repair and maintenance of charging stations for electric vehicles, all of the foregoing being limited to high-power direct-current charging systems for commercial vehicle fleets and depots, and excluding mobile, on-demand and roadside electric vehicle charging services and services for individual electric vehicle owners."
