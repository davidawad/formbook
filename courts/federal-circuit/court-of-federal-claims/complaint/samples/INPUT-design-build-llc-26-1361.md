Plaintiff Design Build, LLC ("DBLLC"), by and through its undersigned counsel, brings this action against the United States of America under the Contract Disputes Act ("CDA"), 41 U.S.C. §§ 7101–7109, to appeal two Contracting Officer's Final Decisions ("COFDs") denying three certified claims arising from the performance of Contract No. W912EP21C0010 for the Fort Buchanan Family Housing Replacement project, Fort Buchanan, Puerto Rico.

## NATURE OF THE ACTION

1\. This action arises from a pattern of conduct in which the United States Army Corps of Engineers ("USACE") furnished defective and internally inconsistent design specifications, took extended periods to resolve discrepancies identified by DBLLC, directed changes that disrupted construction sequencing, issued improper and unwarranted stop-work orders for minor alleged workmanship issues, and then refused to compensate DBLLC for the resulting delays and increased costs.

2\. DBLLC seeks recovery on three certified claims: Claim 0002, seeking $1,309,979.14 in delay damages caused by defective window and door thermal-performance specifications; Claim 0003, seeking $2,450,386.87 in delay damages caused by defective CMU wall bent-plate connection specifications, a stop-work order, and the government's delayed issuance of corrective drawings; and Claim 0004, seeking $654,844.76 in delay damages for the additional time required to complete CMU walls under the government's revised design.

3\. Together, DBLLC's claims seek approximately $4.4 million in delay damages plus associated time extensions to the contract completion date, with CDA interest running from the date of each claim's submission to the Contracting Officer.

## PARTIES

4\. Plaintiff Design Build, LLC, is a limited liability company organized and existing under the laws of Puerto Rico, with its principal place of business in Puerto Rico. DBLLC is the prime contractor for the Fort Buchanan Family Housing Replacement project under Contract No. W912EP21C0010.

5\. Defendant is the United States of America, acting through the United States Army Corps of Engineers, the federal agency responsible for administering the Contract at issue.

## JURISDICTION AND VENUE

6\. This Court has jurisdiction over this action under the Contract Disputes Act, 41 U.S.C. § 7104(b)(1), and the Tucker Act, 28 U.S.C. § 1491(a)(2), which grant the United States Court of Federal Claims jurisdiction over claims by contractors against the United States relating to contracts with the federal government.

7\. DBLLC submitted its claims to the Contracting Officer in writing, certified each claim as required by 41 U.S.C. § 7103(b) and FAR 52.233-1, and received Contracting Officer's Final Decisions denying the claims. DBLLC now brings this action within the twelve-month limitations period prescribed by 41 U.S.C. § 7104(b)(1).

8\. The Contracting Officer issued the COFD denying Claim 0002 on September 18, 2025. This Complaint is filed within twelve months of DBLLC's receipt of that decision.

9\. On July 8, 2026, the government issued letter AFHC-C-0244, which constituted the COFD on Claims 0003 and 0004. This Complaint is filed within twelve months of DBLLC's receipt of that decision.

10\. Venue is proper in this Court under 28 U.S.C. § 1491(a)(2).

## THE CONTRACT

11\. On or about September 24, 2021, the United States, acting through USACE, awarded Contract No. W912EP21C0010 to DBLLC for the construction of family housing replacement units at Fort Buchanan, Puerto Rico (the "Contract").

12\. The Contract is a firm-fixed-price construction contract and incorporates, among other provisions, the Federal Acquisition Regulation ("FAR") clauses applicable to fixed-price construction contracts, including without limitation: FAR 52.233-1 (Disputes); FAR 52.243-4 (Changes); FAR 52.242-14 (Suspension of Work); and FAR 52.236-2 (Differing Site Conditions).

13\. The contract also incorporates FAR 52.233-4 (Applicable Law for Breach of Contract Claim), which states that United States law will apply to resolve any claim of breach of this contract.

14\. The Contract required DBLLC to construct residential housing units in accordance with government-furnished design specifications and drawings. The government retained responsibility for the adequacy of its design specifications under the implied warranty recognized in *United States v. Spearin*, 248 U.S. 132 (1918), and its progeny.

## FACTUAL BACKGROUND

### A. Claim 0002: Window and Door Specification Changes

15\. The Contract specifications originally required windows and doors to meet a thermal-performance U-factor of 0.40. USACE issued Request for Proposal No. 0002 ("RFP-0002"), which revised the U-factor requirement downward from 0.40 to 0.30, a change that made fabrication of compliant aluminum windows commercially impossible given the simultaneous impact-resistance requirements established by the Contract.

16\. The combined thermal-performance requirement (U-factor of 0.30) and impact-resistance requirements imposed by the specifications could not simultaneously be met with commercially available products.

17\. USACE subsequently removed the 0.30 U-factor revision and, on April 23, 2023, granted DBLLC a thermal-performance variance allowing U-factors up to 0.53. This variance effectively acknowledged that the prior specification was unachievable.

18\. On March 13, 2023, DBLLC transmitted window submittals proposing U-factors up to 0.53, as authorized by USACE's April 23, 2023, variance approval. The government's review process for these submittals extended over multiple months while DBLLC's window and door procurement remained on hold.

19\. On June 8, 2023, DBLLC submitted REA-001 to the Contracting Officer seeking $473,725.21 in material cost increases and a 169-calendar-day time extension for delays caused by the specification changes and the government's unreasonably extended review process.

20\. On or about October 6, 2023, the Administrative Contracting Officer ("ACO") acknowledged that the specification issues caused schedule impacts and granted 169 excusable days. The ACO's determination that schedule impacts resulted from the thermal-performance requirements supports causation for DBLLC's delay damages.

21\. Modification A00014 to the Contract, executed thereafter, granted DBLLC 162 excusable, non-compensable days. The modification stated that the time extension was a "complete equitable adjustment for the time extension portion" and "released the government from further equitable adjustments attributable to the facts and circumstances of REA-001," but expressly reserved DBLLC's "right to submit the alleged contract increase portion."

22\. In accordance with DBLLC's express reservation of rights, on June 19, 2025, DBLLC submitted amended Claim 002 as a certified claim under FAR 52.233-1, seeking $1,309,979.14 in delay damages—including extended field-office overhead and home-office overhead—attributable to the 169 days of excusable delay caused by the government's specification changes. DBLLC certified the claim as required by 41 U.S.C. § 7103(b).

23\. The Contracting Officer issued the COFD for Claim 0002 on September 18, 2025. The COFD improperly and erroneously denied Claim 002 primarily on the grounds that the release in Modification A00014 constituted a complete bar to recovery.

24\. Additionally, the government's use of the Default Clause to characterize the 169 days as "excusable but non-compensable" is inconsistent with FAR 52.249-10. The government previously acknowledged the delay was government-caused through defective specifications and directed changes—not an "unforeseeable cause beyond the control" of the contractor. When the government itself causes delay through design defects, the Changes clause (FAR 52.243-4)—not the Default clause—is the proper vehicle, and compensation is required.

25\. The government knew or should have known that its specifications were defective and that its extended review process was delaying DBLLC's procurement of windows and doors.

### B. CMU Wall System Design Defects and Delays

26\. The CMU wall system issues underlying Claims 0003 and 0004 arose from the design and construction of the concrete masonry unit ("CMU") wall system specified in the Contract. The Contract drawings originally required CMU walls to be connected to roof beams through steel bent plates.

27\. On September 10, 2023, DBLLC submitted Request for Information No. 0058 ("RFI-0058") to the government after identifying constructability and compatibility concerns between the specified bent-plate connection detail and the exterior wall finish assembly. Specifically, DBLLC identified 11 drawing sheets that contained contradictory construction details regarding the bent-plate configuration and the Direct Applied Finish System ("DAFS").

28\. The government took 46 days to respond to RFI-0058. During that period, while USACE evaluated a design concern requiring government direction, DBLLC continued taking reasonable steps toward performance, including coordinating with suppliers, investigating constructability issues, developing alternative solutions, and preparing submittals and other project deliverables. On October 24, 2023—two days before the government's response—DBLLC submitted an alternative design approach addressing the identified conflicts. On October 26, 2023, the government directed DBLLC to proceed with the original bent-plate design, characterizing the discrepancies across 11 drawing sheets as mere "typographical errors" despite the scope of the inconsistency.

29\. During and after the 46-day response period, DBLLC did not remain idle or simply wait for the government to resolve the design conflict. Rather than committing the bent plates to fabrication while constructability and system-compatibility issues remained unresolved, DBLLC continued active project performance by submitting RFIs, submittals, and other project deliverables; investigating the conflicts between the bent-plate connection and the wall finish assembly; coordinating with suppliers and manufacturers regarding fabrication and finish compatibility; developing alternative design approaches; and seeking USACE direction.

## CONTRACTING OFFICER'S FINAL DECISIONS

49\. Under the CDA, 41 U.S.C. § 7103(a), a contractor claim must be submitted in writing to the contracting officer. Claims exceeding $100,000 must be certified by the contractor as required by 41 U.S.C. § 7103(b) and FAR 52.233-1.

50\. Each of DBLLC's claims was submitted in writing, sought payment as a matter of right in a sum certain, and was certified as required by 41 U.S.C. § 7103(b). The Contracting Officer issued written final decisions on each claim.

51\. DBLLC submitted amended Claim 002 on June 19, 2025, seeking $1,309,979.14 in delay damages. The claim was certified as required by the CDA. On September 18, 2025, the Contracting Officer issued a written Final Decision denying Claim 002 (the "Claim 0002 COFD"). The COFD denied Claim 002 primarily on release grounds tied to Modification A00014.

52\. DBLLC submitted Claim 003 on or about July 21, 2025, seeking $2,142,867.11 and a 265-calendar-day time extension. DBLLC submitted Claim 004 on July 23, 2025, seeking $501,350.04 and a 62-calendar-day time extension. Both claims were certified as required by the CDA.

53\. The government's March 20, 2026, interim determination on Claims 003 and 004 found partial merit, recognizing 22 calendar days for awaiting revised detail from the Government, and 75 calendar days for additional work/rework—97 calendar days in total—as excusable and compensable government delay, while denying substantial portions of the claimed time extension and damages.

54\. On July 8, 2026, the government issued letter AFHC-C-0244, constituting the Contracting Officer's Final Decision on Claims 0003 and 0004 (the "Claims 0003/0004 COFD").

55\. On July 2, 2026, DBLLC had transmitted a request for clarification regarding whether letter AFHC-C-0244 constituted a formal COFD. By letter dated July 15, 2026 (AFHC-C-0251), the government acknowledged DBLLC's request and confirmed that letter AFHC-C-0244 dated July 8, 2026, constituted the formal Contracting Officer's Final Decision for Claims 0003 and 0004. Notably, the COFD did not provide a competing schedule analysis or otherwise rebut DBLLC's schedule analysis supporting DBLLC's claim.

56\. Under 41 U.S.C. § 7104(b)(1), a contractor may bring an action on a claim in the United States Court of Federal Claims within twelve months from the date of receipt of a contracting officer's decision. This Complaint is filed within twelve months of each COFD and is therefore timely as to all claims.

## COUNT I

### Breach of Contract

57\. DBLLC incorporates by reference the allegations in paragraphs 1 through 56 above as though fully set forth herein.

58\. As set forth in Claim 0002, the government breached the express terms of the Contract, including the Changes clause (FAR 52.243-4), by directing changes to the window and door thermal-performance specifications, causing 169 days of delay to DBLLC's performance, and then refusing to provide an equitable adjustment for the resulting delay damages. The government's revisions to the U-factor specification, extended review of DBLLC's submittals, and ultimate grant of a variance constituted a change to the Contract for which compensation is required under FAR 52.243-4.

59\. As set forth in Claims 0003 and 0004, the government breached the express terms of the Contract, including the Changes clause (FAR 52.243-4) and the Suspension of Work clause (FAR 52.242-14), by: (a) furnishing defective and internally inconsistent drawings for the CMU wall bent-plate connection; (b) directing DBLLC to proceed with a design the government knew to be flawed; (c) issuing a stop-work order on CMU wall construction; (d) taking approximately 327 days to resolve the design deficiency and issue corrective drawings; (e) directing DBLLC to construct CMU walls under a revised design that imposed new requirements not in the original Contract, including the bond-beam grout-to-ceiling requirement; and (f) refusing to provide a full equitable adjustment for the resulting delays and increased costs.

60\. The government breached its implied duty of good faith and fair dealing by, among other things: (a) furnishing design specifications it knew or should have known were defective; (b) taking unreasonable periods to respond to RFIs identifying specification defects; (c) dismissing significant design discrepancies as typographical errors rather than subsequently acknowledging and resolving them but failing to compensate DBLLC; (d) issuing stop-work orders that were improper and unwarranted given the minor nature of the cited workmanship issues, and then contending that those orders created concurrent delay; and (e) seeking to avoid payment of compensation for government-caused delay by characterizing excusable delay as non-compensable through improper application of the Default Clause. In each case, the government's actions and inactions interfered with DBLLC's performance and/or acted to destroy the reasonable expectations of DBLLC regarding the fruits of the contract.

61\. The government breached its implied duty to cooperate and not to hinder DBLLC's performance by: (a) taking 46 days to respond to RFI-0058 and then providing an inadequate response; (b) failing to resolve the bent-plate design defect for approximately 327 days while DBLLC's CMU wall work was disrupted; (c) controlling CMU wall progression by limiting which housing units could proceed and imposing maximum wall elevation restrictions pending its resolution of the issue; and (d) issuing conflicting directives regarding bent-plate procurement and installation. In each case, the government's conduct was unreasonable, or the government wrongfully failed or refused to take an action essential to DBLLC's performance.

62\. The government breached the implied warranty of specification adequacy by furnishing defective design specifications for both the window/door thermal-performance requirements and the CMU wall bent-plate connection. Specifically, the government breached its duty as follows:

- a. With respect to Claim 0002, the combined thermal-performance (U-factor) and impact-resistance requirements made commercially available compliant windows impossible or impracticable to obtain, constituting a classic defective specification. The government's own acknowledgment of the defect—through the variance, RFP-0002 revision, and ACO schedule-impact determination—confirms that satisfactory performance could not result from adherence to the specifications as written.
- b. With respect to Claims 0003 and 0004, the government's drawings contained internal inconsistencies across 11 sheets regarding the bent-plate connection detail and exterior wall finish assembly. These inconsistencies rendered the drawings defective because a contractor could not simultaneously comply with all specified requirements. The government's ultimate elimination of bent plates entirely—with acknowledgment that costs would be borne by the government—confirms the defective nature of the original specification.

63\. The government's actions with respect to all three claims constituted constructive changes to the Contract. The government's specification revisions, extended review processes, directed redesign of the CMU wall system, issuance of revised drawings imposing new requirements, and other informal directives effectively altered the contract requirements and increased the cost and time of performance without formal change orders providing equitable adjustments.

64\. With respect to Claims 0003 and 0004, the government's issuance of a stop-work order on CMU wall construction from approximately May 22, 2024, to July 11, 2024, constituted an unreasonable suspension of work within the meaning of FAR 52.242-14.

65\. As a direct and proximate result of the government's breaches described above, DBLLC has suffered damages including, but not limited to, extended field-office overhead, home-office overhead, and other delay-related costs in amounts to be proven at trial, but not less than: $1,309,979.14 for Claim 0002; $2,450,386.87 for Claim 0003; and $654,844.76 for Claim 0004.
