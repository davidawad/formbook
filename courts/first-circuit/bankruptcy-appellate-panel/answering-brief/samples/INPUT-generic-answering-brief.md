# Argument

## The Security Interest Was Not Perfected for the Full Claim

The bankruptcy court correctly held that the financing statement did not perfect Appellant's interest in equipment that the Debtor had moved to a different county after filing. The statute requires a filing that identifies the collateral and the place where it is kept. See [*Gamma Trust v. Delta Estate*, 300 F.3d 30, 36 (1st Cir. 2002)](toa:gamma). The record shows that the filing listed only the original location.

Appellant's reading would require the Panel to treat an unperfected interest as perfected. The Panel should not adopt that reading. [*Alpha Corp. v. Beta LLC*, 200 B.R. 20, 27 (Bankr. D. Mass. 1999)](toa:alpha).

## The Valuation Did Not Depend on the Excluded Exhibit

The court's valuation rests on the testimony of both witnesses, not only on the late exhibit. The court found that the equipment's condition was the principal driver of value, and that finding is supported by the record. App. at 22-23. Appellant's contention that the court relied on the exhibit is not borne out by the order.

Any error in excluding the exhibit was harmless. Appellant made an offer of proof and does not explain how the exhibit would have changed the result. Under [11 U.S.C. § 506(a)](toa:sample-stat), the value of the collateral is a question of fact on which the court's finding is entitled to deference.

## The Proceeds Argument Was Not Properly Preserved

Appellant's pre-trial memorandum raised proceeds in a single sentence and did not ask the court to rule on it. A party that does not press an argument below cannot complain that the court did not decide it. App. at 31.

# Conclusion

The order of the bankruptcy court should be affirmed.
