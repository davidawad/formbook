# I. Constitutional Authority, Jurisdiction, and Venue

The Court has constitutional authority to enter final orders on this Motion, it has jurisdiction over this case pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1334, this is a statutorily core matter pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 157(b)(2)(A) and (O), and venue is proper pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1409(a).

## F. Beliveau Does not Request Any Relief under 11 U.S.C. § 324(b)

Notwithstanding § 324(b)’s requirement that removed trustees be removed from all other cases in which they are serving, Beliveau does not seek that relief upon the Trustee’s removal in this case under § 324(a). Beliveau leaves it to the Court and the UST to decide whether the Trustee, given his conduct in this case, is fit to serve as a trustee in other cases.

# IV. Request for Expedited Determination

Beliveau seeks expedited determination of this Motion because the Court, sua sponte, scheduled a hearing on the Trustee’s Application to Employ R&B for October 6, 2026 at 9:00 a.m.

Beliveau will be filing an Objection to that Application to Employ in advance of the scheduled hearing. He will also be filing an Objection to the Trustee’s Motion for Attorney Braunstein to appear pro hac vice within the 14 days provided for in the Court’s approval Order.

Beliveau submits that determination of this Motion for Removal should be heard in advance of the Application to Employ R&B, because if cause exists to remove the Trustee under § 324(a), then the successor trustee should have the ability and opportunity to determine whether retention of R&B is in the best interests of the estate, or not.

For these reasons, Beliveau submits that this Motion should be considered on an expedited basis on October 6, 2026 at 9:00 a.m.

# V. Conclusion

The Trustee’s handling of this case has been a study of consistently, impossibly-poor judgment, incompetently executed, over substantive legal matters of which the Trustee has only the barest and superficial understanding. The “administration” of this case has been a debacle.

The Trustee has not administered anything in almost two years. Now the “claims” he does want to “administer” are completely made up, nowhere supported by Debtor’s Amended Schedules, and directly contrary to the same claims he was cajoling people with a few months ago. And because he has bungled every aspect of the case, he has breached multiple fiduciary duties owed to creditors of the estate, he has engaged in multiple instances of negligence, and he has created exposure for himself, his firm, and the estate in the form of malpractice claims and otherwise.

Worse than all that, though, is that his animating motivation in pursuing the current R&B Proceeding is purely retaliation against Beliveau for his perceived role in challenging the Trustee’s “authority” and of preventing the Trustee’s many efforts to give away estate assets for no consideration for the direct benefit of Debtor and Marcus Clegg, all of which was rightfully rejected by the Court. And because the Trustee and Debtor now share a common disdain for Beliveau and his counsel, they are now coordinating to inflict as much financial and other harm on him as possible, which, in turn, has deprived of the Trustee of his once “disinterested” status.

Finally, the Trustee intentionally and knowingly opted for stealth and secret over transparency and objective assessment vis-à-vis the Court and parties-in-interest. He could have sought the Court’s approval to retain R&B well before the firm commenced work on its bloated, internally inconsistent, and nonsensical Complaint, but instead, knowing that that that approach was likely to be met with substantial skepticism from the Court given the circumstances, he intentionally chose to expose the estate to a substantial benefit administrative expense claim, put all fees of R&B at risk, and ask the Court for its rubber-stamp approval of retention, but only after-the-fact.

It is difficult to understand how the Trustee has been entrusted with the role of a fiduciary, an individual required and expected to exercise good, sound, and knowledgeable judgment for the benefit of constituents. That is not who the Trustee is, or what he is capable of. Under the totality of the circumstances, cause exists for the removal of the Trustee under § 324(a) of the Code.
