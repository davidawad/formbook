# Supreme Judicial Court of Maine: briefs, motions, petitions and amicus briefs

Alias `me`. Scope: filings in the Supreme Judicial Court sitting as the Law Court: briefs (answering, reply, brief on the merits), motions with supporting memoranda, petitions (petition for review, petition for writ, petition for rehearing) and amicus curiae briefs. Verified against the Maine Rules of Appellate Procedure (2024-11-01 edition) and the Maine Rules of Civil Procedure (2026-06-01 edition), both as posted on the Judicial Branch rules index, on 2026-10-21.

## Briefs: cover, order and length (appellant, appellee, reply, brief on the merits)

| ID | Requirement | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| ME-COVER-001 | Front cover states, in order: (A) the Supreme Judicial Court sitting as the Law Court and the Law Court docket number; (B) the title of the case; (C) the nature of the proceeding (for example Appeal, Report or Certified Question) and the court, agency or entity appealed from; (D) the title of the document (for example Brief for Appellant); (E) the names and addresses of counsel for the party on whose behalf the brief is filed. | MRAP 7(g)(5) | M |
| ME-COVER-002 | The paper cover of an appellant's brief is blue, an appellee's red, an intervenor's or amicus's green, and a reply brief's gray. The rule governs the paper cover; the e-filed PDF carries no colored stock. | MRAP 7(g)(6) | A |
| ME-ORDER-001 | Appellant's brief contains, under appropriate headings and in this order: a table of contents with page references; a table of authorities listing each case, statute and other authority with page references; an optional short introduction; the statement of facts with procedural history and record or appendix cites; the statement of the issues presented; an optional summary of argument; the argument; and a short conclusion stating the precise relief sought. | MRAP 7A(a)(1)(A)-(H) | M |
| ME-ORDER-002 | The argument for each issue begins with a statement of the standard of appellate review that applies to that issue. | MRAP 7A(a)(1)(G) | M |
| ME-ORDER-003 | Appellee's brief follows the appellant's order, except that the statement of the issues, the standards of review and the statement of facts may be omitted unless the appellee is dissatisfied with the appellant's statements. | MRAP 7A(b) | M |
| ME-ORDER-004 | A reply brief is strictly confined to replying to new facts or arguments raised in the appellee's brief. No further brief is filed without leave of the Law Court. | MRAP 7A(c) | M |
| ME-ORDER-005 | A brief does not include documents or images outside the trial court file or record, images of minors, adults under guardianship or commitment, or nudity or sexual acts, and does not include material made confidential by statute or order (absent leave). | MRAP 7A(a)(2) | M |
| ME-ORDER-006 | Cross-appeal briefs: the second party's brief contains the issues and argument on the cross-appeal and the answer to the appellant's brief. | MRAP 7A(d) | M |
| ME-LEN-001 | A principal brief (appellant or appellee) may not exceed the greater of 40 pages or 10,000 words without prior approval of the Law Court. | MRAP 7(f)(1) | M |
| ME-LEN-002 | A reply brief may not exceed 15 pages or 4,500 words without prior approval. The rule says "or", so the template treats both measures as limits and reads the stricter one. | MRAP 7(f)(1) | M |
| ME-LEN-003 | The cover page, table of contents, table of authorities, certificate of service and any appendix bound with the appellant's brief are not counted in the page or word limits. | MRAP 7(f)(3) | M |
| ME-LEN-004 | The principal brief of an appellant or appellee may include an attachment of up to 3 pages of trial court record documents. | MRAP 7(f)(2) | A |

## Motions and supporting memoranda

| ID | Requirement | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| ME-MOT-001 | Each motion or other document has a caption setting forth the Supreme Judicial Court sitting as the Law Court, the title of the case, the Law Court docket number, and a brief descriptive title of the document. | MRAP 1D(d) | M |
| ME-MOT-002 | A motion is by motion, states with particularity its grounds and the order or relief sought, and is signed under Rule 1C. Supporting papers are filed and served with the motion. | MRAP 10(a)(1) | M |
| ME-MOT-003 | A motion states that opposing counsel and unrepresented parties were notified, their positions on the relief, and whether any intends to respond. | MRAP 10(a)(2) | M |
| ME-MOT-004 | A motion for an extension of time, or a delay of more than seven days, by counsel states that the represented party was notified of the filing. | MRAP 10(a)(3) | M (conditional) |
| ME-MOT-005 | A motion seeking emergency or expedited relief is labeled "Emergency Motion"; it is preceded by advance notice where possible, states the nature of the emergency and the harm, and states the date by which the Law Court must act. | MRAP 10(a)(4)(A)-(E) | M (conditional) |
| ME-MOT-006 | Motions and responses are typewritten and conform to Rule 1D(d). | MRAP 10(a)(1), 10(c) | M |
| ME-MOT-007 | No page or word limit is set for a motion or its supporting papers in Rule 10. | MRAP 10 | A |

## Petitions: rehearing (motion for reconsideration) and review

| ID | Requirement | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| ME-REH-001 | A motion for reconsideration of a Law Court decision, with the fee in the Court Fees Schedule, is filed with the Clerk within 14 days after the decision. | MRAP 14(b)(1)(A) | M |
| ME-REH-002 | The motion states with particularity the points of law or fact the Court overlooked or misapprehended, and conforms to Rule 1D(d). No response is filed unless requested. | MRAP 14(b)(1)(A)-(B) | M |
| ME-REH-003 | A motion for reconsideration of an order (not a decision) is limited to an error, omission or new material that could not previously have been presented. | MRAP 10(a)(5) | M (conditional) |
| ME-PET-001 | A petition to the single justice for habeas corpus (14 M.R.S. § 5501 et seq.) and a petition on a contested municipal election (21-A M.R.S. § 696) are original petitions. Civil Rule 81(b)(1) leaves that practice to statute and common law, so the Civil Rules do not set their form; the template applies the Rule 1D(d) caption and the 14-point double-spaced form, which the Law Court's rules set for documents it receives. | MRCP 81(b)(1)(A), (F); MRAP 1D(d) | C |
| ME-PET-002 | A petition has a caption naming the court, the title of the action with all parties named on the first side, the docket number, and a descriptive title, and is dated. | MRCP 10(a) (form of pleadings, applied by analogy); MRAP 1D(d) | C |

## Amicus curiae briefs

| ID | Requirement | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| ME-AMC-001 | An amicus brief may be filed only with the written consent of all parties or by leave of the Law Court. A motion for leave identifies the applicant's interest and states why an amicus brief is desirable. | MRAP 7(e)(1)(A) | M |
| ME-AMC-002 | An amicus brief is filed by the date the appellee's brief is due, unless the Law Court grants leave for later filing. | MRAP 7(e)(1)(B) | M |
| ME-AMC-003 | An amicus brief is subject to the principal-brief limit (greater of 40 pages or 10,000 words). | MRAP 7(f)(1) | M |
| ME-AMC-004 | An amicus brief's cover uses the amicus cover content of Rule 7(g)(5) and the green cover color of Rule 7(g)(6). | MRAP 7(g)(5)-(6) | A |
