# Jurisdiction & Venue

This Court has jurisdiction over this Adversary Proceeding pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1334. Pursuant to 28 U.S.C. §157(b)(2)(J) this matter is a core proceeding. Venue is proper in this Court pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1409. Plaintiff has standing to pursue this Adversary Proceeding pursuant to 11 U.S.C. §§ 307 and 727(d).

# The Parties

Plaintiff is the U.S. Trustee for Region 1, duly appointed pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 581(a)(1).

Defendant is Kim Ziegelmayer an individual with a last known address at 334 Waterman Avenue, Smithfield, RI 02917 (“Defendant”).

# Factual Background

Sunflower Real Estate, LLC is a Rhode Island LLC formed on or about October 21, 2014 (“Sunflower”).

Lynda L. Glynn (“Ms. Glynn”) is and has been the sole member of Sunflower since its formation.

Ms. Glynn formed sunflower at the direction of the Defendant.

The Defendant directed Ms. Glynn to form Sunflower so there would be a separate legal entity for the Defendant to start a new business venture of buying and flipping real estate.

Upon information and belief, Sunflower was capitalized with an initial investment of $200,000 in October 2014. A friend of the Defendant, Mr. Mauro Poletti, of New South Wales, Australia, made the initial investment.

The Defendant did not become a member of Sunflower to conceal her financial transactions from her divorce proceeding and her contemplated filing for bankruptcy.

At all times relevant to this complaint Ms. Glynn was the only person with signature authority on the checking account of Sunflower maintained at Coventry Credit Union.

Ms. Glynn was authorized by the Defendant to make only small administrative payments on behalf of Sunflower. Sunflower compensated Ms. Glynn $35 for her accounting work and was to pay Ms. Glynn real estate sales commissions on the sales of real estate.

Ms. Glynn signed Sunflower checks in blank and provided them to the Defendant.

The Defendant completed the pre-signed Sunflower checks and used some of them to pay herself.

The Defendant used the Sunflower pre-signed checks to pay herself monthly compensation of $2,500.

The Defendant used the Sunflower pre-signed checks to make transfers from Sunflower to herself totaling $68,825 from January 2017 through June 30, 2017.

The January to June 2017 transfers of $68,825, include the Debtor’s regular monthly compensation, other checks made payable to the Defendant, and a check made payable to an account of the Defendant at TD Ameritrade for $6,500.

At times relevant to this complaint, the Defendant had access to the Sunflower bank accounts using online access including the ability to move funds between the savings and checking account. The Defendant used her online access to the Sunflower bank accounts to facilitate her distributions from Sunflower.

At times relevant to this complaint, the Defendant had access to the Sunflower bank accounts by use of a debit card.

Ms. Glynn did not have a debit card to access the Sunflower bank accounts.

The Defendant had discretion over the issuance of checks and the use of the debit card for Sunflower’s bank account.

The Defendant used the Sunflower bank accounts for her personal banking needs.

The Defendant, on or about May 2, 2016, received a $50,000 check from her mother made payable to cash.

The Defendant delivered the $50,000 check to Ms. Glynn to have it deposited into the Sunflower savings account.

The Defendant caused the $50,000 check to be deposited into the Sunflower bank account to conceal and protect it from her creditors and in anticipation of filing bankruptcy.

The Defendant used the assets of Sunflower to pay some of her personal living expenses.

Upon information and belief and subject to further discovery, the Defendant used the Sunflower bank account to pay her divorce attorney.

Upon information and belief and subject to further discovery, the Defendant used the Sunflower bank account to pay her bankruptcy attorney.

The Defendant used the assets of Sunflower for personal travel with her children to Arizona.

On December 24, 2014, Sunflower bought the property located at 334 Waterman Ave, Smithfield, RI 02917 (“Waterman Ave”).

Sunflower paid to rehabilitate the Waterman Ave property.

The Waterman Ave property is a duplex and the Defendant has lived in one of the two units rent-free from approximately July 2016 to the present.

A tenant occupies the second unit of the Waterman Ave property. The tenant delivers the rent payments to the Defendant.

Sunflower owns a car, which is used exclusively by the Defendant.

As of October 16, 2018, the Defendant continues to use the car owned by Sunflower.

The Defendant enjoyed substantially all of the attributes of ownership of Sunflower and its assets, but for legal title, in an effort to maintain her lifestyle and shield assets from the reach of her creditors.

The Defendant filed a voluntary petition to commence this case on August 4, 2017 (“Petition Date”).

The Defendant as of Petition Date had an interest in Sunflower.

The Defendant did not disclose her interest in Sunflower in her Schedules or Statements filed in her bankruptcy case.

This deposition was taken in connection with a lawsuit brought by Mr. Poletti against Ms. Glynn, Sunflower and others for breach of contract, fraud, conversion and other counts in connection with Sunflower.

# Claims for Relief

## COUNT I

### Revocation of Discharge under 11 U.S.C. § 727(d)(1)

The U.S. Trustee incorporates the allegations contained within all of the preceding paragraphs of this complaint as if fully set forth herein.

The Defendant’s discharge was obtained through the fraud of the Defendant.

The U.S. Trustee did not know of the fraud until after the granting of such discharge.

This fraudulent conduct warrants revocation and denial of the Defendant’s discharge pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 727(d).

WHEREFORE, the U.S. Trustee respectfully requests that this Court enter an Order:

1. Revoking the discharge granted to the Defendant under an Order of this Court dated December 8, 2017, pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 727(d); and
2. Granting the U.S. Trustee such other and further relief as is just and equitable.
