## Nature of the Action

This is a federal securities-fraud action arising from Defendants’ solicitation and sale of membership units or common units in New American Manufacturing LLC, a Wyoming limited liability company.

Plaintiff is an Oklahoma investor. Defendants propositioned Plaintiff to invest in NAM in connection with activities, meetings, communications, and/or solicitations occurring in or directed from Texas, Oklahoma, and interstate channels of commerce.

Defendants sold, offered, or participated in the sale of securities to Plaintiff, including NAM membership units/common units, by means of materially false and misleading statements and omissions concerning, among other things, the value of the securities, the capitalization and unit structure of NAM, the investment terms, the existence and effect of buyback or return-of-investment protections, the Company’s financial condition, the use of investor funds, related-party participation, and the risks and restrictions associated with the investment.

The Subscription Agreement states that J.K. Lord, LLC purchased 2,000 Units at $10.00 per Unit for a stated purchase price of $20,000, and that NAM would issue 8,000 additional bonus units at $0.01 per unit with equivalent value to all units in the Company. “Subscriber wishes to purchase from the Company, and the Company wishes to issue to Subscriber, 2,000 Units (the "Purchased Units"), at a price of $10.00 per Unit, for a total purchase price $20,000 (the "Purchased Price").” “As consideration for entering into this Agreement, the Company will issue 8,000 additional bonus units to the Subscriber at $0.01 per unit to the Subscriber. The bonus units have equivalent value to all units in the Company.”

A Limited Personal Guaranty associated with the transaction states that, pursuant to a subscription agreement, J.K. Lord, LLC purchased 10,000 Units for a total purchase price of $100,000, and that the subscriber was willing to provide the purchase price only if Corbin Blume executed and delivered a limited personal guaranty. “WHEREAS, pursuant to that certain Subscription Agreement (the "Agreement") for Units of New American Manufacturing LLC (the "Company"), dated on August 28, 2024, J.K. Lord, LLC ("Subscriber") purchased 10,000 Units (the "Purchased Units") for a total purchase price of $100,000 (the "Purchased Price");” and “WHEREAS, Subscriber was willing to provide the Purchased Price to the Company only if the undersigned (the "Guarantor") executes and delivers this limited personal guaranty (this "Guaranty").”

The term sheet represented that NAM was a private Wyoming company, that it had a capitalization of 1,319,320 Common Units, that it would issue 2,000,000 Common Units at $12.50 per unit, that funding could be up to $50,000,000, and that Corbin Blume, through San Gabriel Assets LLC, would participate and purchase common units for up to $25,000,000. “It presently has a capitalization consisting of 1,319,320 of Common Units (the “Common Units”). The Company will issue 2,000,000 Common Units fully diluted at $12.50 per common Unit.” “Funding Up to $50,000,000.” “Mr. Corbin Blume, CEO of the Company, through his private holding company San Gabriel Assets LLC, will participate and purchase Common Units for up to $25,000,000 USD.” In addition, 500 bonus units were committed for Weg Energy which were never delivered.

Defendants’ representations were materially misleading because, among other things, the transaction documents themselves contain inconsistent investment amounts, unit counts, pricing, dates, and closing terms; Defendants failed to disclose material facts necessary to make their statements not misleading; and Defendants used contractual disclaimers and investor representations to attempt to shift responsibility for Defendants’ own misconduct to Plaintiff.

Plaintiff seeks rescission, rescissory damages, actual damages, consequential damages, interest, attorneys’ fees where available, costs, and all other relief allowed by law.

## Jurisdiction and Venue

This Court has subject-matter jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 because this action arises under the Securities Act of 1933 and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934.

This Court has jurisdiction under 15 U.S.C. § 77v(a) for claims arising under the Securities Act of 1933 and under 15 U.S.C. § 78aa for claims arising under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934.

This Court has supplemental jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1367 over Plaintiff’s related state-law claims because they arise from the same case or controversy as the federal securities claims.

Personal jurisdiction is proper because Defendants purposefully directed investment solicitations, communications, transaction documents, and/or securities sales to Plaintiff in Oklahoma; accepted funds or commitments from an Oklahoma investor; and caused injury to Plaintiff in Oklahoma.

Venue is proper in this District under 28 U.S.C. § 1391 and the federal securities venue provisions because Plaintiff resides in Oklahoma, Defendants transmitted securities-related communications into Oklahoma, and a substantial part of the events, omissions, losses, and effects giving rise to the claims occurred in Oklahoma. Venue is also proper because Defendants used interstate commerce, wires, emails, phone calls, electronic signatures, bank wires, and/or other instrumentalities of interstate commerce in connection with the offer and sale of the securities.

The NAM membership units, common units, purchased units, bonus units, and related contractual rights sold or offered to Plaintiff are “securities” under the federal securities laws, including investment contracts, notes, evidence of indebtedness, and/or transferable investment interests.

## Factual Allegations

### A. Defendants solicited Plaintiff to invest in NAM

Defendants propositioned Plaintiff, an Oklahoma investor, to invest in NAM in connection with activities and communications involving Texas, Oklahoma, and interstate commerce.

Defendants represented that NAM was a private manufacturing company issuing equity units and that Plaintiff’s investment would acquire a defined number of units at a stated price.

Defendants represented that the investment carried valuable protections, including bonus units and a personal guaranty or buyback right from Blume.

Defendants supplied or caused to be supplied transaction documents, including a Subscription Agreement, term sheet, and Limited Personal Guaranty.

### B. The Subscription Agreement represented that Plaintiff would receive purchased and bonus units

The Subscription Agreement states that it was entered into by NAM and J.K. Lord, LLC as subscriber “as of August 29, 2024,” although the signature block is dated January 28, 2025. “THIS SUBSCRIPTION AGREEMENT (this "Agreement") is entered into by and between NEW AMERICAN MANUFACTURING LLC, a Wyoming limited liability company (the "Company"), and J.K. Lord, LLC the undersigned Subscriber in the Company ("Subscriber") as of August 29, 2024.Dated: January 28, 2025”

The Subscription Agreement states that the subscriber would purchase 2,000 Units at $10 per unit for a total stated purchase price of $20,000. “Subscriber wishes to purchase from the Company, and the Company wishes to issue to Subscriber, 2,000 Units (the "Purchased Units"), at a price of $10.00 per Unit, for a total purchase price $20,000 (the "Purchased Price").”

The Subscription Agreement further states that NAM would issue 8,000 bonus units at $0.01 per unit, and that those bonus units had equivalent value to all units in the Company. “The Company will issue 8,000 additional bonus units to the Subscriber at $0.01 per unit to the Subscriber. The bonus units have equivalent value to all units in the Company.”

The Subscription Agreement contains investor-risk, access-to-information, no-reliance, no-registration, accredited-investor, transfer-restriction, sophistication, and confidentiality provisions. Subscribers acknowledge that, because the Purchased Units have not been registered under the Securities Act of 1933 (the "Securities Act"), and because the Company has no obligation to effect such registration, Subscriber shall continue to bear the economic risk of his investment in the Purchased Units for an indefinite period. Subscriber represents that Subscriber is an "accredited investor" as such term is defined in Regulation D promulgated by the Securities and Exchange Commission under the Securities Act

Defendants cannot avoid liability for their own securities fraud through boilerplate no-reliance, risk, access, confidentiality, or sophistication language, particularly where Defendants knew or recklessly disregarded that material representations and omissions concerning the investment were false or misleading.

### C. The Limited Personal Guaranty represented a return-of-investment or buyback protection

The Limited Personal Guaranty states that Plaintiff was willing to provide the purchase price only if Blume executed and delivered the guaranty. “WHEREAS, Subscriber was willing to provide the Purchased Price to the Company only if the undersigned (the "Guarantor") executes and delivers this limited personal guaranty (this "Guaranty").”

The Guaranty provides that Blume guaranteed, upon liquidation of the units, the return of at least the purchased amount, and that if sale proceeds were less than the purchase price, Blume would pay the difference. “Guarantor hereby guarantees to Subscriber, upon liquidation of the Units, the return of at least the Purchased Amount (the "Guaranteed Amount"). To the extent the proceeds of the sale of the Units are less than the Purchased Price, Guarantor shall pay the difference to Subscriber.”

The Guaranty further provides that, at the subscriber’s option, after 180 days, Blume or an entity of his choosing would buy back the purchased units at the purchased price, while Plaintiff would retain any bonus shares issued at the time of investment. “Notwithstanding the foregoing, at the Subscriber's option, after 180 days, Guarantor, or an entity of his choosing, will buy back the Purchased Units at the Purchased Price. The Subscriber will retain any bonus shares issued at the time of the investment.”

These guaranteed-return and buyback representations were material to Plaintiff’s investment decision.

On information and belief, Defendants failed and refused to honor the guaranty, buyback, return-of-investment promise, and/or related obligations after Plaintiff demanded performance or otherwise became entitled to payment.

### D. The term sheet represented a materially different capitalization and offering structure

The term sheet, dated January 2025, summarized an offering of NAM common units and stated that NAM had 1,319,320 Common Units outstanding and would issue 2,000,000 Common Units at $12.50 per unit. “It presently has a capitalization consisting of 1,319,320 of Common Units (the “Common Units”). The Company will issue 2,000,000 Common Units fully diluted at $12.50 per common Unit.”

The term sheet represented that the post-closing capitalization would consist of 3,319,320 Common Units fully diluted. “The closing will consist of 3,319,320 Common Units fully diluted.”

The term sheet represented that the offering involved $25,000,000 in equity issuances and funding “up to $50,000,000.”

The term sheet represented that Blume, NAM’s CEO, through San Gabriel Assets LLC, would participate by purchasing common units for up to $25,000,000. “Mr. Corbin Blume, CEO of the Company, through his private holding company San Gabriel Assets LLC, will participate and purchase a Common Units for up to $25,000,000 USD.”

These statements were material because they concerned capitalization, valuation, insider participation, related-party participation, funding capacity, dilution, and the economic value of Plaintiff’s investment.

On information and belief, Defendants failed to disclose material facts concerning whether Blume and San Gabriel Assets LLC actually invested on the represented terms, whether NAM received or had access to the represented funding, whether NAM’s capitalization was accurate, whether Plaintiff’s units were validly issued, whether bonus units were validly issued, whether any subsequent issuance diluted Plaintiff, and whether Defendants intended or were able to honor the buyback or guaranty.

### E. Defendants’ statements and omissions were materially false and misleading

Defendants made materially false or misleading statements and omissions, including but not limited to:

1. representing that Plaintiff would receive defined equity units with equivalent value while failing to disclose material facts concerning capitalization, issuance, validity, dilution, and transferability;
2. representing that Plaintiff would receive 8,000 bonus units with equivalent value while failing to disclose whether such units were actually issued, recorded, enforceable, or economically equivalent;
3. representing that the investment was protected by Blume’s personal guaranty and/or buyback obligation while failing to disclose facts showing Defendants’ lack of intent, inability, or refusal to honor that obligation;
4. representing different purchase amounts, unit counts, prices, and dates across transaction documents without adequately disclosing or reconciling the inconsistency;
5. representing or implying that Blume and San Gabriel Assets LLC would make a substantial related-party investment while failing to disclose whether that commitment was real, funded, binding, arm’s length, or intended to induce outside investors;
6. failing to disclose all compensation, commissions, finder’s fees, broker participation, or other consideration paid or promised in connection with Plaintiff’s investment;
7. failing to disclose the Company’s true financial condition, liabilities, capitalization table, assets, operations, use of proceeds, related-party transactions, and ability to perform;
8. failing to provide complete and accurate books, records, capitalization information, offering materials, financial statements, and risk disclosures; and
9. using interstate communications, electronic signatures, wires, emails, telephone calls, and written offering documents to solicit and complete the investment.

Defendants’ misstatements and omissions were material because a reasonable investor would consider them important in deciding whether to invest, how much to invest, whether to rely on the guaranty or buyback, and whether the securities had the represented value.

Plaintiff reasonably and justifiably relied on Defendants’ statements, omissions, written documents, and course of conduct in investing in NAM.

Plaintiff has suffered damages in an amount to be proven at trial, including but not limited to the investment principal, loss of use of funds, lost value of units and bonus units, consequential damages, costs, attorneys’ fees where available, and rescissory damages.
