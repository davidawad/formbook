## Filer note

The two exemplars come from different filers: a creditor's counsel (a Chapter 11 closing brief in a contested matter) and a Chapter 13 standing trustee (a short brief in a claim objection, 2017). They are from different case types and different years, which is what the tier-2 check needs. The Great Plains brief is the longer of the two and carries the table of contents, table of authorities, and an ECF header and footer on every page; the trustee's brief carries the ECF header and footer too. Neither ECF stamp is reproduced by the template (it is applied by the court's system at filing).

## What the exemplars settle, and what they do not

- Caption: both use "IN THE UNITED STATES BANKRUPTCY COURT / FOR THE EASTERN DISTRICT OF OKLAHOMA", a debtor column with "Debtor." (or "Debtor(s).") under the names, and the case number and chapter in the right column. The template follows both.
- Title: centered, bold, capitals. The Great Plains title runs to two lines.
- Body: both are double spaced in the body text, single spaced in block quotations, bullets, footnotes and the signature block. The template follows the rule (LBR 9004-1(A)) and both exemplars.
- Numbered paragraphs: the trustee's brief numbers its facts and discussion consecutively (1. through 16.). The Great Plains brief does not number paragraphs. The template numbers ordered lists across the whole brief, which matches the trustee's brief; the Great Plains sample has no numbered list.
- Signature and certificate: the Great Plains brief signs with "s/" and a firm block, and ends with a certificate of service under the signature. The trustee's brief signs with "/s/" and a block, and ends with a certificate of mailing on its own page. The template takes a certificate as ordinary body text, so either form works.
- Table of contents and table of authorities (Great Plains only): not generated by the template; see `../README.md`, "Known gaps".
