# Relief Requested

Debtor John Quincy Example ("Debtor") moves under 11 U.S.C. § 522(f)(1)(A) for an order avoiding the judicial lien held by Sample Creditor Bank ("Creditor") on Debtor's residence at 1234 Example Avenue, Tulsa, Oklahoma (the "Property"), to the extent the lien impairs Debtor's exemption in the Property. The lien is a judgment lien recorded in the records of Tulsa County, Oklahoma, on account of a money judgment entered against Debtor in 2024.

This motion is brought as a contested matter under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 4003(d) and 9014.

# Background

Debtor filed a voluntary petition under chapter 7 of title 11 on March 3, 2026. Debtor's schedules list the Property with a value of $150,000. A first mortgage held by a third party secures a debt of approximately $120,000. Debtor claims a homestead exemption in the Property, in the amount of $50,000, under 11 U.S.C. § 522(b) and applicable Oklahoma law.[^valuation]

Creditor's judgment lien attaches to the Property and secures a claim of approximately $48,000. The lien was recorded on June 9, 2024. No party has objected to Debtor's exemption claim, and the time to do so has passed.

# Argument

## Legal Standard

A judicial lien is a lien obtained by judgment, levy, sequestration, or other legal or equitable process or proceeding. 11 U.S.C. § 101(36). Creditor's lien arose from a money judgment entered against Debtor and recorded against the Property, and it is therefore a judicial lien within the meaning of the Bankruptcy Code.

Section 522(f)(1)(A) allows a debtor to avoid a judicial lien on an interest of the debtor in property to the extent the lien impairs an exemption to which the debtor would otherwise be entitled. The statute applies only to a lien that attaches to an interest the debtor could have exempted, and only to the extent of the impairment that the statute defines. The motion is therefore limited to the amount of impairment, and not to the full amount of the lien unless the impairment is at least as large.

## Section 522(f)(2)(A) Governs the Amount of the Avoidance

Section 522(f)(1)(A) permits a debtor to avoid a judicial lien on an exempt property to the extent the lien impairs the exemption. Under 11 U.S.C. § 522(f)(2)(A), the lien impairs the exemption to the extent that the sum of three amounts exceeds the value the debtor would have in the property absent any lien. The three amounts are the lien itself, all other liens on the property, and the amount of the exemption claimed.

### The Computation Uses the Schedules

Applying the formula to the figures above, the sum of the three amounts is $218,000: the $48,000 judgment lien, the $120,000 mortgage, and the $50,000 exemption. The value of the Property absent liens is $150,000. The impairment is therefore $68,000, which exceeds the $48,000 judicial lien.

Because the impairment is greater than the lien, the lien impairs the exemption in its entirety. The Court should avoid the lien in the full amount of $48,000, plus any interest and costs that the Court determines are secured by the lien.

## The Motion Is Timely and Properly Brought

No other lien on the Property is a judicial lien that Debtor seeks to avoid by this motion, and the first mortgage is not affected by the relief requested. The relief is requested under 11 U.S.C. § 522(f)(1)(A), which permits avoidance of a judicial lien on an exempt property without regard to the amount of the lien.

### Procedure

A motion to avoid a lien under § 522(f) is a contested matter, governed by Fed. R. Bankr. P. 9014, and is commenced by motion under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 4003(d). Debtor has served the motion on Creditor and the other parties identified in the certificate of service below, and has complied with Local Rule 9013-1(B) by including the notice of opportunity for hearing above.
