# INTRODUCTION AND SUMMARY OF ARGUMENT

Through the Complaint to Avoid and Recover Transfers Pursuant to 11 U.S.C. §§ 502, 548, and 550 [Adv. Dkt. No. 1] (the "Complaint"), Darcy D. Williamson (the "Trustee" or "Plaintiff"), not individually, but solely as Chapter 7 Trustee of Social Networking Technology, Inc. (the "Debtor"), seeks to avoid and recover $367,411.00 in transfers (the "Alleged Transfers") allegedly made to defendant Comcast Cable Communications Management, LLC d/b/a Effectv, erroneously sued as Comcast Spotlight, LP ("Effectv") during the two year period preceding the Petition Date (as defined below) as constructively fraudulent transfers.

Count I of the Complaint, which attempts to assert a constructive fraudulent transfer claim, should be dismissed because the Trustee's allegations are threadbare and simply recite the elements of the cause of action contained in section [548(a)(1)(B)](toa:s548) of the Bankruptcy Code without providing the requisite factual detail to establish a plausible cause of action. The fraudulent transfer claim that the Trustee asserts against Effectv relies on the Trustee's legal conclusion that the Debtor did not receive reasonably equivalent value for the advertising air time Effectv provided to the Debtor in exchange for the Alleged Transfers. The Trustee fails to plead any facts in support of her legal conclusion that reasonably equivalent value was not provided. The Trustee also fails to plead any facts supporting the legal conclusion that the Debtor was insolvent on the date of the Alleged Transfers.

Because the constructive fraudulent transfer claim asserted as Count I of the Complaint should be dismissed, the related claims to (a) recover the value of the Alleged Transfers under section 550 of the Bankruptcy Code asserted as Count II of the Complaint and (b) disallow claims under section 502(d) of the Bankruptcy Code asserted as Count III of the Complaint should also be dismissed.

# NATURE AND STAGE OF PROCEEDINGS

On February 12, 2018 (the "Petition Date"), the Debtor filed a voluntary petition for relief under chapter 7 of title 11 of the United States Code (the "Bankruptcy Code"), in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Kansas (the "Court"), commencing bankruptcy case number 18-10177 (the "Bankruptcy Case"). [Bankr. Dkt. No. 1]. Thereafter, the Trustee was appointed as chapter 7 trustee of the Debtor's bankruptcy estate. On February 7, 2020, just prior to the expiration of the two year limitations period for commencing avoidance actions, the Trustee commenced this adversary proceeding by filing the Complaint.

# FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

The Complaint is wholly deficient of factual allegations supporting the claims for relief asserted by the Trustee. The Complaint alleges that the Debtor was a technology company that provided digital content integrated with advertising. Complaint ¶ 4, at p.2. The Complaint also alleges that Effectv provided advertising services to the Debtor. Complaint ¶ 14, at p. 3. Finally, the Complaint alleges that the Debtor's insolvency on the date of the Alleged Transfers is somehow evidenced by the Debtor's liabilities exceeding its assets as shown by the Debtor's schedules. Complaint ¶ 17, at p. 3. The remaining allegations in the Complaint are merely recitations of the legal elements of the asserted claims and requests for relief. Complaint ¶¶ 8-25 at pp. 2-4.

# QUESTION PRESENTED

Whether the Complaint pleads facts sufficient to state a plausible claim under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B) that the Debtor received less than reasonably equivalent value for the Alleged Transfers and was insolvent when they were made.

# LEGAL STANDARD

To survive a motion to dismiss under [Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6)](toa:rule12b6), "a complaint must contain sufficient factual matter, accepted as true, to 'state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face.'" [*Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009)](toa:iqbal) (quoting [*Bell Atl. Corp. v. Twombly*, 550 U.S. 544, 570 (2007)](toa:twombly)). A claim is plausible when the plaintiff "pleads factual content that allows the court to draw the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct alleged." [*Iqbal*, 556 U.S. at 678](toa:iqbal). The Court must accept only well-pleaded facts as true. *See* [*Robbins v. Oklahoma*, 519 F.3d 1242, 1247 (10th Cir. 2008)](toa:robbins). "Threadbare recitals of the elements of a cause of action, supported by mere conclusory statements, do not suffice." [*Iqbal*, 556 U.S. at 678](toa:iqbal).

The court is "not bound to accept as true a legal conclusion couched as a factual allegation." [*Twombly*, 550 U.S. at 555](toa:twombly) (quotation omitted). The court should dismiss claims that are based on "no more than 'labels and conclusions' or 'a formulaic recitation of the elements of a cause of action.'" *See* [*Robbins*, 519 F.3d at 1247](toa:robbins) (quoting [*Twombly*, 550 U.S. at 555](toa:twombly)); [*Khalick v. United Air Lines*, 671 F.3d 1188, 1193 (10th Cir. 2012)](toa:khalick) ("conclusory and formulaic recitations of the elements are insufficient to survive a motion to dismiss").

# ARGUMENT

## Count I Should Be Dismissed Because the Complaint Fails to State a Plausible Fraudulent Transfer Claim Against Effectv.

Section [548(a)(1)(B)](toa:s548) of the Bankruptcy Code permits a trustee to avoid transfers as constructively fraudulent upon proving: "(1) that the debtor had an interest in property; (2) that a transfer of that interest occurred within [two years] of the filing of the bankruptcy petition; (3) that the debtor was insolvent or was rendered insolvent as a result of the transfer; and (4) that the debtor received less than a reasonably equivalent value in exchange for the transfer." [*Zubrod v. Keffer (In re Keffer)*, 307 B.R. 731, 733 (10th Cir. BAP 2004)](toa:keffer) (citing BFP Resolution Trust Corp., 511 U.S. 531, 535 (1994)). The party seeking to avoid a transfer as constructively fraudulent under section 548(a)(1)(B) "has the burden of proving the transfer was made for less than a reasonably equivalent value." [*Bison Park Dev., LLC v. North American Savings Bank (In re Bison Park Dev., LLC)*, 2009 Bankr. LEXIS 1257, at \*10 (Bankr. D. Kan. May 7, 2009)](toa:bison).

As explained in detail below, the Complaint fails to allege sufficient facts to sustain a claim to avoid the Alleged Transfers as constructively fraudulent and, instead, merely recites the elements to state a claim under section 548(a)(1)(B) of the Bankruptcy Code without any factual support. Accordingly, the Trustee clearly fails to meet the applicable pleading standard and the Complaint should be dismissed in its entirety.

### The Complaint Fails to Plead Any Facts in Support of the Legal Conclusion That the Debtor Received Less Than Reasonably Equivalent Value in Exchange for the Alleged Transfers.

Courts have consistently dismissed constructive fraudulent transfer claims where the complaint fails to contain more than a bare recital of the statutory language that the debtor received less than reasonably equivalent value. [*Goldstein v. BRT, Inc. (In re Universal Marketing)*, 460 B.R. 828, 837-839 (Bankr. E.D. Pa. 2011)](toa:universal) (dismissing constructive fraudulent transfer claim where allegations merely parroted the relevant statutory provision and failed to "provide a factual foundation that would permit a court to conclude that the Complaint states a plausible claim for relief"); [*State ex rel. Woodridge Local School v. Facebook, Inc.*, 5:19-cv-1046, 2000 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 2202 \*11 (N.D. Ohio Jan. 7, 2020)](toa:woodridge) (dismissing fraudulent transfer claim because complaint failed to allege "at least some facts showing that reasonably equivalent value was not provided"); [*Trustees of the Eighth Dist. Elec. Pension Fund v. Wasatch Front Elec. & Constr.*, 2:09-cv-632, 2011 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 94242 (D. Utah Aug. 23, 2011)](toa:wasatch) (refusing to permit amendment of complaint to include fraudulent transfer claim because plaintiff failed "to allege any facts to support the conclusory allegations reciting the bare elements of the claim" in the proposed amended complaint); [*Gonzales v. Liberman (In re Brutsche)*, 11-01189, 2013 Bankr. LEXIS 585 \*22-23 (Bankr. D.N.M. Feb. 11, 2013)](toa:brutsche) (dismissing fraudulent transfer claim for failure to plead sufficient facts).

Here, the Complaint's only allegation regarding lack of reasonably equivalent value is insufficient to state a claim. The allegation simply parrots the statute by stating that the "value of Defendant's services is substantially less than the value of the [Alleged] Transfers, meaning [the Debtor] did not receive reasonably equivalent value in exchange for the [Alleged] Transfers." Complaint ¶ 14, at p. 3.

The Trustee's threadbare, conclusory allegation that the value of the advertising air time provided by Effectv to the Debtor is less than the value of the Alleged Transfers is a legal conclusion and falls well short of sufficiently pleading facts that plausibly allege the Debtor received less than reasonably equivalent value for the Alleged Transfers. The Complaint wholly fails to explain how (if at all) the value of Effectv's services was less than the value of the Alleged Transfers, which reflect the parties' agreed upon price for Effectv's services.

In fact, the Trustee's allegations support that there was consideration—because it acknowledges the Alleged Transfers were made in consideration of advertising services provided by Effectv.

As a result, the Complaint fails to state or allege sufficient factual allegations to support a constructively fraudulent transfer claim and Count I of the Complaint should be dismissed with prejudice.
