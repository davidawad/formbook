# Summary of the Argument

The Bankruptcy Court made the findings the statute requires. The valuation evidence was sufficient, and Appellant's objection to the price was heard and rejected on that evidence. The proceeds provision protects Appellant's interest to the extent the Code allows.

# Argument

## THE VALUATION EVIDENCE SUPPORTS THE SALE FINDING

### The Bankruptcy Court weighed the broker opinion

The Bankruptcy Court found the broker credible after hearing cross-examination. Credibility determinations are reviewed for clear error. [11 U.S.C. § 363(b)](toa:s363) requires a sound business reason for a sale, and the court found one: the equipment was deteriorating and carried storage costs that exceeded its expected net return.

### The sale was noticed and contested

Appellant received notice of the sale motion and objected. The objection was heard. The court's ruling reflects the evidence and is not an abuse of discretion.

## THE PROCEEDS PROVISION PROTECTS APPELLANT TO THE EXTENT THE CODE ALLOWS

Appellant asks the Panel to direct that her interest attach to the proceeds with the same validity and priority it had in the equipment. The order already says that. Any broader remedy would ask the court to override the priority scheme of the Code, which it may not do. See [Law v. Siegel](toa:siegel).

# Conclusion

The Trustee asks the Panel to affirm the sale order.
