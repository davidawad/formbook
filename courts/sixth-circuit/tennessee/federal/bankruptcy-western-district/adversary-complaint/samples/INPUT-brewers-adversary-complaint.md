# THE AGREEMENT

On November 9, 2023, the Debtor – through Brewer – executed a “Revolving Loan Agreement” (“Agreement”) with Idea 247, identifying Idea 247 as the “Lender” and Debtor as the “Borrower.” A copy of the Agreement is attached hereto as Exhibit A.

According to the Agreement, a revolving line of credit was established in a principal amount of up to $211,000, available in advances to Debtor.

Each advance is subject to a mandatory “drawdown fee” of 2.99%, which fee is automatically added to the loan balance and itself accrues interest.

The Agreement further provides that interest accrues daily at a rate of 0.0699%, equating to 25.5% annually.

In the event of a default, the Agreement increases the daily rate to 0.0836%, which is equivalent to approximately 30.5% percent per annum.

The Agreement requires repayment on a fixed schedule of equal weekly installments amortized over a 12-month term.

Pursuant to the Agreement, Debtor is obligated to repay the full principal, interest, and fees regardless of the success or failure of any business operations.

The Agreement also provides that Idea 247 requires Debtor to grant a blanket security interest in all of its assets, as well as personal guarantees from Brewer.

The Agreement sets a “Maturity Date,” defined as twelve months after the most recent advance, on which any unpaid principal, interest, and fees become immediately due by Debtor.

Idea 247 is also authorized under the Agreement to withdraw payments directly from Debtor’s bank accounts by electronic debit.

Within one week of the Agreement being entered, Idea 247 filed a UCC-1 lien against Debtor for the full amount of the loan, $211,000.00, on November 14, 2023.

The Agreement provides a choice of law provision selecting the laws of the Commonwealth of Virginia.

# FIRST CLAIM FOR RELIEF

## (Avoidance of Constructively Fraudulent Obligation – 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B))

Plaintiff repeats and re-alleges the allegations contained in all foregoing paragraphs as if more fully set forth at length herein.

Illegal, unenforceable debts cannot provide reasonably equivalent value. See *Tabas v. Lehman (In re Capitol Invs., Inc.)*, 473 B.R. 838, 846 (Bankr. S.D. Fla. 2012) (“a transaction that is illegal under the laws of the State [ ] will not support ‘value’ for purposes of sections 548 or 550.”).

The incurrence of the Agreement did not provide reasonably equivalent value to the Debtor.

The Agreement states that it charges the Debtor an approximate annual rate of interest, before any advances occurred, of about 25.5%. [^3] [Exhibit A at Section 2.2].

However, as set forth below, the interest rate is actually more than 38%.

In the event of a default, the Agreement charged Debtor a higher annual rate of interest of about 30.5% interest, exclusive of fees and interest accrued in connection with advances. [^4] [Exhibit A at Section 2.2].

In addition to these rates, each advance from Idea 247 to Debtor was subject to a mandatory 2.99% drawdown fee added to the loan balance, which also accrued interest.

The incurrence of the Agreement should be avoided under as it did not provide reasonably equivalent value to the Debtor.

This Court should declare that the incurrence of the Agreement should be avoided under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B) as the Agreement did not provide reasonably equivalent value to the Debtor, is illegal, unenforceable, and void under Tennessee law.

The Tennessee Department of Financial Institutions sets a maximum effective formula interest rate of 11.50% per annum, with higher rates of up to 24% permitted for certain commercial loans.

Under Tennessee Code §47-14-117, if a loan contract on its face requires payment of usurious interest or excessive fees, the contract is unenforceable.

Specifically, Section 47-14-117(c)(1) provides that, where a court finds that “the lender or creditor has been guilty of unconscionable conduct in a transaction by taking interest, loan charges, commitment fees, or brokerage commissions in excess of the limitations fixed by statute, that lender or creditor shall not be entitled to recover any interest, loan charges, commitment fees, or brokerage commissions with respect to that transaction, and shall be required to refund to the borrower or debtor any loan charges, commitment fees, or brokerage commissions, and twice the amount of any interest collected with respect to that transaction, and the borrower shall be entitled to recover reasonable attorneys' fees from the lender.”

Here, the interest rate charged is higher than the cap on interest under Tennessee law and accordingly, the Agreement is void and Plaintiff is entitled to relief from the Defendants.

# SECOND CLAIM FOR RELIEF

## (Avoidance of Constructively Fraudulent Transfer – 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B))

Plaintiff repeats and re-alleges the allegations set forth above inclusive, as if more fully set forth at length herein.

An unenforceable agreement cannot serve as an antecedent debt. The Bankruptcy Code defines a “debt” as “liability on a claim.” 11 U.S.C. § 101(12). No liability can be established on an unenforceable claim; therefore, the Agreement cannot serve as an “antecedent debt” for the payment made by the Debtor.

Additionally, with the Agreement being avoided under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B), the Debtor’s payments on the avoided Agreement were made without receiving reasonably equivalent value. See e.g. *Redmond v. Spiritbank (In re Brooke Corp.)*, 541 B.R. 492, 507 (Bankr. Kan. 2015) (“If the court avoids an obligation under section 548, transfers made by the debtor on account of that obligation are not made for reasonably equivalent value, and may be set aside as actually or constructively fraudulent.”) (citation and quotation omitted). See also *Silverman v. Paul’s Landmark, Inc. (In re Nirvana Rest., Inc.)*, 337 B.R. 495, 502 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2006); *Tabas v. Lehman (In re Capitol Invs., Inc.)*, 473 B.R. 838, 846 (Bankr. S.D. Fla. 2012) (“a transaction that is illegal under the laws of the State [ ] will not support ‘value’ for purposes of sections 548 or 550.”).

The Transfers were made within two (2) years of the June 9, 2025 Petition Date.

The Transfers were a transfer of an interest of the Debtor in property.

For the reasons alleged herein, the Debtor was insolvent on the date that the Transfers were made or became insolvent as a result of such Transfers.

At the time of the Transfers, the Debtor was engaged in business or a transaction, or was about to engage in business or transactions, for which any property remaining with the Debtor was an unreasonably small capital.

At the time of the Transfers, the Debtor intended to incur, or believed that the Debtor would incur, debts that were beyond the Debtor’s ability to pay as such debts matured.

Moreover, receiving $211,000 and repaying $315,640.20 over the course of 471 days is not reasonably equivalent.

The payments totaling $315,640.20 made by the Debtor on account of the Agreement are avoidable as a constructively fraudulent conveyances pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(1)(B), and the Plaintiff is entitled to avoid the Transfers for the benefit of the estate.
