# INTRODUCTION

This is a proposed class action on behalf of California and New York classes of consumers seeking redress for Defendant’s deceptive practices associated with the advertising, labeling, and sale of its Minute Maid Fruit Punch (“Products”) which claim contain “100% Natural Flavors.”

Unfortunately, the claim that the Product consists of “100% Natural Flavors” is false and misleading, because the Product contains a synthetic, non-natural flavoring ingredient in the form of industrially manufactured citric acid which, as Defendant’s own label concedes, was added to “provide[] tartness,” a flavor in the Product.

Despite prominently and boldly representing on the front of the package that the Products contain “100% Natural Flavors,” the inclusion of manufactured citric acid, a synthetic ingredient whose function is to impart flavor (i.e., tartness), belies this representation rendering it false, misleading, and in violation of the law.

Defendant knows that consumers are willing to pay more for products promising “100% Natural Flavors” because they perceive such products to be more natural and healthier than products containing synthetic or artificial ingredients. Indeed, Defendant purposefully advertised its Product with the intention that consumers notice and rely on the representation that the Product consists of only (i.e., 100%) “Natural Flavors.”

Plaintiffs allege that Defendant’s conduct is in breach of warranty, violates California’s Business and Professions Code § 17200, et. seq., California’s Business & Professions Code § 17500, et. seq., California Civil Code § 1750, et seq., and N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law §§ 349 and 350 et seq.

# GENERAL ALLEGATIONS

### Citric Acid Is a Flavoring Ingredient in the Product

Acidulants are chemical compounds that impart a sour or acidic flavor to food products while also enhancing the perception of sweetness. Citric acid is the single most widely used acidulant in food and beverage formulation. In beverages, such as the Product at issue here, citric acid adds a clean, sharp tartness.

By their own admission, citric acid functions as a flavor in Defendant’s Product, (i.e., “Provides Tartness”), which is a taste and a characterizing sensory attribute of the Product.

While acidulants such as citric acid may also have a preservative effect, for that purpose to have been intended, Defendant, by law, would have had to disclose it. 21 C.F.R. § 101.22(j) (“A food to which a chemical preservative(s) is added shall … bear a label declaration stating both the common or usual name of the ingredient(s) and a separate description of its function, e.g., ‘preservative’, ‘to retard spoilage’, ‘a mold inhibitor’, ‘to help protect flavor’ or ‘to promote color retention’). They did not.

Additionally, this Product is pasteurized. It is also considered a high acid beverage, (pH 2.86) wherein microbial stability can be achieved by pasteurization, thereby obviating or minimizing the need for supplemental preservation.

At bottom, by their own admission (i.e., “provides tartness”) combined with the fact this is a highly acidic pasteurized beverage unquestionably shows that the citric acid was intended as, and functions as, a flavoring in the Product.

# FIRST CAUSE OF ACTION

## Breach of Express Warranty

Plaintiffs incorporate each and every allegation contained in the paragraphs above as if restated herein.

Plaintiffs’ express warranty claims are based on violations of N.Y. CLS UCC § 2-313 and § 2-607 and Cal. Com. Code § 2313. Defendants were afforded reasonable notice of this claim in advance of the filing of this complaint.

Defendant made express warranties to Plaintiffs and members of the Class that the Products contained “100% Natural Flavors.”

The express warranties made to Plaintiffs and members of the Class appear on every Product label. This warranty regarding the nature of the Product marketed by Defendant specifically relates to the goods being purchased and became the basis of the bargain.

Plaintiffs and the Class purchased the Products in the belief that they conformed to the express warranties that were made on the Products’ labels.

Defendant breached the express warranties made to Plaintiffs and members of the Class by failing to supply goods that conformed to the warranties it made. As a result, Plaintiffs and members of the Class suffered injury and deserve to be compensated for the damages they suffered.

Plaintiffs and the members of the Class paid money for the Products. However, Plaintiffs and the members of the Class did not obtain the full value of the advertised Products. If Plaintiffs and other members of the Class had known of the true nature of the Products, they would not have purchased them or paid less for them. Accordingly, Plaintiffs and members of the Class have suffered injury in fact and lost money or property as a result of Defendant’s wrongful conduct.

Plaintiffs and the Class are therefore entitled to recover damages, punitive damages, equitable relief such as restitution and disgorgement of profits, and declaratory and injunctive relief.
