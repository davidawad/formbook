This Motion is based upon this Notice, the accompanying Memorandum of Points and Authorities, the accompanying Request for Judicial Notice and Declaration of William A. Delgado, the complete record in this action, and such other matters and arguments as may come before this Court, including those raised in any reply memorandum and oral argument.

[[MEMO]]

# I. INTRODUCTION

Plaintiff Daniel C. Wine’s Complaint depends on a threshold premise that cannot support liability against Morning Law Group, P.C. (“MLG”). According to the Complaint, the alleged debt-resolution relationship between Plaintiff and MLG began in October 2021. Nevertheless, MLG did not even exist until August 17, 2022. If Plaintiff entered into a relationship in October 2021 with some entity, it wasn’t with MLG, and the Complaint fails to identify that entity or a viable theory for attributing that entity’s conduct or liabilities to MLG.

The only apparent connection is a bankruptcy sale in August 2023 through which MLG acquired certain assets of a firm called The Litigation Practice Group, P.C. (“LPG”). But even that connection does not save the pleading. The bankruptcy court approved the sale of LPG’s assets free and clear of claims, expressly including successor-liability claims, and enjoined claims against MLG arising from LPG’s pre-closing conduct.

The Complaint therefore fails at the threshold. If Plaintiff’s theory depends on the LPG bankruptcy sale, the bankruptcy court’s sale order bars it. If Plaintiff relies on some other connection to MLG, the Complaint does not plead one. Those threshold defects, together with the claim-specific pleading failures addressed below, require dismissal under Rule 12(b)(6). At minimum, Plaintiff should be required to provide a more definite statement under Rule 12(e).

# II. RELEVANT FACTS

## A. The Complaint’s Allegations

The Complaint’s chronology is unclear from the outset. It alleges that Plaintiff faced financial hardship “[i]n March 2024,” but also alleges that he entered into “a contract with Defendant for legal and debt resolution services” “[i]n or around October 2021.” Compl. ¶¶ 1, 7.

# III. ARGUMENT

## D. In the Alternative, the Court Should Grant MLG’s Motion for a More Definite Statement Under Rule 12(e).

If any claim survives dismissal, Rule 12(e) relief is warranted. The Complaint does not identify the entity that allegedly contracted with Plaintiff in October 2021, explain how that entity’s conduct could be attributed to MLG, or allege when MLG supposedly became involved. As a result, MLG cannot reasonably determine what conduct it must answer. See Comm. for Immigrant Rts., 644 F. Supp. 2d at 1209 (ordering a more definite statement where the complaint was “vague and difficult to reasonably decipher” and the defendant needed “a fair opportunity to frame a responsive pleading”).

If the Court does not dismiss the Complaint, MLG requests an order requiring Plaintiff to file a more definite statement identifying:

1. Which entity Plaintiff contracted with in October 2021.
2. What legal theory (successor liability, agency, alter ego, or other) connects that entity’s conduct to MLG.
3. When Plaintiff’s relationship with MLG specifically began.
4. Which contract governs the relationship with MLG and its specific terms.
5. For each alleged misrepresentation: who made the statement, when, through what medium, and on behalf of which entity.
6. Which payments were made to MLG versus any prior entity, and under which agreement.
7. Which specific post-sale MLG conduct is alleged to violate each statute.

Without those details, MLG cannot determine whether Plaintiff’s claims are directed at MLG, LPG, another predecessor entity, or some combination of entities. A more definite statement is therefore necessary if any claim survives dismissal.

# IV. CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, MLG respectfully requests that the Court dismiss the Complaint in its entirety. In the alternative, MLG requests that the Court dismiss Counts V and VI, with prejudice as to the October 2021 enrollment-related statements, for failure to satisfy Rule 9(b), and dismiss the remaining counts without prejudice to the filing of an amended complaint that pleads MLG-specific conduct and a cognizable legal theory for attributing pre-incorporation conduct to MLG. In the further alternative, MLG requests that the Court order Plaintiff to file a more definite statement under Rule 12(e) addressing the deficiencies identified above.
