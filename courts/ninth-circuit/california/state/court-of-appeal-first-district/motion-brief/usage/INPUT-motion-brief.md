# MOTION FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

Pursuant to Rule 8.252 of the California Rules of Court, Appellant People of the State of California (“People”) hereby move the Court to take judicial notice of certain official acts, specifically the December 2021 declaration of a state of emergency in the Tenderloin District, and factual matters not subject to reasonable dispute, namely that the nuisance in the Tenderloin continues to persist unabated:

1\. The Proclamation by the Mayor Declaring the Existence of a Local Emergency: Drug Overdoses in the Tenderloin by London N. Breed, Mayor of the City and County of San Francisco, dated December 17, 2021, a true and correct copy of which is attached as Exhibit A.

2\. Motion Concurring in Proclamation of Local Emergency - Drug Overdoses in the Tenderloin, dated December 23, 2021, a true and correct copy of which is attached as Exhibit B.

3\. That the nuisance in the Tenderloin found by the trial court is still ongoing.

# DISCUSSION

The recent emergency declarations and the continuing crisis in the Tenderloin are relevant to this appeal, in that they provide further proof of the urgent need for this Court to correct the error of the Superior Court, and to reverse and remand with instructions to the Superior Court to issue the requested preliminary injunctions against Respondents, as more fully described in the People’s merits briefs.

The official declaration of a state of emergency and concurrence by a county board of supervisors is a proper subject of judicial notice. (Evid. Code § 452(b), (c); *e.g.*, *Bullock v. Superior Court of Contra Costa County* (2020) 51 Cal.App.5th 134, 141 fns. 4 & 5 [taking judicial notice of declaration of COVID-19 emergency by county board of supervisors]; *see also*, *e.g.*, *Inns by the Sea v. California Mutual Insurance Company* (2021) 71 Cal.App.5th 688, 286 Cal.Rptr.3d 576, 580 fn. 2 [taking judicial notice of county orders during COVID-19 emergency, and noting subdivision (c) provides for judicial notice of actions of counties].)

The continued existence of the nuisance conditions and drug crisis in the Tenderloin as found by the trial court is a proper subject of judicial notice per Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (g), which provides for judicial notice of “facts and propositions that are of such common knowledge within the territorial jurisdiction of the court that they cannot reasonably be the subject of dispute.” (Evid. Code § 452(g); *e.g.*, *Porter v. Fiske* (1946) 74 Cal.App.2d 332, 338 [upholding injunction against violator of pricing controls, and taking judicial notice of “emergency occasioned by the scarcity of housing accommodations in areas where war supplies were being produced” during World War II].) The existing nuisance conditions in the Tenderloin are well known within the territorial jurisdiction of the Superior Court and this Court. Respondents and Amici have not disputed them, and no one could reasonably dispute that they continue to exist.
