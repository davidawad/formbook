# MEMORANDUM IN SUPPORT OF MOTION

## I. NATURE OF APPEAL

This interlocutory appeal centers around and relates to the constitutional authority of the counties of Hawai´i pursuant to Article VIII, Section 3 of the Hawai’i State Constitution, to establish real property tax classifications, and set real property tax rates. The issues remaining for resolution by this court are raised in Points of Error Nos. 1, 2, 3, and 4 of Appellants’ Amended Opening Appellate Brief, filed December 25, 2018, as follows:

> 1\) The Second Circuit Court's assertion of subject matter jurisdiction over the claims raised in the underlying lawsuit was/is improper, ignores the special and exclusive subject matter jurisdiction of the Tax Appeal Court, and undermines Maui County’s constitutional authority to adjudicate real property tax appeals. The Tax Appeal Court has exclusive subject matter jurisdiction over the real property tax matters and disputes raised in the underlying lawsuit as a matter of law.

> 2\) The Second Circuit Court's declaratory ruling and voiding as "illegal" duly enacted property tax code provisions establishing the County's "Timeshare" general real property tax classification was/is contrary to the constitutional doctrine of the Separation of Powers and interferes with the County of Maui's constitutional "functions, powers, and duties" to identify, define, classify, and assess real property for purposes of taxation, land use policy, and economic policy.

> 3\) The Second Circuit Court's conclusion that the general classification of "Timeshare" for real property tax purposes may only be established upon the sole consideration of "actual use" of property was/is wrong and undermines the entire structure of general classification and application of Maui County's real property tax code.

> 4\) The Second Circuit Court’s ruling that Plaintiffs are entitled to as much as $34,000,000.00 of tax refunds in the aggregate for combined assessment years dating back 12-14 years to 2006 despite Plaintiffs as taxpayers having not appealed their assessments within the required statutory period for each assessment year was/is wrong, is an abuse of discretion, threatens imminent and irreparable harm to Maui County’s bond credit rating, and imperils the fiscal security of Maui County and its residents.

See JEFS Dkt. 212, PDF pp. 28-29 (CAAP-18-0000578).

As discussed shortly below, Points of Error Nos. 5, 6, and 7 of this appeal were settled through the Appellate Mediation Program. See JEFS Dkt. 128, 142, 152, and 154.

## II. PROCEDURAL HISTORY AND PARTIAL SETTLEMENT OF APPEAL

This interlocutory appeal was filed on July 20, 2018. See CAAP-18-0000578 JEFS, Dkt. 1. Appellants’ Amended Opening Brief was filed on December 25, 2018. See JEFS Dkt. 212 (CAAP-18-0000578). A Brief of Amicus Curiae was filed by the Tax Foundation of Hawai´i on January 16, 2019. See JEFS Dkt. 221 (CAAP-18-0000578). Appellees’ and Appellants’ respective responses to the Brief of Amicus Curiae were filed on February 15, 2019. See JEFS Dkt. 227 and 229 (CAAP-18-0000578). This court’s order accepting transfer of the case was entered on February 20, 2019. See JEFS Dkt. 78 (SCAP-18-0000578). Appellees’ Answering Brief was filed on January 16, 2019. See JEFS Dkt. 103 (SCAP-18-0000578).

On May 3, 2019, Appellants and Appellees requested this appeal be entered into the Appellate Mediation Program. See JEFS Dkt. 118 (SCAP-18-0000578). On May 22, 2019, this appeal was stayed by the Appellate Clerk pending mediation in the Appellate Mediation Program. See JEFS Dkt. 122. On November 13, 2019, the Appellate Mediation Program Case Report was filed, reporting that Appellants and Appellees partially settled the case, narrowed the issues, but did not resolve the entire appeal. See JEFS Dkt. 128. On November 13, 2019, the case was returned to the appellate docket. See JEFS Dkt. 132. On November 27, 2019, the case was placed on the Ready Calendar. See JEFS Dkt. 136. The Notice Placed on Ready Calendar noted that no Reply Brief had been filed.[^2] Id.

On December 23, 2019, the Order by McKenna, J. was entered, directing the parties to file a status report, explaining which issues on appeal were settled, and which issues remain to be resolved by this court. See JEFS Dkt. 140. The Court Ordered Status Report was filed on January 2, 2020, identifying Points of Error Nos. 5, 6, and 7 as having been partially resolved with the assistance of the Appellate Mediation Program. See JEFS Dkt. 142. The Court Ordered Status Report identifies Points of Error Nos. 1, 2, 3 and 4 as remaining to be resolved by this court. Id.

The Settlement and Release Agreement disposing of Points of Error Nos. 5, 6, and 7, was filed pursuant to further order by this court. See JEFS Dkt. 149 (Order) and 152 (Settlement and Release Agreement). Appellants’ and Appellees’ counsel also executed a Stipulation and Order for Partial Dismissal of Appeal With Prejudice and for Remand, which was filed on January 3, 2020. See JEFS Dkt. 154.

## III. RELIEF REQUESTED BY THIS MOTION

By this motion and as reflected in the Stipulation and Order for Partial Dismissal of Appeal With Prejudice and for Remand [JEFS Dkt. 154], the parties have agreed to partial dismissal, remand, and vacating of certain orders and partial final judgment. Therefore, Appellants respectfully request this court enter a partial dismissal of the appeal reflected in Points of Error Nos. 5, 6, and 7 raised in the Amended Opening Appellate Brief, and as related to the second circuit court’s following findings, conclusions, orders, and partial final judgment:

a) Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law; Order Granting Plaintiffs’ Motions for Partial Summary Judgment on Counts V, VI, VII and VIII of the Second Amended Complaint Filed August 12, 2016, Filed on December 1, 2016; and Denying Defendants County of Maui and the Maui County Council’s Motions for Partial Summary Judgment as to Count V of the Plaintiffs’ Second Amended Complaint Filed 2016-08-12, Filed on December 12, 2016, filed on August 9, 2017;

b) Order Granting in Part and Deferring in Part Plaintiffs’ Motion re Damages Related to Count VIII (42 U.S.C. § 1983) of the Second Amended Complaint Filed August 12, 2016, Filed on September 22, 2017, filed on January 23, 2018;

c) Order Granting (1) Plaintiffs’ Motion for Further Hearing on Plaintiffs’ Request for Fees and Costs Relating to Count VIII of the Second Amended Complaint Filed August 12, 2016, Filed March 6, 2018; and (2) Plaintiffs’ Motion re Damages Related to Count VIII (42 U.S.C. § 1983) of the Second Amended Complaint Filed August 12, 2016, Filed September 22, 2017, Regarding Attorneys’ Fees and Costs, filed on May 30, 2018; and

d) Final Judgment on Counts V, VI, VII, and VIII of the Second Amended Complaint Filed on August 12, 2016, filed July 2, 2018.

The parties’ agreement for the vacating of these finding, conclusions, orders, and partial final judgment by the circuit court is provided for in ¶ 2.2.3 of the Settlement and Release Agreement:

> 2.2.3. Vacate Certain Second Circuit Court Orders. Upon remand to the Second Circuit Court as contemplated in section 2.2.2 of this Agreement, the Parties shall execute and submit for entry by the Second Circuit Court the Stipulation and Order Partially Lifting Stay, attached here as Exhibit A, in order to vacate (a) the Amended Assessment Order; (b) the Damages Order; (c) the Fees and Costs Order; and (d) the Final Judgment. The Parties anticipate the Second Circuit Court’s agreement to vacate these orders and the Final Judgment, but its failure to do so does not effect the enforceability of this Agreement, including but not limited to the monetary consideration due pursuant to paragraph 2.1 of this Agreement. The Parties’ obligation to execute and file the Stipulation and Order Partially Lifting Stay is also not effected by the Second Circuit Court’s failure to vacate any of the above-referenced orders or Final Judgment.

See JEFS Dkt. 152 (Settlement and Release Agreement); see also Exhibit 1 attached.

As reflected in ¶ 2.2.3 of the Settlement and Release Agreement [JEFS Dkt. 152] and as agreed to by the parties, it would therefore seem not inappropriate for this court to direct the circuit court on remand to vacate the above identified findings, conclusions, orders, and partial final judgment.[^3] Appellants further request on this basis that if this court deems it appropriate, the remand order issued by this court also direct the circuit court to vacate those findings, conclusions, order, and partial final judgment which are identified above.

## IV. CONCLUSION

The agreement between Defendants/Appellants and Plaintiffs/ Appellees for partial disposition of this appeal pursuant to the terms of the Settlement and Release Agreement are not ambiguous. Therefore, Appellants/Defendants respectfully request this court issue an order which 1) dismisses the part(s) of the appeal as provided for in the settlement and as specified herein, and 2) remands for further disposition by the circuit court of the findings, conclusions, orders, and partial final judgment entered below.

Finally, to the extent this court may deem it appropriate, Defendants/ Appellants request on remand the circuit court be directed to vacate the findings, conclusions, orders, and partial final judgment entered below.
