# NATURE OF THIS ACTION

This adversary proceeding is brought by the Trustee to avoid the fraudulent transfers by which value was stripped from Green Oregon Enterprises, Inc. (the “Debtor” or “GOE”) and conveyed to insider affiliates and successor entities, and to recover the transferred property itself — most importantly, the Debtor’s master leasehold interest in its retail premises — or, where the Court so orders, its value. The Defendants engaged in a coordinated scheme to transfer substantially all of GOE’s valuable assets, contracts, and business operations to newly formed entities controlled by the same individuals, leaving GOE an empty shell unable to pay its creditors.

Before the Debtor’s bankruptcy filing on July 12, 2024 (the “Petition Date”), the Debtor served as the management company for Green Mart, LLC (“Green Mart”), a retail cannabis dispensary generating $200,000 to $400,000 per month in gross revenue. GOE was the original lessee of the retail premises at 12745 SW Walker Road, Beaverton, Oregon, where Green Mart operated (and Defendant Arbon Enterprises currently operates) the retail store, and received management fees of approximately $60,000 per month. GOE, Green Mart, Herban Renewal, LLC (“Herban”), and Platinum Parachute Holdings, LLC (“PPH”) operated under the common control of Douglas Lanphere (“Lanphere”) and are referred to collectively herein as the “Original Entities.” Lanphere, acting in concert with Robert Lanphere, Jr. (“Bob Lanphere”), London Stricklin (“Stricklin”), Jeffrey Arbon (“Mr. Arbon”), and Georgene Arbon (“Mrs. Arbon”) (collectively, Lanphere, Bob Lanphere, Stricklin, Mr. Arbon, and Mrs. Arbon, the “Individual Defendants”), orchestrated the transfer of GOE’s assets and revenue streams to Arbon Enterprises, Inc., NW High Octane, LLC, Legacy Cannabis Distribution, LLC, and Abide Operations, LLC (collectively, the “Successor Entities” or “Transferee Defendants”).

The scheme involved the transfer of GOE’s leasehold interest in the Walker Road premises (transferred to Abide Operations, LLC for grossly inadequate and circular consideration), the management fee revenue stream (approximately $60,000 per month), and substantially all other assets (collectively, the “Fraudulently Transferred Assets”). GOE’s leasehold interest in the Walker Road premises, together with all rights of extension, renewal, replacement, and holdover appurtenant thereto and all tenancies derived therefrom, is referred to herein as the “Leasehold Interest.” Stricklin controls Arbon Enterprises, which operates as a “mere continuation” of the Debtor’s former operations at the same location, using the same employees, equipment, and customer base.

In addition to the Successor Entities, the Defendants utilized Perennial Operations Technology, LLC (“POT”), Golden Parachute Holdings, LLC (“GPH”), and Allied Personnel Services, LLC (“Allied”) (collectively, the “Back-Office Entities”) as the operational nervous system of the enterprise. These entities served as conduits for financial transactions, payroll and the like.

Through this action, Plaintiff seeks to: (a) avoid the transfers under 11 U.S.C. §§ 544 and 548, or, in the alternative, as unauthorized post-petition transfers under 11 U.S.C. § 549; (b) recover, pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 550(a), the transferred property itself — including the Leasehold Interest in specie — or, where the Court so orders, the value of the Fraudulently Transferred Assets; (c) avoid the insider lien that Lanphere caused to be perfected … (d) obtain a declaratory judgment that Abide is a mere continuation of, and successor to, the Debtor with respect to the Leasehold Interest, and is the alter ego of the Debtor as to the Leasehold Interest and its transfer; (e) obtain injunctive relief preserving the Leasehold Interest pending resolution of this action; and (f) obtain a money judgment against the Individual Defendants, including for conspiracy to defraud, aiding and abetting fraudulent transfers, and piercing the corporate veil.

# JURISDICTION AND VENUE

This Court has jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. §§ 157 and 1334 as this matter is a “core proceeding” under 28 U.S.C. § 157(b)(2)(A), (E), (F), (H) and (O), as it involves the administration of the Estate, orders to turn over property of the Estate, proceedings to determine, avoid, or recover preferences, and proceedings to determine, avoid, and recover fraudulent conveyances.

Venue is proper under 28 U.S.C. §§ 1408 and 1409, as a matter arising in or under or related to the GOE Bankruptcy Case pending before this Court.

This adversary proceeding is filed pursuant to Federal Rules of Bankruptcy Procedure 7001(a) (proceedings to recover money or property), 7001(b) (proceedings to determine the validity, priority, or extent of an interest in property), 7001(g) (proceedings to obtain an injunction or other equitable relief), and 7001(i) (proceedings to obtain a declaratory judgment relating to any of the foregoing).

This adversary proceeding is timely commenced. The order for relief in the GOE Bankruptcy Case was entered upon the filing of the Debtor’s voluntary petition on July 12, 2024, and this action is commenced within two years after entry of the order for relief, within the periods prescribed by 11 U.S.C. §§ 546(a) and 108(a), and within the applicable period of 11 U.S.C. § 549(d) as to the post-petition transfers alleged herein. The avoidance and recovery claims asserted herein arise under 11 U.S.C. §§ 544, 547, 548, 549, 550, and 551 and belong exclusively to the Trustee for the benefit of the Estate; they did not exist in, were not asserted in, and could not have been asserted in any prepetition or state-court action, including Washington County Circuit Court Case No. 24CV42167.

Pursuant to Federal Rule of Bankruptcy Procedure 7008, Plaintiff states that this adversary proceeding is a core proceeding and that Plaintiff consents to the entry of final orders and judgment by the bankruptcy court. To the extent any claim asserted herein is determined to be non-core, or a claim on which the bankruptcy court may not constitutionally enter final judgment absent consent, Plaintiff consents to the entry of final orders and judgment by the bankruptcy court on such claim.

# FACTUAL BACKGROUND COMMON TO ALL CLAIMS

## The Valuable Enterprise

Prior to the Petition Date, the Debtor served as the management company for a vertically integrated cannabis enterprise operated at 12745 SW Walker Road, Beaverton, Oregon (the “Retail Location”). Terry Wilson (“Wilson”) and Lanphere created an integrated enterprise that included a retail store (Green Mart), a production facility (Herban), a management company (GOE), and an entity to accept credit card payments (PPH) (the “Original Enterprise”).

Herban was formed in 2017 and obtained an OLCC license to produce products that were sold to the public by Green Mart. Herban leased the Farm Property from LCD, using three of its approximately 15 acres of the Farm Property to grow agricultural products. Another seven acres of the Farm Property were used to grow grapes, which qualified the Farm Property for federal farm tax exemptions.

PPH began processing credit card payments on behalf of Green Mart in 2018. The Original Enterprise was designed for PPH to process Green Mart’s credit card transactions and then transfer the funds into Green Mart’s bank account. On information and belief, PPH failed to transfer all of the funds to Green Mart’s account, retaining most of the credit card sales for other purposes that did not benefit Green Mart.

While Green Mart was the key revenue-producing retail entity, GOE was the critical infrastructure entity that held the master lease of the Retail Location.

Wilson and Lanphere agreed that distributions from the Original Enterprise were to be paid proportionally, 75% to Lanphere and 25% to Wilson.

## The Arbitration and Judgment

Beginning as early as 2019, Lanphere undertook a series of actions intended to divest Wilson of his ownership interest in the Original Entities and the Original Enterprise. Lanphere has claimed that the Original Entities had no value but, in reality, the enterprise that Wilson helped establish and partially owned was worth millions before Lanphere stripped the assets and the Successor Entities continued to flourish in the same locations as the Original Entities.
