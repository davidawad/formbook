Defendant Platinum Pre-Owned, LLC ("Platinum") submits the following Memorandum of Law in support of its Motion to Dismiss Plaintiff's Second Amended Complaint under Rule 12(b)(2) and 12(b)(6) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure.

# INTRODUCTION

Plaintiff sued several defendants relating to a used car she purchased in Pennsylvania over four years ago.

Platinum is a used car dealership in Pennsylvania. Platinum sold Plaintiff a 2021 Mitsubishi Outlander in January 2022 from its Carlisle, Pennsylvania location. Plaintiff alleges that, at the time of the sale, Platinum (1) charged her for services that she did not authorize or receive, (2) did not provide her with certain required disclosures, (3) "coerced" Plaintiff to use a specific insurer for the vehicle, and (4) failed to accurately disclose the finance charge and annual percentage rate for the sale. The sale documents for the purchase (each of which includes Plaintiff's signature) accurately reflect all of the charges that Plaintiff authorized for the transaction and show that Platinum provided her with the appropriate disclosures.

Platinum moves to dismiss Plaintiff's Second Amended Complaint against it for three reasons. First, this Court lacks personal jurisdiction over Platinum. Courts in North Carolina cannot constitutionally exercise personal jurisdiction over a Pennsylvania car dealership for claims based solely on its conduct in Pennsylvania. Second, Plaintiff's claims against Platinum are time-barred under their applicable limitations periods. Third, the Second Amended Complaint fails to state a claim against Platinum for which relief can be granted because the claims are contradicted by the contract documents and not plausibly alleged.

Plaintiff attempts to allege claims against Platinum violating the Truth in Lending Act (Count VIII), Unfair and Deceptive Trade Practices Act (N.C. Gen. # ARGUMENT

**Standard of Review**

The party asserting jurisdiction has the burden of showing that personal jurisdiction is proper "by a preponderance of evidence." *E.g.*, *Grayson v. Anderson*, 816 F.3d 262, 268 (4th Cir. 2016); *accord Combs v. Bakker*, 886 F.2d 673, 676 (4th Cir. 1989). When reviewing a motion to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction, the Court may consider affidavits submitted by the parties. *E.g.*, *Grayson*, 816 F.3d at 268; *Combs*, 886 F.2d at 676.

To withstand a motion to dismiss under Rule 12(b)(6), "a complaint must contain sufficient factual matter, accepted as true, to state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face." *ACA Fin. Guar. Corp. v. City of Buena Vista, Virginia*, 917 F.3d 206, 212 (4th Cir. 2019) (quoting *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009)). "Labels, conclusions, recitation of a claim's elements, and naked assertions devoid of further factual enhancement will not suffice to meet the Rule 8 pleading standard." *Id.* (quoting *Iqbal*, 556 U.S. at 678).

## The Court Lacks Personal Jurisdiction Over Platinum.

Due Process under the United States Constitution requires that a defendant have sufficient ties to a forum before that forum's courts can exercise personal jurisdiction over the defendant. *E.g.*, *World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson*, 444 U.S. 286, 295 (1980); *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310, 320 (1945). Courts have recognized two types of personal jurisdiction: "general" jurisdiction and "specific" jurisdiction. *E.g.*, *Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Ct. of California, San Francisco Cnty.*, 137 S. Ct. 1773, 1779 (2017); *Goodyear Dunlop Tires Operations, S.A. v. Brown*, 564 U.S. 915, 919 (2011). General jurisdiction over a defendant is only appropriate when the defendant's "affiliations with the [forum] State are so 'continuous and systematic' as to render them essentially at home" there. *Goodyear*, 564 U.S. at 919 (citing *International Shoe*, 326 U.S. at 317).

### The Court Lacks General Jurisdiction Over Platinum.

Here, Plaintiff does not allege and could not allege sufficient facts to support general jurisdiction over Platinum. Plaintiff does not claim that Platinum is properly considered "at home" in North Carolina. Platinum is incorporated and headquartered in Pennsylvania, does not have any offices or employees in North Carolina, and does not do business in North Carolin. Ex. 1, p 2. As a result, Plaintiff could only show personal jurisdiction over Platinum through specific jurisdiction.

### The Court Lacks Specific Jurisdiction Over Platinum.

"A federal district court may only exercise personal jurisdiction over a foreign corporation if such jurisdiction is authorized by the long-arm statute of the state in which it sits and application of the long-arm statute is consistent with the due process clause of the Fourteenth Amendment." *Consulting Engineers Corp. v. Geometric Ltd.*, 561 F.3d 273, 277 (4th Cir. 2009).[^3]

# CONCLUSION

The Court should dismiss Plaintiff's Complaint as against Platinum because (1) the Court lacks personal jurisdiction over Platinum, (2) Plaintiff's claims against Platinum are time-barred; and (3) Plaintiff's Second Amended Complaint fails to state a claim against Platinum.
