# I. INTRODUCTION

This is a civil action for damages and injunctive relief arising under the United States Copyright Act of 1976, as amended, 17 U.S.C. §§ 101 et seq. (the “Copyright Act” or the “Act”), and under 17 U.S.C. §§ 1202 and 1203 for the provision and distribution of false copyright management information (“CMI”).

This Court has original subject-matter jurisdiction over all claims pursuant to 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331 and 1338(a).

Plaintiff Michael Grecco Productions, Inc. is the photography production company of Michael Grecco.

Michael Grecco is an internationally recognized, award-winning professional photographer and director.

Defendant New Century Foundation is the publisher of American Renaissance.

American Renaissance is a self-described “race-realist” publication founded and operated by Jared Taylor.

Defendant New Century Foundation is the owner and operator of the website www.amren.com which Jared Taylor operates.

In or about October 2022, Defendant copied Plaintiff’s copyrighted photograph of Baseball Hall of Famer Rod Carew and his wife (the “Photo”) and exploited it, without license or permission, to illustrate an article that Defendant published and has continuously displayed on its website ever since, while conveying a false copyright notice attributing ownership of the website’s contents — including the Photo — to New Century Foundation.

# IV. FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

## A. Grecco, MGPI, and the Copyrighted Photo

Michael Grecco is a well-known, award-winning commercial photographer, director, and author who has photographed hundreds of magazine covers and advertising campaigns worldwide.

Michael Grecco’s celebrated portraits of actors, musicians, athletes, and other public figures have been commissioned by leading magazines, studios, and television networks, published internationally, and exhibited in galleries and museums.

Michael Grecco creates his photographs through, and licenses them with, Plaintiff MGPI, which owns and manages the copyrights in his works.

On September 27, 1997, in Los Angeles, California, Grecco created the photograph at issue in this action: a portrait of Baseball Hall of Famer Rod Carew and his wife, Marilynn Levy (the “Photo”).

The Photo was licensed and distributed through Getty Images and was first published in 2009.

Plaintiff is, and at all relevant times has been, the owner of all right, title, and interest in and to the Photo, including the copyright therein and the exclusive rights under 17 U.S.C. § 106 to reproduce the Photo, to distribute copies of the Photo, to publicly display the Photo, and to prepare derivative works based upon the Photo.

Plaintiff licenses Grecco’s works, including the Photo, under a “rights-managed” licensing model, in which each license is limited in duration, scope, and medium, and each separate use requires a separate license.

Plaintiff charges, and the market regularly pays, substantial licensing fees for authorized editorial and commercial uses of Grecco’s works.

Plaintiff has never licensed the Photo to Defendant or any entity associated with Defendant.

Plaintiff has never authorized Defendant to reproduce, distribute, publicly display, or otherwise exploit the Photo for any purpose.

## B. Defendants’ Unauthorized Copying and Display of the Photo

New Century has published American Renaissance since in or about 1990, first as a print periodical and now through the Website, under the direction and control of Jared Taylor.

On or about October 14, 2022, Defendant Jared Taylor published on the Website an article titled “How Can Anyone Think Race Isn’t Real?” (the “Article”), presented together with a video of the same title.

The Article has remained continuously and publicly accessible at the website https://www.amren.com/videos/2022/10/how-can-anyone-think-race-isnt-real/.

Defendant Taylor copied the Photo and – without Plaintiff’s license, permission, or knowledge – reproduced, stored, publicly displayed, and distributed the Photo on Defendant New Century’s Website as a photographic illustration for the Article, accompanied by the caption “Baseball player Rod Carew poses for a photo with his wife Marilynn Levy on September 27, 1997 in Los Angeles, California. (Photo by Michael Grecco).”

Defendants also reproduced, stored, publicly displayed, and distributed a copy of the Photo on the Website as a standalone image file at the website https://www.amren.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/32-1.jpg.

On information and belief, Defendants’ unauthorized display of the Photo on the Website began on or about October 14, 2022, when Defendants published the Article, and has continued uninterrupted since that date.

Defendants operate the Website as the principal channel of its publishing operations, through which New Century solicits donations, subscriptions, and conference registrations.

Defendants used the Photo to illustrate the Article, to make the Article more attractive to readers, and to draw and retain traffic to the Website and its archives, from which New Century derives revenue, promotional value, and other benefit.

Grecco discovered Defendants’ unauthorized use of the Photo in or about January 2025.

On May 23, 2025, Grecco sent a written notice of claim and takedown demand to New Century by e-mail to info@newcenturyfoundation.org and dcomfort@newcenturyeducation.org.

Grecco sent follow-up notices to New Century on June 9, 2025, June 17, 2025, and July 2, 2025.

New Century did not respond to any of those notices.

## C. Defendants’ Provision and Distribution of False Copyright Management Information

The Article page on which Defendant New Century displayed the Photo conveys, at the foot of the page, the copyright notice, “The contents of this website are copyright © 1990-2025 New Century Foundation.”

That notice was conveyed, and on information and belief continues to be conveyed, in connection with copies and public displays of the Photo.

The copyright notice described above, including the name of the purported copyright owner, constitutes copyright management information within the meaning of 17 U.S.C. § 1202(c), because it was conveyed in connection with copies and public displays of the Photo.

That CMI was false.

Neither Defendant New Century nor Taylor has ever owned, or held any license to, any copyright interest in the Photo.

Defendant New Century knew the CMI was false as applied to the Photo.

Defendant Taylor knew the CMI was false as applied to the Photo.

Defendant New Century and Taylor provided and distributed the false CMI knowingly and with the intent to induce, enable, facilitate, or conceal infringement: attributing copyright ownership of the Website’s contents, including the Photo, to Defendant New Century made Defendants’ use appear licensed and authorized to the public and to rights-holders such as Plaintiff, thereby concealing the infringing nature of the use.

# FIRST CAUSE OF ACTION

#### (Copyright Infringement, 17 U.S.C. §§ 106 and 501)

Plaintiff repeats and realleges each and every allegation set forth in the preceding paragraphs of this Complaint as though fully set forth herein.

Plaintiff owns a valid and subsisting copyright in the Photo, registered under Registration No. VA 1-431-698.

Plaintiff never authorized Jared Taylor, New Century or, or any of its owners, officers, agents, employees, contractors, distributors, or licensees, to use, copy, reproduce, publicly display, distribute, license, manipulate, or otherwise commercially exploit the Photo.

Defendants directly infringed Plaintiff’s exclusive rights under 17 U.S.C. § 106 by reproducing, storing, publicly displaying, and distributing the Photo on the Website in connection with the Article, and by causing the Photo to be transmitted and displayed to visitors to the Website, all without Plaintiff’s authorization.

Pursuant to 17 U.S.C. § 504(b), Plaintiff is entitled to recover its actual damages — including the fair market value of the license fees Defendants New Century and Jared Taylor would have been required to pay for its uses of the Photo — together with disgorgement of New Century’s and Taylor’s profits attributable to the infringement, in amounts to be proven at trial.

Defendants’ infringement was willful: Defendants published the Photo beneath a caption identifying Grecco as its photographer, received at least five written notices of its infringement, and nonetheless continued — and continues — to display the Photo without license or permission.

Pursuant to 17 U.S.C. § 502, Plaintiff is entitled to a permanent injunction prohibiting further infringement of Plaintiff’s copyright in the Photo by Defendants and all persons acting in concert with Defendants.

# SECOND CAUSE OF ACTION

#### (False Copyright Management Information, 17 U.S.C. §§ 1202(a) and 1203)

Plaintiff repeats and realleges each and every allegation set forth in the preceding paragraphs of this Complaint as though fully set forth herein.

Defendant New Century, by and through Taylor, knowingly and with the intent to induce, enable, facilitate, or conceal infringement, provided copyright management information that is false, in violation of 17 U.S.C. § 1202(a)(1), by conveying the copyright notice “The contents of this website are copyright © 1990-2025 New Century Foundation” in connection with copies and public displays of the Photo on the Website.

Defendant New Century, by and through Taylor, knowingly and with the intent to induce, enable, facilitate, or conceal infringement, distributed copyright management information that is false, in violation of 17 U.S.C. § 1202(a)(2), by causing the false copyright notice to be distributed to and displayed for visitors to the Website in connection with the Article and the Photo, continuously, since in or about October 2022.

By reason of Defendant New Century and Taylor’s violations of 17 U.S.C. § 1202(a), Plaintiff has been injured and is entitled to recover its actual damages and any additional profits of Defendants attributable to the violations pursuant to 17 U.S.C. § 1203(c)(2), or, alternatively, at Plaintiff’s election made at any time before final judgment is entered, statutory damages of not less than $2,500 and not more than $25,000 for each violation pursuant to 17 U.S.C. § 1203(c)(3)(B).

Plaintiff is entitled to an injunction pursuant to 17 U.S.C. § 1203(b)(1) restraining Defendants, its officers, directors, agents, employees, representatives, and all persons acting in concert with Defendants, from further violations of 17 U.S.C. § 1202.
