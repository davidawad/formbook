# Jurisdiction and Venue

This is a core proceeding under 28 U.S.C. § 157(b)(2)(I) and is brought pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 523(a) and Rule 4007 of the Federal Rules of Bankruptcy Procedure.

This Court has jurisdiction as to the claims for relief sought herein under 28 U.S.C. §§ 157 and 1334.

Venue is proper pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1409(a).

If the Court finds that this is not a core proceeding, Kapitus consents to entry of final judgment by the Court pursuant to Rule 7008 of the Federal Rules of Bankruptcy Procedure.

This Adversary Proceeding is brought in connection with the bankruptcy case filed by the Debtor under Chapter 7 of the United States Bankruptcy Code (the “Bankruptcy Code”) as Case No. 26-14436 (the “Bankruptcy Case”).

Pursuant to the Notice of Chapter 7 Bankruptcy Case – No Proof of Claim Deadline, August 3, 2026 is the deadline for Kapitus to file a complaint under 11 U.S.C. § 523(a). See Case No. 26-14436, ECF 9. This Complaint is timely filed.

# Parties

The Debtor is an individual residing in the State of Maryland. See Case No. 26-14436, ECF 1.

Debtor is or was, at all relevant times, an owner, officer, director, member, and/or manager of Raja M. Din, M.D. PLLC (“Borrower”) and made decisions and statements on behalf of Borrower to Kapitus relevant to the issues herein.

Kapitus is a Virginia corporation with principal offices located in Arlington, Virginia and in New York, New York.

Service may be obtained on the Debtor by mailing a copy of the summons and complaint to the Debtor at the address shown in his petition and by service, by first class mail, on Debtor’s attorney pursuant to Rule 7004(b)(9) and (g) of the Federal Rules of Bankruptcy Procedure.

# Facts

## I. Transaction Documents

Pursuant to that certain Loan Agreement dated November 24, 2025, and related Security Agreement and Guaranty (collectively, the “$60,000 Agreement”), Kapitus loaned $60,000.00 to Borrower. True and correct copies of the documents that comprise the $60,000 Agreement are attached hereto as Collective Exhibit A.

Pursuant to the terms of the Guaranty executed in connection with the $60,000 Agreement, the Debtor personally guaranteed Borrower’s payment and performance of all representations, warranties, covenants, and obligations under the $60,000 Agreement. Coll. Ex. A, (Guaranty).

Pursuant to that certain Loan Agreement dated January 29, 2026, and related Security Agreement and Guaranty (collectively, the “$184,000 Agreement,” and together with the $60,000 Agreement, the “Agreements”), Kapitus loaned $184,000.00 to Borrower. True and correct copies of the documents that comprise the $184,000 Agreement are attached hereto as Collective Exhibit B.

To secure the loans, the Debtor also granted Kapitus a security interest in Borrower’s assets (collectively, the “Collateral”). See Coll. Ex. A, (Security Agreement), Coll. Ex. B, (Security Agreement).

Kapitus perfected its security interest in the Collateral by filing a UCC-1 Financing Statement with the Maryland Department of Assessments & Taxation at File Number 230209-0135000 (the “Financing Statement”) on February 9, 2023. A true and correct copy of the Financing Statement is attached as Exhibit C.

## II. The Debtor’s Representations to Kapitus to Induce Funding Under the Agreements

Kapitus requires, as a threshold step in its consideration of whether to provide funding, that each merchant sign the Agreements and related documents and provide certain written representations about its financial situation, operational plans, and intentions. Because Kapitus’ funding process is truncated from that of a standard financing institution, as is typical for the alternative financing industry, Kapitus relies heavily upon a merchant’s representations and warranties. In fact, Kapitus’ counter signature to the Agreements and funding are the last steps in the funding process.

### A. Applications

Prior to entering into the Agreements with Kapitus and in order to induce Kapitus to advance funds under the Agreements, the Debtor executed applications on June 16, 2021, and February 2, 2023 (together, the “Applications”) and represented, among other things, that he was 100% owner of Borrower and Borrower’s monthly sales were $50,000 to $100,000. True and correct copies of the Applications are attached hereto and incorporated herein as Collective Exhibit D.

### B. Representations and Acknowledgements

Prior to funding under the $184,000 Agreement in January of this year, and in order to induce Kapitus to advance funds pursuant to the $184,000 Agreement, the Debtor also executed a certain Representations and Acknowledgments – Funding is Contingent Upon Your Completion and Review by Kapitus form (the “R&A”), pursuant to which the Debtor acknowledged and represented to Kapitus, among other things, that: he was the authorized representative of Borrower; he was the authorized signatory on the business bank account; he did not anticipate closing or selling the business for any reason over the next 12 months; he did not plan to file for bankruptcy protection in the next 12 months for either the business or himself personally; and all information that he provided to Kapitus to obtain financing accurately reflected the current ownership of the business, business status and/or financial condition of the business.

A true and correct copy of the R&A is attached hereto and incorporated herein as Exhibit E.

Pursuant to the R&A, the Debtor also acknowledged that “I submitted the statements herein with the intent to obtain financing, Kapitus is relying on such statements to determine whether to approve the financing and that any false statements or misrepresentations made to obtain funding constitute fraud and will subject me and my business to legal action.” Exhibit E at 1-2.

### C. The Agreements

In the Agreements, the Debtor represented to Kapitus that the funding would be used exclusively for a business purpose and no other. See Coll. Ex. A, (Agreement), § 1.13 and p.7, Use of Proceeds Certification, Coll. Ex. B (Agreement), § 1.13 and p.7, Use of Proceeds Certification.

In fact, the Debtor executed a USE OF PROCEEDS CERTIFICATION whereby Debtor certified:

> I. The entirety of the loan will be used in the ordinary course of business.
>
> II. The entirety of the loan will be used exclusively for a Business Purpose and no other. A Business Purpose as applied to use of proceeds obtained under this Loan Agreement, refers solely to the purchase and acquisition of specific products or services used for the following purposes only: (i) working capital, (ii) business insurance (but not self-insurance programs), (iii) franchise fees, (iv) employee training, (v) the purchase of equipment, (vi) inventory, (vii) business supplies and raw materials, and (viii) the construction, renovation or improvement of facilities (but not the purchase of real estate). Business Purpose does not include: (a) payment for, or purchase of, any items, goods, materials real property, personal property or services for personal, individual or household use; or (b) use of funds for any proceeding under the United States Bankruptcy Code or any similar law (whether arising under Federal or State law) and/or any other legal proceeding or alternative dispute resolution proceeding.

See Coll. Ex. A, (Agreement) at p.7, Use of Proceeds Certification, Coll. Ex. B (Agreement) at p.7, Use of Proceeds Certification.

The Debtor also made the following representations and warranties to Kapitus via the Agreements, each of which Kapitus relied upon to provide funding to Borrower: Borrower and Guarantor represent, warrant and covenant that as of this date and during the term of this Agreement that the information and financial statements which have been furnished to LENDER by Borrower and Guarantor fairly represent the ownership and operations of the Borrower’s business and the financial condition of Borrower and Guarantor at such dates, and since those dates, there has been no material adverse change, financial or otherwise in such condition, operation or ownership of Borrower or Guarantor (as applicable). Borrower and Guarantor are current on any and all lease, rent or mortgage payments due. Coll. Ex. A (Agreement), Coll. Ex. B (Agreement) § 2.1.

Borrower and Guarantor further warrant that they are solvent, that no transfer of property is being made by Borrower or Guarantor and no obligation is being incurred by Borrower or Guarantor in connection with the Transaction Documents with the intent to hinder, delay, or defraud either present or future creditors of Borrower or Guarantor, and that neither Borrower nor Guarantor has filed any petition for bankruptcy protection under Title 11 of the United States Code or any state-law analogue. Coll. Ex. A (Agreement), Coll. Ex. B (Agreement) § 2.8.

Borrower shall not sell, dispose, transfer or otherwise convey its business, assets and/or any equity interest in Borrower, or effectuate the suspension, dissolution, or termination of Borrower’s business, without the express prior written consent of LENDER. Coll. Ex. A (Agreement), Coll. Ex. B (Agreement) § 2.13.

All information provided by Borrower and Guarantor to LENDER in the Transaction Documents, in any application for financing, in all other LENDER forms and in response to any request by Lender for information whether oral or in writing, is true, accurate and complete in all respects. Coll. Ex. A (Agreement), Coll. Ex. B (Agreement) § 2.14.

In addition, it was further represented in the Agreements that Borrower would only utilize one account (the “Account”) and irrevocably authorized Kapitus to ACH debit the specific remittances from that Account provided under the Agreements. Id., §§ 1.1, 2.6.

## III. The Debtor’s Misrepresentations

Upon information and belief, the Debtor made affirmative misrepresentations in the R&A and the Agreements, each of which Kapitus relied upon to provide funding to Borrower including, but not limited to, the following: misrepresented that he and his business were solvent; misrepresented the financial condition of the business and/or omitted material adverse changes in the condition, operation and ownership of the business; misrepresented that they would use the proceeds under the Agreements for business purposes alone; misrepresented that they would not transfer or otherwise convey business assets or equity interest without Kapitus’ express prior written consent; misrepresented that they would use one account for the business and that they would provide Kapitus irrevocable access to the business funds; and misrepresented that he would not permit diversion of receivables and that he would not engage in actions that would have an adverse effect upon his obligations under the Agreements.

Upon information and belief, the Debtor and Borrower were in a precarious position for some time prior to the funding provided by Kapitus.

Upon information and belief, the Debtor did not intend to, nor did the Debtor, use the proceeds of Kapitus’ funding for business purposes.

For instance, upon information and belief, the Debtor diverted the proceeds of Kapitus’ funding for a “Wedding Deposit” and “Wedding Clothes” for the Debtor’s child in December of 2025, just one month after the $60,000 Agreement was executed. See Case No. 26-14436, ECF 3 at p. 7 of 13.

Upon information and belief, following Borrower’s execution of and the Debtor’s guaranty of the Agreements, the Debtor was arrested in April of 2026 in Sarpy County, Nebraska for crimes related to financial dishonesty and misconduct.

In addition to the Debtor’s documented financial impropriety and the Debtor’s fraudulent misrepresentations made to Kapitus during the funding process, the Debtor has also made numerous misrepresentations and contradictions in its Schedules and other filings in the Bankruptcy Case.

Specifically, the Debtor denies that the Debtor “own[ed] a business or ha[d] any of the following connections to any business” such as a “limited liability company (LLC) or limited liability partnership (LLP)” within the four years prior to the Petition Date, defined below. See Case No. 26-14436, ECF 3 at p. 12 of 13. In the same filing, the Debtor discloses income from operating a business in 2024 and 2025. Id. at p. 2 of 13. The Debtor likewise discloses guarantees of commercial debts, including that of Borrower to Kapitus, which debt was incurred as recently as January of 2026. See Case No. 26-14436, ECF 1 at pp. 35, 38 of 57; see also Coll. Ex. A, (Agreement).

In summary, the Debtor and Borrower made numerous affirmative misrepresentations and intentional omissions, each of which Kapitus reasonably relied upon to fund money to Borrower.

## IV. Kapitus’ Funding and the Debtor’s Default

In justifiable reliance on the representations made by the Debtor in the Applications, R&A, Agreements, and the supporting documentation provided, Kapitus agreed to counter-execute the Agreements and provide funding to Borrower on the terms and conditions set forth in the Agreements.

In November of 2025, Kapitus transferred the proceeds of the $60,000 Agreement less applicable fees into the Account.

In January of 2026, Kapitus transferred the proceeds of the $184,000 Agreement less applicable fees into the Account.

In return, Borrower and the Debtor were to remit monthly payments to Kapitus, via ACH debit from the Account, until the entire Repayment Amount and all other fees and expenses provided for in the Agreements were paid in full. See Coll. Ex. A, (Agreement), Coll. Ex. B, (Agreement).

Pursuant to the Agreements, the Debtor was to maintain the Account with sufficient funds to cover the payments due under the Agreements to Kapitus. However, upon information and belief, the Debtor had no intention to honor the Agreements. See, id.

By March of 2026, the Debtor and Borrower defaulted on their payment obligations under the $60,000 Agreement due to insufficient funds in the Account, thus preventing Kapitus’ ACH payments from processing.

By April of 2026, the Debtor and Borrower defaulted on their payment obligations under the $184,000 Agreement due to insufficient funds in the Account, thus preventing Kapitus’ ACH payments from processing.

Debtor and Borrower only satisfied approximately 14% of the $60,000 Agreement and 36% of the $184,000 Agreement.

On April 24, 2026 (the “Petition Date”), the Debtor initiated this Chapter 7 bankruptcy proceeding. See Case No. 26-14436, ECF 1.

Kapitus is a creditor in this bankruptcy case in the amount of at least $340,116.27 as of the Petition Date.

The Debtor never intended to perform his obligations under the Agreements and obtained money from Kapitus by false pretenses, false statements, or actual fraud.

# Count I

## Nondischargeability of Debt Pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A)

Kapitus repeats and incorporates the allegations set forth above as though fully stated herein.

The Debtor’s liability to Kapitus, as alleged herein, is a debt for money owed and services provided within the meaning of 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2).

The Debtor obtained money and services by false pretenses, false representation(s), and/or actual fraud within the meaning of 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A).

As more fully detailed herein, the Debtor made material misrepresentations of fact including, but not necessarily limited to, representing that: (1) the Debtor and Borrower were not insolvent; (2) Borrower’s financial condition and ownership was accurately reflected to Kapitus; (3) Borrower would use Kapitus’ funding solely for business purposes; (4) Borrower/Debtor would not transfer or otherwise convey business assets or equity interest without Kapitus’ express prior written consent; (5) Borrower would use one Account for payment under the Agreements and provide Kapitus with irrevocable access to that Account for payment; and (6) Borrower/Debtor would not permit diversion of receivables or engage in actions that would have an adverse effect upon obligations under the Agreements.

The Debtor made material omissions of fact that induced Kapitus into funding the proceeds under the Agreements, including, without limitation, the true nature of Borrower’s financial condition and intent for the funds.

Upon information and belief, at the time the representations and/or material omissions were made, the Debtor knew that his and Borrower’s representations and omissions were false, untrue, misleading, and, as a direct and proximate cause of these intentional misrepresentations and omissions, the Debtor knew that Kapitus would be induced to provide Borrower the funding contemplated by the Agreements.

Upon information and belief, the Debtor intended to induce Kapitus to act or refrain from acting upon his misrepresentations and/or material omissions, and Kapitus justifiably relied upon such false representations and omissions.

As a result of misrepresentations and fraud by the Debtor, the Debtor obtained funding from Kapitus which would not have been authorized by Kapitus if the misrepresentations had not been made, or if material facts that were omitted and concealed, were actually disclosed.

Kapitus suffered damages as a direct and proximate consequence of the misrepresentations made by the Debtor.

All of the acts and omissions that constitute a breach or default of the Agreements were performed or omitted by, at the direction of, or with the consent of the Debtor.

Kapitus is entitled to an Order from this Court declaring that, pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A), the debt owed to Kapitus is non-dischargeable. Kapitus is also entitled to its attorneys’ fees and costs incurred in prosecuting this Complaint along with interest at the statutory rate.

# Count II

## Non-dischargeability of Debt Pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(B)

Kapitus repeats and incorporates the allegations set forth above as though fully stated herein.

The Debtor’s liability to Kapitus, as alleged herein, is a debt for money owed and services provided within the meaning of 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2).

Pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(B), a debt in which a debtor obtains money by a statement in writing that is materially false respecting an insider’s financial condition, on which the creditor reasonably relied, and which the debtor caused to be published with intent to deceive is nondischargeable.

Borrower, an insider of the Debtor, did obtain money from Kapitus by the following written, materially false statements respecting Borrower’s financial condition on which Kapitus reasonably relied, among others, that: (1) the Debtor and Borrower were not insolvent; (2) Borrower’s financial condition was accurately reflected to Kapitus; (3) Borrower would use Kapitus’ funding solely for business purposes; (4) Borrower/Debtor would not transfer or otherwise convey business assets or equity interest without Kapitus’ express prior written consent; (5) Borrower would use one Account for payment under the Agreements and provide Kapitus with irrevocable access to that Account for payment; and (6) Borrower had monthly sales of $50,000 to $100,000; and (7) Borrower/Debtor would not permit diversion of receivables or engage in actions that would have an adverse effect upon obligations under the Agreements.

As more fully detailed above, at the same time the representations were made, the Debtor knew these representations were materially false, untrue, and misleading.

The Debtor caused these false statements to be made in writing, including, but not limited to publishing such statements in the Agreements.

Borrower is an insider of the Debtor, because, at the time, the Debtor served as an owner, shareholder, officer, and/or director of Borrower, and/or person in control of Borrower, within the meaning of “insider” under 11 U.S.C. § 101(31)(A)(iv).

Kapitus reasonably relied on these written statements made by the Debtor in deciding to transfer the funds to Borrower.

Kapitus suffered damages as a direct and proximate consequence of the materially false statements made by the Debtor.

As a result of these materially false written statements by the Debtor, the Debtor obtained from Kapitus, inter alia, the funding under the Agreements which would not have been authorized by Kapitus if the materially false statements had not been made.

The Debtor’s activities described above constitute obtaining money by materially false written statements regarding an insider’s financial condition, on which Kapitus relied, and which Debtor made with the intent to deceive.

Kapitus is entitled to an Order from this Court declaring that, pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(B), the debt owed to Kapitus is non-dischargeable. Kapitus is also entitled to its attorneys’ fees and costs incurred in prosecuting this Complaint along with interest at the statutory rate.

# Count IV

## Non-dischargeability of Debt Pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(6)

Kapitus repeats and incorporates the allegations set forth above as though fully stated herein.

Pursuant to section 523(a)(6) of the Bankruptcy Code, a debt incurred by a debtor who engages in willful malicious conduct which results in damage shall be nondischargeable.

Kapitus believes and alleges that the Debtor entered into the Agreements with the specific intent to divert the funds for his own use. In addition, the Debtor made false statements to Kapitus with the intent to induce Kapitus to advance funds.

The Debtor almost immediately diverted the funds away from Borrower.

Further, the Debtor willfully and maliciously executed the Agreements and represented to Kapitus that: (1) the Debtor and Borrower were not insolvent; (2) Borrower’s financial condition was accurately reflected to Kapitus; (3) Borrower would use Kapitus’ funding solely for business purposes; (4) Borrower/Debtor would not transfer or otherwise convey business assets or equity interest without Kapitus’ express prior written consent; (5) Borrower would use one Account for payment under the Agreements and provide Kapitus with irrevocable access to that Account for payment; and (6) Borrower/Debtor would not permit diversion of receivables or engage in actions that would have an adverse effect upon obligations under the Agreements.

The Debtor’s activities described herein constitute willful and malicious conduct which resulted in damage to Kapitus.

Kapitus is entitled to an Order from this Court declaring that, pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(6), the Debtor’s debt to Kapitus is non-dischargeable and a judgment for money damages pursuant to the request herein. Kapitus is also entitled to its attorneys’ fees and costs of suit incurred in prosecuting this Complaint along with interest at the statutory rate.
