# Affirmation in Support of Motion for Leave to Appear as Amicus Curiae

SUNGSO LEE, an attorney admitted to practice law in the courts of New York, affirms the following to be true under penalty of perjury:

1\. I am an attorney for The Legal Aid Society (“Legal Aid”) and counsel for the proposed amicus curiae in this matter.

2\. Pursuant to this Court’s Rule of Practice 500.23(a)(1), Legal Aid requests permission to appear as amicus curiae in the above captioned case.

3\. This case asks how this Court should determine when the search of a cell phone is executed under CPL 690.30 and whether suppression is warranted when law enforcement fail to execute a timely search. This case, therefore, raises a question of first impression for this Court. This Court’s ruling will affect thousands of future criminal cases, including cases in which Legal Aid is likely to represent the defendant or appellant.

## Statement of Interest of Proposed Amicus Curiae

4\. Legal Aid has provided free legal services to low-income New York City residents since 1876. As the primary public defender in New York City, Legal Aid provides representation to hundreds of thousands of low-income New Yorkers arrested and accused of crimes in all five boroughs and, thus, has a strong interest in ensuring that the police lawfully search a person’s property and that remedies are available when the police conduct an unlawful search.

5\. Legal Aid regularly represents clients arrested in the same manner as the Appellant, both at the trial and appellate levels. Legal Aid attorneys spend thousands of hours a year litigating suppression issues, among them issues similar to those presented in this case. Legal Aid is committed to promoting fairness within the legal system and protecting New Yorkers’ rights. To that end, Legal Aid has a vested interest in the development of the law relating to suppression and its application as a remedy for violations of statutory and constitutional rights.

## Proposed Brief of Amicus Curiae

6\. Amicus respectfully seeks leave to file the proposed brief of amicus curiae, a copy of which is included with this submission as Exhibit A.

7\. In the proposed brief, amicus argues that the police may not execute a search outside the statutory ten-day deadline, regardless of whether the warrant includes “deemed executed at the time of issuance” language, and that the fruits of such an untimely search must be suppressed.

8\. In support of its position, amicus raises three arguments. First, amicus argues that CPL 690.30 is clear and unambiguous, and it makes plain that the search (or execution) is a separate act from the issuance of the warrant. As a result, the First Department’s ruling conflicts with the statute’s plain text and must be rejected.

9\. Second, amicus argues that this Court should hold that, when a cellphone is already in police custody, the search warrant is “executed” when the actual search of the phone’s data has begun. That reading of the CPL’s plain text both protects the rights of New Yorkers, as the Legislature intended, and accounts for the practical realities of searching a cellphone.

10\. Finally, amicus argues that this Court should hold that the fruits of an untimely search in violation of CPL 690.30 must be suppressed, consistent with the legislative intent behind the law and the numerous court decisions that have ordered suppression.

11\. The proposed brief satisfies the criteria of this Court’s Rule of Practice 500.23(4) because it “identif[ies] . . . arguments that would otherwise escape the Court’s consideration” and because it “would be of assistance to the Court.” The brief explains why the Criminal Procedure Law precludes courts from deeming a search warrant executed upon the warrant’s issuance, how this Court should define the “execution” a search warrant of a phone already in police custody, and why suppression is warranted when law enforcement fail to execute a search warrant within the CPL’s time limit.
