# U.S. Coast Guard Court of Criminal Appeals (opening brief, answering brief): rules

Alias `uscgcoca`. Scope: the appellant's assignments of error and brief (`opening-brief/`) and the United States' answer and brief (`answering-brief/`), filed by e-mail by appellate counsel. Verified against primary sources on 2026-10-17.

Re-check cadence: re-check annually each December 1 and whenever the court posts a new revision of its Rules of Appellate Procedure (current: "Rev. 03/25", published together with the Joint Rules of Appellate Procedure effective 27 May 2024; earlier revisions April 2023 and July 2024).

Access note: `www.uscg.mil` returns HTTP 403 to this box (and to the WebFetch tool); the rules PDF and the exemplars were read from Internet Archive captures of the court's own URLs.

Kinds: **M** = mandated by a rule (CGCCA Rules or JRAP). **C** = convention (from the exemplars). **T** = project/tooling rule. **A** = author responsibility.

Findings that differ from what many templates assume:

1. **Line spacing is a minimum of 15 points, not "double-spaced"**: "All pleadings shall appear in proportional type such as Times New Roman, at least 12-point, typed and with at least 15-point spacing" (Rule 17.1). Every exemplar is in fact double-spaced (line pitch about 27-28 pt).
2. **Word limits are long**: briefs and answers 20,000 words, replies 10,000; every brief, answer and reply carries a word-count certificate (Rule 17.3). None of the exemplars has one (EX-RODRIGUEZ predates the rule; the Steen and Weiser copies do not show one).
3. **Pleadings over 30 pages** must have a table of contents and authorities, and "summaries of arguments" (Rule 17.3).
4. **Minimum contents** for assignment-of-error briefs and answer briefs: statement of the case including jurisdiction; assignments of error or issues presented; statement of facts; argument (standard of review, contentions, reasons, authority, relief for each issue); certificate of service (Rule 17.2(a)).
5. **Pleadings are public**: names of complainants, victims and witnesses masked, and other identifiers trimmed, in the pleading itself (Rule 17.2(b)).

## Doc type: opening-brief

| ID | Requirement | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| USCGCOCA-CONT-010 | Assignment-of-error briefs contain at a minimum: (1) Statement of the Case, including a statement of jurisdiction; (2) Assignments of Error or Issues Presented; (3) Statement of Facts; (4) Argument addressing, for each assignment of error, the applicable standard of review, contentions, reasons, supporting authority and relief sought; (5) Certificate of Service. | Rule 17.2(a) | M |
| USCGCOCA-CONT-011 | Appellate counsel may file assignments of error, setting forth separately each error asserted; Grostefon issues in the Rule 18.2 form ("Pursuant to United States v. Grostefon ... Appellant, through appellate defense counsel, personally requests that this Court consider the following matters:"). | Rule 18(a)-(b) (JRAP text); Rule 18.2 | M |
| USCGCOCA-CONT-012 | A merits brief without assignment of error need only contain a statement of the case (other doc type). An appendix may carry unpublished opinions not on the Court's research platform and extracts of statutes, rules or regulations. | Rule 18.1; Rule 18(e) (JRAP text) | M |
| USCGCOCA-CONT-013 | Section order seen: EX-STEEN-OB: Statement of the Case and Jurisdiction, Statement of Facts, Issues Presented, Argument, Conclusion; EX-RODRIGUEZ: Statement of the Case, Statement of Facts, Assignments of Error, Argument, Conclusion, certificate of service. Both put the statement of the case before the errors. | EX-STEEN-OB, EX-RODRIGUEZ | C |
