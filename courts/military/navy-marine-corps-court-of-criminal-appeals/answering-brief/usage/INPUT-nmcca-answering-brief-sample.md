# Assignments of Error

I. WHETHER THE MILITARY JUDGE ERRED BY DENYING THE DEFENSE MOTION TO DISMISS SPECIFICATION 1 OF CHARGE I AS AN UNREASONABLE MULTIPLICATION OF CHARGES.

# Summary of Argument

The military judge did not abuse his discretion in declining to find an unreasonable multiplication of charges. The two specifications describe separate acts with separate harms, and the defense did not show that the charging scheme was unreasonable in the sense required by the Court's precedent. [Authority.]

The evidence is legally and factually sufficient. A rational factfinder could conclude beyond a reasonable doubt that the appellant committed the offense, and the Court should affirm the finding on Specification 2 after its own independent review. [Authority.]

# Argument

## Standard of Review

This Court reviews whether a charge is an unreasonable multiplication of charges for an abuse of discretion, and reviews questions of law de novo. [Authority.] Legal and factual sufficiency are reviewed de novo, and the Court must be convinced of guilt beyond a reasonable doubt after making its own independent judgment as to factual sufficiency. Article 66(c), UCMJ. [Authority.]

## The military judge did not err in declining to dismiss Specification 1 of Charge I.

### Principles of Law

Multiplication of charges is a doctrine of the Fifth Amendment protection against double jeopardy, and an unreasonable multiplication of charges is a separate doctrine that protects against prosecutorial overreach. [Authority.] The Court considers, among other factors, whether each charge is aimed at a distinct harm, whether the charges misrepresent or exaggerate the appellant's criminality, and whether the number of charges unreasonably increases the punitive exposure. [Authority.]

### Discussion

The defense argues that the two specifications are a single act charged twice. The record does not support that argument. The first specification alleges conduct on [date] at [location]; the second alleges separate conduct on [date] at [location]. [Record citation.] Each specification has its own element of proof, and each rests on a separate witness. [Record citation.]

The defense also argues that the charges inflate the appellant's punitive exposure. The military judge rejected that argument after considering the sentencing guidelines and the maximum punishment for each offense. [Record citation.] The judge's conclusion was within the range of reasonable choices and was not an abuse of discretion. [Authority.]

### The charges describe distinct harms.

The first specification charges the offense against [victim], and the second charges the offense against [victim] on a different date. The harms are separate, and the offenses do not merge under the elements test. [Authority.] The defense does not identify any overlap in proof that would make the two specifications a single offense.

### The charging scheme was not unreasonable.

The convening authority was entitled to charge each act separately, and doing so did not exaggerate the appellant's criminality. The military judge reviewed the charge sheet, heard argument, and made findings on the record. [Record citation.] Nothing in that record shows that the number of charges was chosen to increase punishment. [Authority.]

## The evidence is legally and factually sufficient to support the finding of guilty to Specification 2 of Charge II.

### Standard of Review

The Court reviews legal sufficiency de novo, asking whether any reasonable factfinder could have found the essential elements beyond a reasonable doubt, viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to the prosecution. [Authority.] The Court then independently weighs the evidence for factual sufficiency. Article 66(c), UCMJ.

### Discussion

The appellant argues that the only evidence of the element of [element] was the testimony of one witness, whom he says was not credible. Credibility is for the factfinder, and the military judge found the witness credible after observing the testimony. [Record citation.] The testimony was corroborated by [corroborating evidence]. [Record citation.]

Viewed in the light most favorable to the prosecution, the evidence permits a rational finding of guilt on each element. The Court should affirm, and it should be convinced of guilt beyond a reasonable doubt after its own review of the record. [Authority.]

# Conclusion

The United States respectfully requests that this Honorable Court affirm the findings of guilty to Specification 1 of Charge I and Specification 2 of Charge II, and affirm the sentence as approved by the convening authority.
