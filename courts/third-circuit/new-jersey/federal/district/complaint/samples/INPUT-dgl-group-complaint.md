Plaintiff DGL Group Ltd. is a New Jersey limited company with its principal place of business at 2045 Lincoln Hwy, Floor 3, Edison, NJ 08817. It sells hoverboards, scooters, e-bikes, go-karts, and numerous other rideable products under the well-known Hover-1 brand.

Defendant GoLabs Inc., the American subsidiary of a Chinese conglomerate, is a Texas corporation with its registered office at 2201 Luna Road, Carrollton, Texas 75006, and maintains additional offices at 3900 W. Plano Parkway, Plano, Texas 75075.

# Introduction

This is a straightforward breach of contract action concerning a Settlement Agreement (the "Agreement") duly entered into between Plaintiff and Defendant. In November 2021, the Parties settled a dispute involving their respective trademarks for hoverboard products. Each party agreed to respect the other's trademarks and allow it to sell hoverboard products. Among other mutual covenants and obligations, including that Plaintiff drop its proceeding before the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office to cancel Defendant's hoverboard trademark, Defendant agreed not to restrict "use of . . . the mark HOVERBOARD in connection with [Plaintiff's] Goods or related goods" and promised "to provide written consent to the use and/or registration of any DGL trademark that includes the term HOVERBOARD." (*See* Ex. 1 – Settlement Agreement §§ 2, 6). In exchange, Plaintiff agreed "not to oppose, contest, challenge the use of, oppose the registration or re-registration of, or seek cancellation of [Defendant's hoverboard] Mark, or to assist others in doing so," but only "[f]or so long as [Defendant] is in compliance with this Agreement." (*Id.* § 7). The Agreement is unambiguous as to Plaintiff's right to use the term "HOVERBOARD" in connection with its goods.

Despite this clear and express commitment to live-and-let-live, Defendant brazenly breached the Agreement directly prior to the 2025 holiday shopping season, when the vast majority of hoverboard sales occur, in a transparent and craven effort to destroy Plaintiff's hoverboard business and capture market share. With blatant disregard for the Agreement, Defendant sent trademark infringement notices to various retailers hosting Plaintiff's hoverboard products, causing Amazon, Wal-Mart, Target and other vendors to remove those products just as planning for the holiday season was beginning to ramp up.

The intended result of this breach was achieved: Plaintiff was not permitted or able to sell virtually any hoverboard products during the 2025 holiday season, and its hoverboard business has now entirely collapsed. As a result, Plaintiff has suffered actual and consequential damages exceeding $180 million, including lost sales, costs to restore relationships with its vendors and suppliers, and reputational harm.

# Jurisdiction and Venue

This Court has subject matter jurisdiction pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a) because this suit involves the citizens of two different states and the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000.00, exclusive of interest and cost.

This Court has personal jurisdiction over Defendant because the Agreement contains an exclusive forum selection clause designating the courts of New Jersey and/or the United States District Court for the District of New Jersey as the exclusive forum for any dispute arising out of the Agreement brought by Plaintiff. (*See* Ex. 1 § 14). A forum selection clause controls absent a strong showing that it should be set aside. Additionally, Defendant engaged in continuous and systematic business in New Jersey and purposefully availed itself of this state, and Plaintiff's claims arise out of or relate to Defendant's contacts with this state, including but not limited to contracting with Plaintiff.

Venue is proper in the United States District Court for the District of New Jersey pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1391 because a substantial part of the events giving rise to the claims occurred in this district. Further, venue is proper pursuant to Section 14 of the Agreement (*Id.*).

# Facts

Plaintiff offers a line of self-balancing scooters commonly referred to as "hoverboards." As such, it sought to register the mark "MY FIRST HOVERBOARD" with the United States Patent and Trademark Office (hereafter "DGL's Mark") on or about March 13, 2020.

Defendant owns U.S. Trademark Registration No. 3,685,723 for the mark "HOVERBOARD" (hereafter referred to as "GoLabs' Mark"), which was registered on September 22, 2009, for "Electrically powered two wheeled scooter for terrestrial human transportation" in Class 12.

After the United States Patent and Trademark Office cited GoLabs' Mark as a bar to registration of DGL's Mark, Plaintiff filed for the cancellation of GoLabs' Mark before the U.S. Trademark Trial and Appeal Board. In that proceeding, Plaintiff "request[ed] that the [Hoverboard] Mark be cancelled pursuant to 15 U.S.C. §1064(3) . . . on the following grounds: (1) the Registrant's Mark is or has become generic; (2) Registrant's Mark has not been used as a trademark and it fails to indicate a source of goods; and (3) Registrant has abandoned Registrant's Mark." The TTAB formally instituted Cancellation Proceeding No. 92077388 on June 16, 2021, and Defendant filed an Answer on July 21, 2021.

Following negotiations, Plaintiff and Defendant entered into a Settlement Agreement to resolve the matter.

## The Settlement Agreement

The Agreement, attached as Exhibit 1 to this Complaint and incorporated as if fully set out herein, was executed in November 2021. The Parties additionally executed a Consent Agreement which was attached and incorporated into the Agreement as an exhibit. (*See* Ex. 1-A – Consent Agreement).

As part of the Agreement, Plaintiff agreed to withdraw the Cancellation Proceeding against GoLabs' Mark. (Ex. 1 § 4). Pursuant to Sections 4 and 5 of the Agreement, the Parties stipulated to withdrawal of the cancellation proceeding with prejudice on November 17, 2021. Plaintiff fully performed under the Agreement and withdrew the Cancellation Proceeding.

In exchange, pursuant to the Agreement, Defendant agreed not to oppose, challenge or contest Plaintiff's use of DGL's Mark in connection with its goods. Specifically, Defendant promised that "GoLabs recognizes DGL's ownership of and right to use DGL's Mark in connection with the goods listed in U.S. Trademark Application Serial No. 88833507 (hereafter referred to as "DGL's Goods"). (Id. § 1)"; and that "GoLabs agrees not to oppose, contest, challenge the use of, oppose the registration or re-registration of, or seek cancellation of DGL's Mark, or to assist others in doing so. GoLabs further agrees not to oppose, contest, challenge the use of, oppose the registration or re-registration of, or seek cancellation of any present or future DGL trademark that includes the term HOVERBOARD, or to assist others in doing so, and GoLabs further agrees, upon request by DGL, to provide written consent to the use and/or registration of any DGL trademark that includes the term HOVERBOARD." (Id. § 6).

The Agreement also allowed Plaintiff to use DGL's Mark and GoLabs' Mark without limitation: "Nothing herein shall be interpreted as preventing or limiting use of DGL's Mark or the mark HOVERBOARD in connection with DGL's Goods or related goods." (Id. § 2).

Further in support of Plaintiff's bargained-for rights, Defendant executed the Consent Agreement, permitting the registration of DGL's Mark in connection with "two-wheeled, electric, portable devices, namely self-balancing scooters[.]" (See Ex. 1-A § 1). Plaintiff and Defendant agreed that were any confusion to arise they would "communicate to each other all details of each instance" and "cooperate to take steps to abate the confusion, and to prevent confusion from recurring." (Id. § 7) (emphasis added).

The Agreement further states, "The failure of any party to enforce the terms and conditions of this Agreement, or the waiver by any party of a breach by the other party of any provision herein, shall not in any way limit or waive the right of that party thereafter to enforce the compliance by the other party with respect to any term or condition herein." (Ex. 1 § 9). The Parties also agreed that "irreparable harm will result in the event of a breach of any provision herein." (Id. § 8).

Despite Plaintiff's compliance with the Agreement, and the numerous assertions by Defendant that Plaintiff had the right to use the mark "HOVERBOARD" in connection with its products, Defendant still asserted trademark infringement allegations against Plaintiff.

## Defendant's Breach of the Agreement and the Resulting Harm to Plaintiff

In or around August 2025, as Plaintiff was preparing for the busy holiday season, Defendant contacted several of Plaintiff's retail partners, including Amazon, and alleged that Plaintiff's hoverboard products infringed on Defendant's trademark rights. Given that hoverboards are most popular with school-aged children, the majority of sales occur leading up to the holiday season at the end of the year. Plaintiff must coordinate its supply to satisfy the increased demand generated by gift-shopping and special holiday sales. On information and belief, Defendant chose this critical time in the sales cycle to bring their unfounded trademark infringement allegations and sideline a market competitor to gain market share.

As a direct and proximate result of Defendant's unfounded trademark infringement allegations, Plaintiff's retail partners, including Amazon, Target, and Wal-Mart, removed the falsely accused products from their online and in-store shops. The effect of Defendant's breach of the Agreement was immediate and devastating. Plaintiff found its product inventory sidelined in warehouses during its peak sales season.

Defendant's actions effectively stopped a competing product from being offered for sale, through the most popular online and in-person shopping markets, during the highest point of the season for hoverboard sales. This systematic takedown of a competitor's product is not incidental or unexpected; in fact, the Parties expressly and deliberately noted that "irreparable harm will result in the event of a breach of any provision" of the Agreement. (Ex. 1 § 8). Moreover, Defendant's breach damaged Plaintiff's status and reputation both with consumers and with its retail partners, causing Plaintiff to lose hoverboard sales, incur costs to restore the relationships with its vendors and suppliers, and suffer reputational and consequential harm. Plaintiff's losses were not limited to hoverboards: because the Hover-1 name appeared across Plaintiff's broader portfolio, customers, distributors, and retailers all became reluctant to purchase not only hoverboards, but also scooters, e-bikes, go-karts, and other rideable products sold under the Hover-1 brand.

Defendant's actions to undermine and sabotage Plaintiff's hoverboard sales are in direct breach of the Agreement between the Parties. It is in fact the exact scenario Plaintiff attempted to protect against and the reason it bargained for the Agreement. Plaintiff is now forced to seek damages through the courts by instituting this action in New Jersey as directed by the Agreement's forum selection clause, which instructs that if Plaintiff brings a claim based on the Agreement, the action "shall be brought exclusively in any appropriate state court located in, or in the federal court for Middlesex County, New Jersey" (Ex. 1 § 14).
