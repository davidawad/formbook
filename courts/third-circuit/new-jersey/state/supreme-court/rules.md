# Supreme Court of New Jersey: briefs, motions, petitions and amicus briefs

Alias `nj`. Scope: papers filed in the Supreme Court of New Jersey under the Rules of Court, Part II (appellate practice, R. 2:1 et seq.): briefs of parties (formal and letter briefs), reply briefs, amicus curiae briefs, motions and supporting briefs, and the complaint in lieu of prerogative writs filed in the court's original jurisdiction. Verified against the Rules of Court, Part II, R. 2:6 (Appendices; Briefs; Transcript) on 2026-10-21. The copy fetched is a third-party reproduction of the text (courtcaddy.com, HTTP 200) whose notes end with amendments of 2012 (R. 2:6-1) and 2000 (R. 2:6-10). It is not the official njcourts.gov text, and it may be superseded for R. 2:6-10 (see OPEN below).

Local rules not retrieved: https://www.njcourts.gov/attorneys/court-rules (HTTP 403, 2026-10-21, after a 60 s wait and a retry); https://www.njcourts.gov/sites/default/files/forms/10836_cklist_brief.pdf (the Judiciary's brief checklist, revised 11/2025, HTTP 403); https://www.courtrules.net/new_jersey/new-jersey-appellate/rule-2-6-10 (HTTP 403); https://www.njcourts.gov/self-help/rules-and-references (HTTP 403); https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/court-rules/part-ii/ (HTTP 403). The official R. 2:6-10 text was therefore not read.

OPEN (for David): the fetched R. 2:6-10 reads "no more than 26 double-spaced lines of no more than 65 characters including spaces, each of no less than 10-pitch or 12-point type." Search snippets quoting the current rule and the 11/2025 checklist (not fetched) read "Times New Roman or a similar font in 14-point with character spacing expanded by 0.3 points, double-spaced." The 2016 answering brief in usage/ is set in Courier New; the 2021 brief on the merits is set in Times. The template follows the 14-point reading (stricter than the 12-point minimum in both texts) and does not impose the 26-line or 65-character cap. Which text governs must be confirmed from the official Rules of Court before a filing is judged.
