# District Court of the Virgin Islands (complaint, motion brief): rules

Alias `vid`. Scope: a civil complaint (`complaint/`) and a motion with its argument, typically a Rule 12(b) motion to dismiss (`motion-brief/`), filed by counsel through CM/ECF. Verified against primary sources on 2026-10-17.

Re-check cadence: re-check annually each December 1 (FRCP amendment date) and whenever the court posts new Local Rules (the current set took effect February 1, 2026, by order of January 30, 2026 in Misc. No. 3:25-mc-0003; the previous set was effective April 23, 2021).

Access note: on 2026-10-17 `www.vid.uscourts.gov` rejected direct requests from this box; the court's Local Rules page and the rules PDF were retrieved through the WebFetch tool from the court's own URLs.

Kinds: **M** = mandated by a rule (FRCP or LRCi). **C** = convention (from the exemplars). **T** = project/tooling rule. **A** = author responsibility.

Findings that differ from what many templates assume:

1. **No separate memorandum of law.** Since February 1, 2026: "A motion shall include a statement of the relief requested, any relevant factual or procedural background, and the argument in support of the motion, with citation to relevant legal authority. No separate memorandum in support may be filed." (LRCi 7.1(a)(1)). The `motion-brief` template is therefore the motion itself, argued. EX-WOOTEN (2023, separate memorandum) predates the rule; EX-GVI (2026) follows it.
2. **Length: the greater of 20 pages or 6,000 words**, counting only substantive portions; a filing over 20 substantive pages carries a word-count certificate that counts headings, footnotes and quotations (LRCi 7.1(b)).
3. **Footnotes may be 10 point and single-spaced** while text is at least 12 point (LRCi 5.1(a)).
4. **"Demand For Jury Trial" notation** on the front page immediately after the title when the demand is endorsed on the pleading (LRCi 38.1).
5. **Signature block includes the U.S. Virgin Islands bar registration number**, and for pro hac vice counsel the same information for both local and pro hac vice counsel (LRCi 5.4(h)(1)).
