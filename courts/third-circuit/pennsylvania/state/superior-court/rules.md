# Superior Court of Pennsylvania: briefs and applications

Alias `pasuperct`. Scope: briefs filed in the Superior Court (opening, answering, reply and amicus briefs) and the application for reargument filed under Pa.R.A.P. 2542-2544 (the `motion-brief` exemplar). Verified against the Pennsylvania Rules of Appellate Procedure as shown on the Pennsylvania Code website (Title 210), which reflects Pennsylvania Code changes effective through 56 Pa.B. 5370 (August 8, 2026), on 2026-10-21. Rule 124's Official Note cites the Superior Court's copy-and-fee page, which was not retrieved (see Sources).

## Briefs (opening, answering, reply, amicus): form

| ID | Requirement | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| PASUPERCT-FORM-001 | Documents are on 8 1/2 x 11 inch pages with a white background. | PARAP 124(a), (a)(1) | M |
| PASUPERCT-FORM-002 | The first page has a three-inch space from the top of the document for court stampings and filing notices. | PARAP 124(a)(2) | M |
| PASUPERCT-FORM-003 | Text is double spaced; quotations more than two lines long may be indented and single spaced; footnotes may be single spaced. | PARAP 124(a)(3) | M |
| PASUPERCT-FORM-004 | Margins are at least one inch on all four sides, except the first-page space in PASUPERCT-FORM-002. | PARAP 124(a)(3) | M |
| PASUPERCT-FORM-005 | Lettering is clear and legible, at least 14 point in the text and at least 12 point in footnotes. Print on one side of a page for documents other than briefs and reproduced records. | PARAP 124(a)(4) | M |
| PASUPERCT-FORM-006 | Typeface: the Official Note to Rule 124 says the Superior Court's preferred face is Verdana, and that Arial, Verdana or Times New Roman will be satisfactory. The template uses Times New Roman (Liberation Serif metric twin), one of the faces the Note accepts; Verdana is not vendored. | PARAP 124 Official Note | A |
| PASUPERCT-FORM-007 | Originals are unbound; copies are firmly bound with metal fasteners covered. Physical; not applicable to the electronic PDF. | PARAP 124(a)(5), (c) | A |

## Briefs: length and certificates

| ID | Requirement | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| PASUPERCT-LEN-001 | A principal brief (opening or appellee's brief) shall not exceed 14,000 words; a reply brief shall not exceed 7,000 words; a cross-appeal brief of the designated parties, 16,500 words. Unless the court orders otherwise. | PARAP 2135(a)(1)-(2) | M |
| PASUPERCT-LEN-002 | Cover, tables of contents and citations, proof of service and addenda do not count toward the word limit. | PARAP 2135(b) | M |
| PASUPERCT-LEN-003 | An amicus curiae brief filed during merits briefing is limited to 7,000 words. | PARAP 531(b)(3) | M |
| PASUPERCT-CERT-001 | Each filing contains a certification of compliance with the Case Records Public Access Policy of the Unified Judicial System of Pennsylvania (the Rule 127 certification text: "I certify that this filing complies with the provisions of the Case Records Public Access Policy of the Unified Judicial System of Pennsylvania that require filing confidential information and documents differently than non-confidential information and documents."). | PARAP 127(a) and Comment; PARAP 2111(a)(12); PARAP 2112; PARAP 2113(a); PARAP 531(b)(2) | M |
| PASUPERCT-CERT-002 | A brief contains a certification of compliance with the word count limit. Required when the principal brief exceeds 30 pages or the reply brief exceeds 15 pages (word processor); the template includes it on every brief, as Rule 2111(a)(12) lists it among the brief's required matters. | PARAP 2135(a)(1), (d); PARAP 2111(a)(12) | M |
| PASUPERCT-CERT-003 | Amicus briefs contain a statement of interest disclosing any person other than the amicus, its members or counsel who paid for or authored the brief. | PARAP 531(b)(2) | M |

## Opening brief (appellant's brief)

| ID | Requirement | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| PASUPERCT-OPEN-001 | The brief of the appellant contains, separately and distinctly entitled and in this order: statement of jurisdiction; order or determination in question; statement of scope and standard of review; statement of the questions involved; statement of the case; summary of argument; (when challenging discretionary aspects of sentence) statement of reasons to allow appeal; argument; short conclusion stating the precise relief sought. | PARAP 2111(a)(1)-(9) | M |
| PASUPERCT-OPEN-002 | The statement of jurisdiction cites the precise statutory provision, general rule or other authority conferring jurisdiction. | PARAP 2114 | M |
| PASUPERCT-OPEN-003 | The text of the order or determination in question is set out verbatim immediately after the statement of jurisdiction. | PARAP 2115(a) | M |
| PASUPERCT-OPEN-004 | The statement of questions involved is concise; each question is followed by an answer stating whether the court agreed, disagreed, did not answer or did not address it. Discretionary-sentence questions must appear in this statement or are waived. | PARAP 2116(a)-(b) | M |
| PASUPERCT-OPEN-005 | The statement of the case has the order given in PARAP 2117(a)(1)-(5), contains no argument, and states the place of raising or preservation of each issue where preservation matters. | PARAP 2117(a)-(c) | M |
| PASUPERCT-OPEN-006 | The argument is divided into as many parts as there are questions argued; each part has at its head a distinctly displayed statement of the point treated. | PARAP 2119(a) | M |
| PASUPERCT-OPEN-007 | An appellant challenging the discretionary aspects of a sentence sets out a separate statement of reasons for allowance of appeal immediately before the argument on that issue. | PARAP 2119(f); 2111(a)(7) | M |
| PASUPERCT-OPEN-008 | The brief has appended any trial-court or intermediate-court opinion relating to the order, and the Rule 1925(b) statement of errors or an averment that none was ordered. | PARAP 2111(b), (d) | M |
| PASUPERCT-OPEN-009 | Citations of authorities follow Pa.R.A.P. 126; references to the record follow Rule 2132. | PARAP 2119(b)-(c) | M |

## Application for reargument (motion-brief)

| ID | Requirement | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| PASUPERCT-MOT-001 | An application for reargument is filed within 14 days after entry of the order (7 days in a children's fast-track appeal). | PARAP 2542(a) | M |
| PASUPERCT-MOT-002 | Reargument is allowed only for compelling reasons; reasons are given concisely (overlooked fact or authority, panel inconsistency, subsequent reversal of authority). | PARAP 2543 | M |
| PASUPERCT-MOT-003 | The application identifies the order and the portions sought to be reargued with its date of entry; specifies with particularity the points overlooked or misapprehended; states the concise reasons; appends any opinions. No separate supporting brief may be annexed. | PARAP 2544(a)-(b) | M |
| PASUPERCT-MOT-004 | The application does not exceed 3,000 words except by permission of the court, excluding table of contents, table of citations and addendum. Eight pages or fewer on a word processor meet the limit without a word count certificate. | PARAP 2544(c)-(d)(1) | M |
| PASUPERCT-MOT-005 | The application contains the Rule 127 Public Access Policy certificate. | PARAP 2544(d)(2); PARAP 123(f) | M |
| PASUPERCT-MOT-006 | An application for relief is in writing, served on all parties with proof of service, and states the grounds with particularity. No oral argument is permitted on an application unless the court orders it. | PARAP 123(a), (d); PARAP 121(d) | M |
