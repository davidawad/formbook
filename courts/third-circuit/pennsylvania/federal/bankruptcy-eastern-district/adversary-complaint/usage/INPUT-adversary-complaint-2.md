# PRELIMINARY STATEMENT

This action arises out of Defendant’s breach of contract, violation of the duty of good faith and fair dealing, improper attempts to seize Plaintiffs’ assets to collect on debt owed by third parties, and tortious interference with a $30 million sale of those assets to interested purchasers. Plaintiffs seek damages, injunctive relief, and a declaration that Defendants have no interest in those assets or, in the alternative, that such interest is subordinated.

In sum, this matter concerns an aggressive lender who, acting in bad faith, manufactured (non-monetary) defaults and seized on ambiguities within its own loan documents to strongarm Plaintiffs into paying debts owed by separate businesses.

Plaintiffs had no obligation regarding those debts. In fact, Defendant never obtained (or even sought) a guarantee from Plaintiffs to secure the separate debts at issue.

Even though Plaintiffs remained current on their loan payments, Defendant purported to accelerate their loans (without providing the required notice and cure periods under the loan documents) and took the extraordinary measure of commencing litigation to appoint a receiver singularly to liquidate an otherwise going concern.

These actions have caused significant economic and reputational harm to Plaintiffs’ business.

At the time of Defendant’s improper interference, Plaintiffs sought to sell the business at a favorable price, more than sufficient to pay off its debts to Defendant.

However, Defendant’s interference has undermined efforts to conduct an orderly sale, greatly reducing the value and marketability of the business.

# FACTUAL BACKGROUND

Plaintiffs own and operate Dave’s Hot Chicken restaurants (“Restaurants”) as franchisees of Dave’s Hot Chicken Franchise Co., LLC (“Franchisor”).

To pursue the development of additional Restaurants, TIG Reaper obtained two loans from Defendant:

1. a certain term loan in the principal amount of $1,650,000.00 (the “Term Loan”), as evidenced by the Term Loan A Note dated August 15, 2024 (“Term Note”). A true and correct copy of the Term Note is attached as Exhibit 1.
1. a certain drawdown line of credit in the principal amount of $5,000,000.00 (the “DLOC Loan,” together with the Term Loan, the “Loans”), as evidenced by the DLOC Note dated August 15, 2024 (“DLOC Note,” together with the DLOC Note, the “Notes”). A true and correct copy of the DLOC Note is attached as Exhibit 2.

Contemporaneous with the Notes, Plaintiff TIG Reaper and Defendant executed a Loan Agreement, a true and correct copy of which is attached as Exhibit 3 and a Security Agreement, a true and correct copy of which is attached as Exhibit 4.

Plaintiffs initially entered into the Loans to support the expansion of their successful restaurants.

As stated in the Loan Agreement, the purpose of the Term Loan was to refinance existing debt, and the purpose of the DLOC Loan was to acquire leasehold sites for new Restaurants and to develop or improve existing Restaurants. Ex. 3, §§ 1.2(b), 1.3(b).

The Loan Agreement further provided that the Loans were to be secured by specific collateral, to wit TIG Reaper’s tangible and intangible personal property in connection with three Restaurants (“Collateral”). Ex. 3, § 1.1 and Exhibit 1.1 thereto.

While the first DLOC draw period was 24 months, the Loan Agreement provided for an “Accordion” whereby Plaintiffs had the right to request, subject to Defendant’s sole discretion, a 24-month extension, commensurate with $3.35 million in additional financing from Defendant. Ex. 3, § 1.3(i).

# COUNT I: BREACH OF CONTRACT & BREACH OF DUTY OF GOOD FAITH AND FAIR DEALING

Plaintiffs incorporate the foregoing averments as if fully set forth herein.

Plaintiffs and Defendant are parties to the Loan Agreement, which provides that Plaintiffs have 30 days after written notice thereof to cure non-monetary defaults as a matter of course. Ex. 3, § 6.1(a).

As noted above, Defendants unlawfully accelerated the Loan by ignoring Plaintiffs’ timely loan payments.

The Loan Agreement further provides that any non-willful failure that does not place the Collateral “in immediate jeopardy” shall not constitute a default if Plaintiffs begin to cure the failure within 30 days and proceed to cure within 90 days. Id.

None of the defaults alleged by Defendant are for non-payment.

None of the alleged defaults by Plaintiffs were willful.

The Collateral was not and is not “in immediate jeopardy” as a result of any alleged defaults, nor does Defendant so allege in the Lawsuit.

Plaintiffs were entitled to 90 days (and in no instance less than 30 days) to cure the alleged defaults.

Instead, Defendant accelerated the loan in bad faith two days after sending a notice of default, even after Plaintiffs timely cured any alleged nonpayment.

Defendant’s unlawful acceleration effectively prevented any cure of the non-monetary breaches.

Defendant’s actions breach the terms of the Loan Documents, and also breach the duty of good faith and fair dealing implied in every contract.

Defendant’s breaches have caused Plaintiffs at least $14 million in damages, which damages are accruing.

> WHEREFORE, Plaintiffs seek judgment in their favor and against Defendant in an amount in excess of $75,000, and costs, and such other relief as this Court shall deem just and proper.

# COUNT II: TORTIOUS INTERFERENCE WITH PROSPECTIVE CONTRACTUAL RELATIONSHIP

Plaintiffs incorporate the foregoing averments as if fully set forth herein.

Plaintiffs had been actively exploring the sale of the Restaurants for an amount sufficient to repay all indebtedness owed under the Loan Documents and had a reasonable probability of entering into a contractual relationship with prospective purchasers of the Restaurants, having engaged in tentative discussions with a prospective buyer concerning a sale of the Restaurants for approximately $30 million.

Defendant knew or should have known of Plaintiffs’ efforts to sell the Restaurants and the prospective contractual relationship with the prospective buyer.

Plaintiffs’ prospective sale was a valid and reasonably probable economic relationship, and not merely a speculative hope of future business.

Defendant intentionally interfered with Plaintiffs’ prospective contractual relationship by, among other things, improperly declaring Plaintiffs in default, accelerating the Loans, asserting rights in Plaintiffs’ assets that Defendant did not possess, threatening to take control of and liquidate Plaintiffs’ assets through a receivership, and pursuing the Lawsuit seeking appointment of a receiver to control and sell the Restaurants.

Defendant’s conduct interfered with Plaintiffs’ ability to consummate a sale of the Restaurants and substantially diminished their value to prospective purchasers.

As a direct and proximate result of Defendant’s intentional interference, Plaintiffs have suffered damages in an amount to be proven at trial.

Defendant’s interference was improper and without justification or privilege because, among other things, Defendant sought to exercise rights in Plaintiffs’ assets to secure obligations allegedly owed by non-party TIG Queso, despite Plaintiffs’ Collateral securing only Plaintiffs’ obligations under the Loan Documents, and did so before Plaintiffs had been afforded the contractual opportunity to cure the alleged non-monetary defaults.

> WHEREFORE, Plaintiffs seek judgment in their favor and against Defendant in an amount in excess of $75,000, together with costs, and such other relief as this Court deems just and proper.

# COUNT III: INJUNCTION AGAINST CROSS-COLLATERALIZATION

Plaintiffs incorporate the foregoing averments as if fully set forth herein.

Defendant asserts a security interest in Plaintiffs’ Collateral for liabilities owed to Defendant by TIG Queso.

Defendant has not yet exhausted its pursuit of TIG Queso’s collateral under the relevant loan agreements.

Further, on or about July 21, 2026, Defendant’s counsel stated to Plaintiffs’ counsel that Plaintiffs’ Collateral was not cross collateralized as security for TIG Queso liabilities to Defendant.

Plaintiffs’ business is a going concern and has a market value more than sufficient to cover the indebtedness owed to Defendant.

Defendant’s pursuit of the Collateral will result in immediate and irreparable harm to Plaintiffs in the form of loss of the Restaurants.

Greater injury would result to Plaintiffs from denial of the injunction than from granting it, because Defendant has separate collateral owned by TIG Queso to satisfy the debts under those separate loan agreements.

Injunctive relief would preserve the status quo of Plaintiffs’ operating Restaurants and pursuing a sale for fair market value prior to Defendant’s wrongful declaration of defaults.

Plaintiffs have no adequate remedy at law because liquidation of the Collateral to satisfy debts of a non-party would unjustifiably terminate Plaintiffs’ businesses without cause.

The public has an interest in the continuity of restaurants that serve the public, and in avoiding unnecessary forfeiture or closure of such establishments.

> WHEREFORE, Plaintiffs seek a preliminary and permanent injunction against Defendant’s pursuit of the Collateral for obligations owed by TIG Queso, and such other relief as this Court shall deem just and proper.

# COUNT IV: DECLARATORY JUDGMENT

Plaintiffs incorporate the foregoing averments as if fully set forth herein.

Defendants should be estopped from liquidating the Collateral for liabilities of TIG Queso because on or about July 21, 2026, Defendant’s counsel stated to Plaintiffs’ counsel that the Collateral was not cross-collateralized as security for TIG Queso liabilities to Defendant.

Plaintiffs seek a declaration that the Collateral shall not be used to satisfy the debts of TIG Queso to Defendant unless and until Defendant demonstrates to this Court that Defendant has exhausted the collateral under the TIG Queso loan agreements, and only after a hearing to determine whether the Collateral properly is subject to such a security interest.

> WHEREFORE, Plaintiffs seek a declaratory judgment that Defendant may not pursue the Collateral for obligations owed by TIG Queso, and such other relief as this Court shall deem just and proper.

# COUNT V: SUBORDINATION OF INTEREST

Plaintiffs incorporate the foregoing averments as if fully set forth herein.

Defendant has an adequate remedy against TIG Queso in the form of the collateral provided in the TIG Queso loan documents.

Further, Defendant breached the Loan Documents and acted in bad faith by failing to give Plaintiffs the required time to cure non-monetary defaults.

Defendant’s interest, if any, in the Collateral should be subordinated to other interests in this proceeding.

> WHEREFORE, Plaintiffs seek judgment in their favor and against Defendant, subordinating Defendant’s claim to the Collateral for obligations owed by TIG Queso, and such other relief as this Court shall deem just and proper.
